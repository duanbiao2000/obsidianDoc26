
## Speech Script

**Hello, everyone.**

Today, I would like to introduce a famous painting called **The School of Athens**.

It was painted by the Italian artist **Raphael** during the Renaissance.  
It is a fresco, which means that it was painted directly on a wall.  
You can still see it today in the Vatican Museums in Rome.

At first, this painting may look crowded.  
There are many people, large buildings, and many small details.  
But it is not simply a picture of a school.  
It is a picture about a very big question:

**How can human beings find truth?**

Raphael imagines a meeting of great thinkers from the ancient world.  
They did not all live at the same time.  
Some were philosophers, some were mathematicians, and some studied the stars and the natural world.  
But in this painting, they come together to talk, think, teach, write, and learn.

Now, let us look at the center of the painting.

The two most important people are **Plato** and **Aristotle**.  
They are walking together and talking.  
Plato is on the left. He points one finger up toward the sky and holds a book called _Timaeus_.  
Aristotle is on the right. His hand points forward and down, and he holds a book about ethics.

Their hand movements are very meaningful.

Plato points upward because he believed that truth comes from great ideas and perfect forms.  
For Plato, the world we see is always changing.  
So, we need to think beyond what we can see.

Aristotle points down toward the world around us.  
He believed that we should study real things: nature, people, actions, and experience.  
In simple words, Plato asks, “What should the perfect truth be?”  
Aristotle asks, “What can we learn from the real world?”

Raphael does not show them fighting.  
He shows them walking side by side.  
This may tell us that we need both ideas and experience.  
We need imagination, but we also need evidence.

Now, look to the left side of the painting.

We can see **Socrates**, one of the most famous philosophers in history.  
He is talking with a group of young people.  
His hands look active, as if he is counting points in an argument.  
Socrates is important because he asked questions.  
He did not only give answers.  
He asked people to explain what they meant by words like “justice,” “courage,” and “the good life.”

Near the lower left, we can see **Pythagoras**.  
He is writing and explaining ideas to people around him.  
He is connected with mathematics, numbers, and music.  
For him and his followers, the world had order, and numbers could help us understand that order.  
This part of the painting shows that mathematics is not only calculation.  
It is also a way to discover patterns in the world.

In the middle, on the steps, there is a man lying down.  
He is usually identified as **Diogenes**.  
He looks very different from the other people.  
Many people are talking, studying, or teaching.  
But Diogenes seems not to care about status, rich clothes, or formal education.  
He represents a simple but difficult question:

**Do we really need so many things to live a good life?**

Near him, there is another man who is writing alone on a block of marble.  
He is usually identified as **Heraclitus**.  
His face was painted to look like Michelangelo, another great Renaissance artist.  
This is a small but important connection between the ancient world and Raphael’s own time.

Now, let us move to the lower right side.

There, we see **Euclid** teaching geometry.  
He bends down and uses a compass to draw a shape.  
Students watch him carefully.  
This scene is very important because it shows a different kind of knowledge.  
Euclid does not ask people to believe him only because he is famous.  
He shows them how to follow steps, use logic, and check an answer.

Behind Euclid, there are two figures holding spheres.  
One holds a sphere of the sky, and the other holds a sphere of the Earth.  
They are usually identified as **Zoroaster** and **Ptolemy**.  
They remind us that people have always looked at the sky and tried to understand the universe.

Finally, look at the far right side of the painting.

There is a young man wearing a black hat.  
He looks directly at us, the viewers.  
This is Raphael himself.  
He places himself inside the painting, beside the great thinkers.

Why did Raphael do this?

Maybe he wanted to say that art is also a form of knowledge.  
An artist does not only make beautiful pictures.  
An artist also observes the world, organizes ideas, and helps us see things in a new way.

For me, this is why _The School of Athens_ is still powerful today.

It shows many different kinds of people and many different ways of thinking.  
Some people ask questions.  
Some study numbers.  
Some observe nature.  
Some teach.  
Some write alone.  
Some even challenge society itself.

The painting does not say that only one person has the truth.  
Instead, it suggests that truth may grow through conversation, reason, observation, and imagination.

**Thank you for listening.**

## Easier Key Lines

如果担心原稿仍然偏难，以下几句可替换成更自然、简单的口语：

|原句|更简单的说法|
|---|---|
|_How can human beings find truth?_|_How do people find what is true?_|
|_We need both ideas and experience._|_We need to think, but we also need to look at real life._|
|_Mathematics is a way to discover patterns in the world._|_Math helps us see order in the world._|
|_He represents a simple but difficult question._|_He makes us ask a simple but important question._|
|_Truth may grow through conversation, reason, observation, and imagination._|_We can get closer to truth by talking, thinking, looking, and imagining._|

人物识别中最稳妥的部分是画面中央的柏拉图与亚里士多德、右下的欧几里得、持天球的琐罗亚斯德、持地球仪的托勒密，以及最右的拉斐尔自画像；其他角色可在演讲中保留 _usually identified as_，这样既清楚又符合艺术史研究的谨慎表达。
