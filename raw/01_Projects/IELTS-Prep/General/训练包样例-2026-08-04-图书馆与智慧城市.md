### Section 1 & 2 综合场景：社区图书馆服务变更

你将听到一段图书馆工作人员对读者的说明。**（请使用下方文本朗读录音，或请人代读。自然语速，英式发音。）**

**【听力文本】**

"Good morning, everyone. Thank you for coming to our community meeting. I'm here to update you on the changes to our library services, which take effect from the first of next month.

First, the good news: we are extending our opening hours. The library will now be open from 8 a.m. until 9 p.m. Monday to Friday, instead of the current 9 to 6. On weekends, we will open at 9 a.m. and close at 5 p.m., which is unchanged. However, please note that the self-service area will be accessible 24 hours a day using your membership card.

Second, we are introducing a new borrowing policy. The maximum number of items you can borrow at one time will increase from 12 to 20. DVDs, however, are still limited to 4 per member. The loan period for books is now 3 weeks instead of 2, but DVDs remain at 1 week. Overdue fines have been revised: books will cost 30 pence per day, and DVDs 50 pence per day. But here's an important change: children's books will no longer incur fines at all, to encourage early reading.

Third, we are expanding our digital services. All members will get free access to our e-book and audiobook platform. You can download up to 10 titles per month. We are also launching a new app that allows you to reserve items, renew loans, and check your account. The app will be available from the 15th of next month.

Now, regarding the community events: we will discontinue the weekly storytelling session for children due to low attendance, but we are starting a new monthly author talk series. The first talk will feature Dr. Sarah Milford, a local historian, on the 20th of next month at 6:30 p.m. Admission is free but you need to book a ticket in advance at the front desk.

Finally, we are temporarily closing the reference room for renovation from next Monday until the end of the month. During this period, reference materials can be requested at the enquiry desk and consulted in the study area on the ground floor.

Are there any questions? Yes, the gentleman in the blue coat."

**【题目】**

**A. 完成句子（Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer）**

1. The library will be open until __________ on weekdays from next month.
2. The opening hours on Sunday are from __________.
3. Members can access the __________ area at any time.
4. The new borrowing limit for books and CDs combined is __________ items.
5. The fine for an overdue children's book is __________.

**B. 多选题（Choose TWO letters A–E）**

Which TWO changes to the digital services are mentioned?
A. Free e-books and audiobooks
B. A new library app
C. Free Wi-Fi in the library
D. Online renewal of loans
E. Reduced charges for digital downloads

**C. 笔记填空（Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS for each answer）**

| Event | Change |
| :--- | :--- |
| Children's storytelling | being __________ (11) |
| Author talk | featuring a __________ (12) |
| Reference room | closed for __________ (13) |

---

**【答案】**

A:
1. **9 p.m. / 9 pm**
2. **9 a.m. to 5 p.m.** (accept "9–5")
3. **self-service**
4. **20**
5. **no fine / 0 / nothing**

B: **A, D** (A = e-book and audiobook platform; D = renew loans via app)

C:
11. **discontinued / stopped**
12. **local historian**
13. **renovation**

**What makes a city 'smart'?**

The term 'smart city' has become a buzzword in urban planning circles over the past decade, but its meaning remains surprisingly fluid. Broadly speaking, a smart city uses digital technology and data to improve the efficiency of services, reduce resource consumption, and enhance the quality of life for its residents. However, as a growing body of research shows, the success of a smart city depends less on the technology itself and more on how that technology is integrated into the social and physical fabric of the city.

One of the most visible applications of smart city technology is in transport. Sensors embedded in roads and traffic lights can collect real-time data on vehicle movements, allowing traffic management systems to adjust signal timings dynamically and reduce congestion. In Barcelona, for example, smart traffic lights have reduced journey times by 25 per cent in some areas. Similarly, smart parking systems guide drivers to vacant spaces via a mobile app, cutting the time spent circling city blocks and, consequently, lowering emissions. According to a study by the European Commission, such systems can reduce urban traffic by up to 15 per cent.

Energy management is another domain where smart technology has shown promise. Smart grids enable more efficient distribution of electricity by balancing supply and demand in real time. In combination with smart meters, which provide households with detailed information about their energy consumption, these grids have been found to reduce household electricity use by between 5 and 10 per cent. More ambitious projects, such as the smart streetlights in San Diego that dim when no pedestrians are present, demonstrate how even small innovations can accumulate into significant energy savings.

Waste management, too, has benefited from the smart city approach. Traditional waste collection operates on fixed schedules, often leading to unnecessary pick-ups or overflowing bins. Smart bins equipped with sensors can alert collection services when they are full, optimising collection routes and reducing fuel consumption. In Seoul, this system has cut waste collection costs by 20 per cent while improving cleanliness in residential areas.

Yet, for all its promise, the smart city concept has attracted criticism. Privacy advocates have raised concerns about the mass surveillance that underpins many smart city systems. Cameras and sensors that track movement can, in theory, be used to monitor citizens' behaviour in ways that erode civil liberties. In Toronto, a controversial smart city project was scaled back significantly after public backlash over data governance and privacy protection. Furthermore, critics argue that smart city technologies often favour high-income neighbourhoods, exacerbating existing social inequalities. A report by the United Nations highlighted that in many cities, low-income areas have the poorest digital infrastructure, which means that the benefits of smart technology are unevenly distributed.

There is also the question of cost. The upfront investment required for smart city infrastructure is substantial. A small city might need to spend tens of millions of dollars on sensors, communication networks, and data centres, with no guarantee of a clear return on investment. Some economists have questioned whether the efficiency gains justify the expenditure, particularly when many cities are already struggling with budget deficits.

A more fundamental point, raised by urban sociologist Dr Hannah Patterson, is that smart city initiatives often overlook the human element. 'Cities are not just collections of systems,' she argues. 'They are living communities. Technology should serve the needs of people, not the other way around.' In her view, the most successful smart city projects are those that actively involve residents in the design and decision-making process. She cites the example of Amsterdam, where a 'smart city lab' invites citizens to propose and test local solutions, from community-owned solar panels to neighbourhood car-sharing schemes.

In conclusion, the smart city is not a fixed destination but an ongoing process of experimentation and adaptation. The cities that will thrive in the coming decades are likely to be those that strike a balance between technological innovation and social inclusion, between efficiency and privacy, and between top-down planning and grassroots participation.

**【答案】**

1. **FALSE** – "its meaning remains surprisingly fluid" (paragraph A).
2. **NOT GIVEN** – "reduced ... by 25 per cent" – exact figure given, but no indication that every case is exactly this.
3. **FALSE** – "smart grids ... in combination with smart meters ... reduce by 5–10%" – not grids alone.
4. **TRUE** – "improving cleanliness in residential areas" (paragraph D).
5. **FALSE** – "scaled back significantly" – not cancelled entirely (paragraph E).
6. **FALSE** – "low-income areas have the poorest digital infrastructure" (opposite).
7. **TRUE** – "invites citizens to propose and test local solutions" (paragraph G).

8–12:
B = **iii**
C = **v**
D = **vii**
E = **ii**
F = **i**

## 审计记录（2026-08-04）

1. **时间预算不符**：声称 4 小时，实际分科合计 295 分钟、计划表 280 分钟——超时 40-55 分钟；且为单科大块结构（听 70m / 读 90m / 写 75m / 口 60m），与 v5 单元结构（每单元含听说读写，50+10）不一致。此结构更适合"全真模考版"（M3/M4 使用），不适合日常训练。
2. **听力**：Section 1/2 混用一文本三种题型，非真题结构（S1 应为对话+表单/笔记，S2 应为独白+地图/多选）；多选题 A/B/D 三个选项均直接出现在原文，答案 A、D 存在歧义，B 同样成立——命题失效。
3. **阅读**：Q2 答案依据不稳（原文限定 "in some areas"，陈述无限定 → 按官方口径 FALSE 更标准；答案理由 "no indication that every case is exactly this" 逻辑站不住）。
4. **口语**：Part 2 cue card 第 4 点 "explain why" 未以 bullet 呈现；Part 3 使用 PEEL 而非统一 C-R-E-D；材料未内建三查清单与禁用清单。
5. **写作**：吸收版缺示范与结构拆解（仅题目）。
6. **升级动作**：提示词守则已按本审计升级（v6），新增命题校验与总量硬约束；口语/写作统一 C-R-E-D；明确"单元版"与"全真模考版"双模式。
