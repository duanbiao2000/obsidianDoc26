### Part 2（准备 1 分钟 + 讲述 2 分钟）

- Cue card：Describe a time when you learned something new.（what you learned / how you learned it / why you decided to learn it / and explain how you felt about it）
- 我的讲述（录音 / 文字）：
  "I'd like to talk about a time when I learned to use an AI writing assistant to improve my English. Actually, I had been studying English on my own for a while, but my writing still felt clumsy and unnatural. One day I came across a tool that could correct my sentences and suggest better ways to express my ideas.
  I decided to learn how to use it properly because I really wanted to become more fluent in writing. In the future, I hope to join international communities or even work in a more open environment. So good English is essential for me.
  As for how I learned it, I made it a daily habit. Every morning, I would write a short paragraph about what I did the day before and something I was thinking about. Then, I would paste it into the AI and ask it to revise. I didn't just copy the corrections; I compared my original version with the revised one, word by word. I paid close attention to vocabulary choices, grammar, or sentence structure. Sometimes I even asked the AI to explain why it made a certain change, and that really helped me understand the logic behind it.
  Why did I choose these specific things to learn? As I mentioned, I wanted to communicate better with people from different cultures. I also realized that my social circle is quite closed, and improving my English would be a first step to connect with like-minded people online. So this learning feels very practical and meaningful to me.
  Now about my feelings. At the beginning, I was a bit discouraged because I saw so many mistakes in my writing. It made me feel that my English was worse than I thought. But after a few weeks of consistent practice, I started to notice real improvement. My sentences became clear, and I could express more complex thoughts. Every time I saw a revised version that sounded much better than mine, I felt a sense of achievement. That excitement kept me going. Now I actually enjoy the process and I feel much more confident writing in English. It's not just about learning a tool, it's about growing step by step."

### Part 3（4-5 分钟）

- Q1-Q5 与我的回答：
  1. "I believe technology makes learning more efficient, especially when you need instant feedback. Traditional methods like textbooks or lectures are quite passive, and you usually have to wait a long time to know if you are correct, which slows down progress. For instance, when I practice English writing with AI tools, I get a revised version immediately. I can compare my original sentences with the suggestions side by side, which helps me understand my weak points much faster than waiting for a teacher's feedback. However, I don't think technology completely replaces traditional methods. Deep reading of physical books and memorizing basics still build a solid foundation, so the best approach is to combine both."
  2. "I don't fully agree with that opinion because it really depends on how you use the tools. If you simply copy and paste the AI's correction without thinking, then yes, you will become lazy. But if you treat it as a partner and actively question it, you can learn a lot. In my own daily practice, I never just accept the written version. I always compare each change and ask the AI, 'Why did you change this?' That forces me to think about grammar and vocabulary patterns. So in my experience, AI acts like a 24/7 personal coach. It only makes you lazy if you are already a passive learner. For active learners, it actually speeds up the learning curve significantly."
  3. "I think many people stop learning because they lose a clear sense of purpose and mistakenly view education as something that only happens in a classroom after graduation. Work and daily routines take up most of their energy, leaving little mental space for exploring new topics. From what I observe around me, young people are often exhausted by long working hours, while older retired people tend to spend their free time on light entertainment rather than deep or meaningful discussions. Learning is linked to exams and grades, not to personal curiosity. They forget that developing new skills can actually be a lifelong hobby or a way to stay mentally active."
  4. "Ideally, I believe learning with other like-minded people is more rewarding. Even though I mostly learn alone right now, studying together allows you to exchange different perspectives, and it also keeps you motivated because you cannot isolate yourself. For example, I am open to my personal perspective, which is efficient for deep focus. However, I really miss having a small group to brainstorm with. It feels like visiting a foreign art museum. The shared experience gives you extra inspiration that you can get from a screen. That said, learning alone helps you build a strong personal foundation, but joining a large community and organization leader can push your creativity to a much higher level."
  5. "In my view, the role of a teacher should shift from being a knowledge giver to being a guide and an inspiration source. Since AI can already provide facts, correct mistakes, and give examples much faster than any human, teachers no longer need to simply teach information. Instead, they should create immersive experiences for students. For instance, when teaching art, a good teacher should take students to galleries or encourage them to feel the atmosphere of a city like Barcelona, rather than just reading theories from a textbook. Ultimately, a teacher's real value lies in sparking curiosity, facilitating open discussion, and providing emotional support. These are human qualities that AI couldn't easily replicate, especially for adults who want to grow in a more civilized and open environment."
