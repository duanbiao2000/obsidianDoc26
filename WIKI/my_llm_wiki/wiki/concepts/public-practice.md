# Public Practice

**Public Practice** in language learning refers to the intentional act of seeking out public spaces to interact with native speakers or other users of the target language. It is a method for generating high-volume, spontaneous conversational opportunities.

## Definition

Unlike structured classroom settings or planned conversations, public practice involves initiating interactions in everyday environments (e cafes, parks, transport hubs) with strangers or acquaintances. The goal is to maximize exposure and usage frequency.

## Role in Language Acquisition

*   **High-Volume Interaction:** Provides the necessary environment for strategies like the **[[200-mistakes-a-day-strategy]]**, where a large number of interactions are needed to reach error targets.
*   **Real-World Feedback:** Offers immediate, authentic feedback on comprehensibility and appropriateness.
*   **Anxiety Exposure:** Directly confronts **[[overcoming-language-anxiety]]** by forcing the learner to operate outside their comfort zone.

## Implementation

*   **Location Selection:** Choose areas with high density of target language speakers.
*   **Initiation:** Develop simple openers or questions to start conversations.
*   **Mindset:** Adopt a mindset of curiosity and learning, where rejection or confusion is part of the process.

## Comparison with Other Methods

*   **Vs. [[group-discussions]]:** Public practice is often more spontaneous and involves strangers, whereas group discussions are typically structured and involve known peers.
*   **Vs. Classroom Learning:** Public practice lacks the scaffolding and immediate correction of a classroom, requiring greater self-reliance and resilience.
