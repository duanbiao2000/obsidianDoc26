# IELTS Speaking Social Rudeness Framework

The **IELTS Speaking Social Rudeness Framework** is an original analytical framework that reframes common errors in the IELTS Speaking test as **social faux pas** rather than purely linguistic mistakes. The framework argues that the examiner's core question is social: *"Would I be comfortable having an extended conversation with this person in a professional setting?"*

## Core Principle

The framework treats language errors as **social signals** that make the examiner feel uncomfortable, rather than as technical violations of grammar or vocabulary. The key insight is that **the same error can have different social impacts** depending on how it is delivered.

## Key Dimensions

The framework maps specific candidate behaviors to the four IELTS scoring criteria:

| Behavior | Social Interpretation | Scoring Impact |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|
| Mechanical greeting | "This person is executing a script" | Fluency + Overall Impression |
| Vocabulary repetition | "This person has limited resources" | Lexical Resource |
| No logical structure | "This person cannot organize thoughts" | Coherence |
| Absolute positions | "This person cannot hold a nuanced view" | Nuance |
| Rising intonation | "This person lacks confidence" | Intelligibility |

## Application

The framework is used to **diagnose** errors, not just to **describe** them. It provides a path from identifying the social signal to correcting the underlying behavior. The document's core recommendation is to **eliminate the "performance vibe"** (表演感) — the sense that the candidate is executing a test procedure rather than having a conversation.

## Limitations

The framework is **prescriptive** (it tells candidates what to do) but **not descriptive** (it does not prove that these behaviors are actually the most common or most damaging). It is the author's own analysis, not a validated assessment tool.
