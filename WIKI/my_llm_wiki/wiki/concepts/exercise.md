# Exercise (for Learning)

Physical exercise is recognized as a supporting factor for cognitive function and learning. It is rated **B Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Mechanisms

- Increases **blood flow** and **oxygen** to the brain.
- Promotes **neurogenesis** (growth of new neurons) in the hippocampus.
- Releases **neurotransmitters** (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine) that improve mood and focus.
- Enhances **memory consolidation** when performed after learning.

## Recommendations

- **Aerobic exercise** (running, swimming, cycling) — 20-30 minutes, 3-5 times per week.
- **Timing** — Exercise before study can improve focus; exercise after study can enhance consolidation.
- **Breaks** — Short physical activity breaks during study sessions (e.g., walking, stretching).

## Relationship to Other Techniques

- [[sleep]] — Exercise improves sleep quality, which is essential for memory consolidation.
- [[study-planning]] — Scheduling exercise as part of a study plan.
