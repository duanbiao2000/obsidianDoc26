# Spaced Repetition

Spaced repetition is a learning technique where review sessions are scheduled at increasing intervals over time, rather than massed practice. It is rated **A Tier** (Table 1) and **S Tier** (Table 2) in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Mechanism

Spaced repetition exploits the **spacing effect** — information is better remembered if study sessions are spaced out over time. Each review session reactivates the memory trace, strengthening it and extending the interval before the next review. This aligns with the concept of [[desirable-difficulty]].

## Implementation

- **Flashcard apps** like [[Anki]] and [[RemNote]] implement spaced repetition algorithms (e.g., SM-2).
- **Manual scheduling** — planning review sessions at increasing intervals (1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month).
- **Leitner system** — physical flashcards sorted into boxes based on recall success.

## Relationship to Other Techniques

- [[active-recall]] — Spaced repetition is most effective when combined with active recall (retrieving information from memory).
- [[interleaving]] — Mixing topics across spaced sessions further enhances learning.
- [[retrieval-practice]] — Spaced repetition is a scheduling strategy for retrieval practice.

## Evidence

Spaced repetition is one of the most well-supported learning techniques in cognitive psychology (Cepeda et al., 2006; Dunlosky et al., 2013).
