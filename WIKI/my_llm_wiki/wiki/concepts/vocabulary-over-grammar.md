# Vocabulary Over Grammar

A language-learning principle asserting that lexical knowledge (vocabulary size and word choice) is the primary driver of fluency, far more important than grammatical precision. The claim originates from the source "How to Get Fluent," which states "Words over grammar."

## Evidence

The claim is supported only by anecdotal experience. No empirical studies are cited. The boundary between "grammar" and "phrasing" is unclear — the same source also emphasizes "correct word order," which is a syntactic concern.

## Practical Implications

- Prioritize extensive listening and reading to build vocabulary naturally.
- Focus on natural phrasing and collocations rather than isolated grammar rules.
- For IELTS, vocabulary range and precision are weighted heavily in the scoring rubric.
