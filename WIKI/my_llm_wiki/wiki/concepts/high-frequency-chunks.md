# High-frequency chunks

High-frequency chunks are multi-word units used regularly and naturally in everyday spoken English. The key insight is that learners should treat them as **single sound units** rather than separate words.

## Examples

- "What do you think?" → /ˈwɒdjə θɪŋk/ (one sound unit)
- "Can I…?" → "Can I have a look? Can I help you?"
- "How was…?" → "How was your weekend? How was the film?"

## Why They Matter

Treating chunks as sound units builds fluency because:
- The brain processes them as a single unit, reducing cognitive load
- Once the chunk is automated, the speaker can flexibly swap the ending (e.g., "What do you think/do/like?")
- They mirror how native speakers actually produce speech

## Application at B1→B2

At the B1→B2 stage, high-frequency chunks are the **primary focus** for building spoken fluency. The mindset shift is from thinking in individual words to thinking in chunks and sounds.

## Relationship to Collocations

High-frequency chunks are a subset of [[collocations for natural English]] — they are the most frequent, everyday multi-word units. At B2→C1, the focus expands to less frequent but more nuanced collocations.
