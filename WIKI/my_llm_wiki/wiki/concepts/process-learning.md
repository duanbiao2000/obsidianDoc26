# Process Learning

**Process Learning** is a learning strategy that focuses on observing and adopting the operational systems of experts, rather than just acquiring specific technical knowledge. It involves studying *how* work is organized, not just *what* is produced.

## Key Components
- **File Structure:** How experts organize folders, assets, and project files.
- **Naming Conventions:** Standards for naming layers, components, and variables.
- **Workflow Sequencing:** The order in which tasks are executed (e.g., moodboard before wireframe).
- **Tool Configuration:** Setup of plugins, shortcuts, and environments.

## Benefits
- **Efficiency:** Adopting proven systems reduces setup time and cognitive load.
- **Scalability:** Structured processes are easier to scale and replicate.
- **Consistency:** Standardized workflows lead to more predictable outcomes.
- **Meta-Skill Development:** Builds a **Personal Operating System** that can be applied across different tools and projects.

## Application
Instead of watching a tutorial to learn a specific Figma plugin, a process learner observes:
1.  How the instructor names their layers.
2.  How they group components.
3.  How they transition from design to prototype.
4.  How they export assets for development.

This approach aligns with concepts like the **Second Brain** and **PARA Method**, emphasizing organization and retrieval over rote memorization.
