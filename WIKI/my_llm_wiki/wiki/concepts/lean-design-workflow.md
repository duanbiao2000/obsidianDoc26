# Lean Design Workflow

A **Lean Design Workflow** is a streamlined design process that minimizes upfront documentation and artifacts in favor of rapid iteration and direct concept creation. It prioritizes early engagement with real problems over abstract planning.

## Typical Process
1.  **Discovery:** Short questionnaire to understand goals and constraints.
2.  **Moodboarding:** Visual exploration of style and tone.
3.  **Concept Design:** Direct creation of high-fidelity designs (skipping wireframes/sitemaps).
4.  **Iterative Feedback:** Sharing refined concepts with clients for immediate input.

## Rationale
- **Wireframe Redundancy:** Wireframes often evolve directly into final designs, making them a redundant step.
- **Speed:** Reduces time spent on non-value-added documentation.
- **Authentic Feedback:** Clients respond more honestly to visual concepts than abstract wireframes.
- **Flexibility:** Allows for quicker pivots based on technical or aesthetic insights.

## Risks and Mitigations
- **Scope Creep:** Lack of detailed specs can lead to scope expansion. *Mitigation:* Clear initial brief and frequent check-ins.
- **Client Confusion:** Some clients prefer structured sign-offs. *Mitigation:* Educate clients on the benefits of rapid prototyping.
- **Team Coordination:** May be challenging in large teams. *Mitigation:* Best suited for solo builders or small, agile teams.
