# Collocations for natural English

Collocations are two or three words that typically go together the way a native speaker uses them. Mastering collocations is described as "super important" for moving from B2 to C1.

## Examples from the Source

| Learner Expression | Native Collocation |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| "That's a completely crazy idea" | "That idea is utterly absurd" |
| "I'm in strong pain" | "I'm in excruciating pain" |

## Why They Matter

- They make speech sound natural rather than merely correct
- They demonstrate nuanced understanding of register and connotation
- They are a key differentiator between B2 (functional) and C1 (natural) speakers

## How to Learn Them

- **Notice** collocations in authentic native input (podcasts, videos, conversations)
- **Record** them in context (not as isolated lists)
- **Use** them actively in discussions, debates, and explanations
- **Reference** tools like [[ozdic.com]] (a collocation dictionary)

## Relationship to High-Frequency Chunks

While [[high-frequency chunks]] are the most common everyday multi-word units (focus of B1→B2), collocations are broader and include less frequent but more nuanced combinations (focus of B2→C1).
