# TED Method

The **TED Method** is a learning framework designed to accelerate skill acquisition through active engagement rather than passive consumption. It stands for **Try**, **Execute**, and **Document**.

## Components

1.  **Try (T):** Initial exposure to the skill or concept. This involves diving into the material without expecting immediate mastery. The goal is to familiarize oneself with the basics and identify pain points.
2.  **Execute (E):** Practical application of the learned material. This step emphasizes "doing" over "studying." It involves implementing the skill in real-world scenarios, even if imperfectly. This phase often relies on [[delusional-confidence]] to overcome hesitation.
3.  **Document (D):** Recording the process, outcomes, and lessons learned. Documentation serves as a reference book or cheat sheet for future review and helps solidify knowledge by forcing the learner to articulate their understanding.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

The TED Method aligns with principles of [[active-recall]] and [[project-based-learning]] by prioritizing output and reflection. It contrasts with traditional rote learning by encouraging a cycle of continuous improvement and actionable feedback. The method suggests that learners should focus on quality rather than quantity, ensuring that each cycle of Try-Execute-Document leads to tangible progress.

## Application

This method is particularly useful for professionals transitioning into new roles or migrants adapting to new workplace cultures, where rapid adaptation and practical competence are valued over theoretical knowledge.
