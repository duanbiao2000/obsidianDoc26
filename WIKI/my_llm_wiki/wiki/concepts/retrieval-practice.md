# Retrieval Practice

Retrieval practice is a learning strategy that involves actively recalling information from memory without external cues. It is one of the two core criteria (alongside [[high-quality processing]]) for evaluating the effectiveness of any learning method.

## Key Forms

- **Free recall:** Recalling information without any prompts (e.g., closing a book and explaining a concept from memory).
- **Cued recall:** Recalling information in response to a specific prompt or question (e.g., flashcard with a question on one side).
- **Practice testing:** Taking practice tests or quizzes without referring to notes.

## Why It Works

Retrieval practice strengthens memory by forcing the brain to reconstruct information, which reinforces neural pathways. The effort of retrieval itself is a form of [[Desirable Difficulty]] that enhances long-term retention and transfer.

## Free vs. Cued Retrieval

- **Free/un-cued retrieval:** More challenging and more effective for building flexible knowledge. It reduces [[Cue-Dependent Forgetting]], where memory is locked to specific prompts.
- **Cued retrieval:** Easier but can create an [[Illusion of Competence]] if the cues become the primary trigger for recall.

## Application

- Close the book and write or explain a concept from memory.
- Use [[Sneaky Plagiarism]] to restructure content without looking at the source.
- Record yourself explaining a topic without notes.
- Use [[Anki]] for spaced retrieval practice, but ensure you are actively recalling rather than passively recognizing.
