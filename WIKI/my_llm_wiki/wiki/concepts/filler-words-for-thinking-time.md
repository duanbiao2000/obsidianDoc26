# Filler Words for Thinking Time

Filler Words for Thinking Time is a pragmatic strategy where speakers use discourse markers and hesitation sounds (hmm, I mean, honestly, actually, you know, basically) to buy processing time during conversation. It is Rule 3 of the [[natural-english-playbook]] framework.

## The Rule

Never respond with dead silence. One filler word buys you 2 seconds naturally.

|Situation|Use|
|---|---|
|Need a second to think|"Hmm, let me think..."|
|Slightly surprised|"Oh wow, actually..."|
|Reconsidering|"I mean, I guess..."|
|Softening opinion|"Honestly, I feel like..."|

## Why ESL Learners Avoid Fillers

- **Negative transfer from written standards:** Fillers are considered errors in writing
- **Fear of sounding unprepared:** Learners may believe fillers signal incompetence
- **L1 differences:** Some languages use different filler strategies or value silent pauses

## Reframing Fillers as a Skill

The playbook repositions fillers from a flaw to a skill. In native speaker discourse, fillers serve multiple functions:
- **Floor-holding:** Signal that the speaker is not finished
- **Cognitive planning:** Buy time for formulation
- **Social signaling:** Indicate thoughtfulness or consideration
- **Affective modulation:** Soften the impact of what follows

## Relationship to Verbal Reactivity

Fillers (Rule 3) and [[verbal-reactivity]] (Rule 6) both involve using small words to manage conversation flow. The distinction: fillers are used by the **current speaker** to hold the floor while thinking; reactions are used by the **listener** to signal engagement. Over-application of both can create verbal clutter.
