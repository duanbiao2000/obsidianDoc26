# Short Sentence Hammer

The short sentence hammer is a rhythmic device that places a brief, declarative sentence immediately after a long, complex one. The abrupt shift in rhythm creates a sense of finality and certainty, delivering the conclusion without elaboration.

## Syntactic Shape

```
[Long, complex sentence with multiple clauses]. [Short, blunt sentence].
```

## Argumentative Effect

The contrast in length and rhythm signals that the argument is complete. The short sentence feels like a verdict — it doesn't need to explain itself because the preceding structure has already done the work. The hammer delivers closure.

## Examples

- **Value of failure:** `Without that particular failure — humiliating as it was — I'd probably still be operating under the delusion that effort alone is a sufficient condition for success. It wasn't. It never is.`

The two short sentences at the end (`It wasn't. It never is.`) function like nails being driven into a coffin. The argument is already complete; the hammer sentences just seal it.
