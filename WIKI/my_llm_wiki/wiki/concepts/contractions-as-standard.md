# Contractions as Standard

Contractions as Standard is the principle that in casual spoken English, contracted forms (don't, I'm, it's, we're, they've, can't, won't) are the default, not an option. Uncontracted speech sounds robotic or hyper-formal. It is Rule 2 of the [[natural-english-playbook]] framework.

## The Rule

Use contractions always in casual speech. If you catch yourself not using a contraction, stop and redo it.

|❌ Uncontracted|✅ Contracted|
|---|---|
|I do not know.|I don't know.|
|I am not sure.|I'm not sure.|
|It is not a problem.|It's not a problem.|
|We are going.|We're going.|
|They have arrived.|They've arrived.|

## Why ESL Learners Avoid Contractions

- **Written-language transfer:** Many ESL learners learned English primarily through reading and writing, where contractions are often discouraged
- **Explicit instruction gap:** Textbooks may teach contractions but rarely emphasize them as a **mandatory** feature of spoken English
- **Perception of correctness:** Learners may believe uncontracted forms are "more correct" or "clearer"

## The Register Dimension

Contractions are not optional — they are a **register marker**. Using uncontracted forms in casual conversation signals:
- Formality or distance
- Deliberate emphasis (rare, marked usage)
- Non-native speaker status

## Relationship to Spoken Grammar

Contractions interact with [[spoken-grammar]] (Rule 8): spoken grammar constructions like "Who'd you go with?" depend on contractions for their naturalness. Without the contraction, "Who did you go with?" sounds slightly more formal, and "With whom did you go?" sounds written.
