# Spoken Vocabulary Register

Spoken Vocabulary Register is the principle that spoken English uses a different lexical set than written English. Formal, Latinate words common in academic writing (however, therefore, commence, assist) are replaced with shorter, more common Germanic equivalents (though, so, start, help). It is Rule 4 of the [[natural-english-playbook]] framework.

## The Rule

If the word appears in a formal essay, find its spoken equivalent.

|❌ Textbook|✅ Spoken|
|---|---|
|However|Though / But honestly|
|Therefore|So|
|In addition|Also / Plus|
|Nevertheless|Still / Even so|
|Commence|Start|
|Inform|Tell|
|Assist|Help|

## Why ESL Learners Use Textbook Vocabulary

- **Explicit instruction bias:** ESL curricula often prioritize academic vocabulary for test preparation
- **Transfer from reading:** Learners acquire vocabulary through reading and apply it to speaking
- **Perception of sophistication:** Learners may believe formal words make them sound more educated

## The Register Continuum

Spoken vocabulary is not a binary (formal vs. informal) but a continuum:

|Register|Example|
|---|---|
|Academic written|"The experiment commenced at 0900 hours."|
|Formal spoken|"The experiment started at 9 AM."|
|Conversational|"We started the experiment at 9."|
|Casual|"We kicked things off around 9."|

## Relationship to Advanced Vocabulary Resources

The playbook's Rule 4 provides a **selection criterion** for vocabulary resources like the [[advanced-english-vocabulary]] index: learners should prioritize words that are frequent in spoken contexts over those that are frequent only in written contexts.
