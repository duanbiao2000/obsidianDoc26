# SHARE Framework

The **SHARE Framework** is a five-pillar heuristic for structuring memorable speech, introduced by [[Yasir Khan]]. It is designed to optimize verbal communication for maximum retention and perceived value by leveraging cognitive biases such as contrast, self-relevance, and emotional encoding.

## Components

### S - Stand Out
*   **Principle:** The brain ignores sameness and retains contrast. Generic responses are filtered out by cognitive habituation.
*   **Application:** Break routine conversations. Be different from the expected norm.
*   **Example:** A hotel guest giving an enthusiastic, specific reply instead of the standard "I'm fine."

### H - Highlight What Matters
*   **Principle:** Cognitive load limits retention. Extraneous details dilute the core message.
*   **Application:** Distill the message to its core value. "Give the gift, not just the wrapping." Tell listeners exactly what to remember.
*   **Connection:** Resonates with Stoic ideals of focusing on the essential, though applied here for rhetorical effect.

### A - Address Them
*   **Principle:** People care most about topics related to themselves.
*   **Application:** Speak directly to the audience. Use "you" to make it personal. Align the message with the listener's interests and needs.

### R - Repeatable
*   **Principle:** Information that is easy to recall is more likely to be shared and retained.
*   **Application:** Use stories, phrases, metaphors, and analogies. Ensure the message is "sticky."
*   **Example:** A job candidate using a memorable story to distinguish themselves.

### E - Emotion
*   **Principle:** Emotional arousal (positive or negative) strengthens long-term memory retention. "People believe what they feel."
*   **Application:** Leverage emotional words and narratives. Understand that both hurtful comments and profound compliments stick due to emotional encoding.

## Applications

*   **Professional Communication:** Enhancing presentations and pitches to ensure key points are remembered by stakeholders.
*   **Personal Development:** Improving everyday social interactions by moving beyond generic exchanges.
*   **AI Prompting:** The framework can be adapted into a prompt engineering technique for [[Prompting / ChatGPT]], instructing AI to apply SHARE principles (e.g., "Make this response Stand Out and Emotional") to generate more engaging outputs.

## Critiques and Tensions

*   **Authenticity vs. Performance:** The advice to "Stand Out" and use "Emotion" could be interpreted as manipulative or performative if not grounded in genuine intent. This creates tension with values of authenticity often found in personal development.
*   **Simplicity vs. Nuance:** The directive to "Cut extraneous details" may conflict with complex topics requiring nuance. The framework does not explicitly address how to handle complex, non-binary information without oversimplification.
