# Feynman Technique

The Feynman Technique is a learning method named after physicist Richard Feynman. It involves teaching a concept to someone else (or to yourself) in simple terms to identify gaps in your own understanding. The technique is rated **S Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List," considered one of the most effective learning methods.

## Core Steps

1. **Choose a concept** — Select the topic you want to learn.
2. **Teach it to a child** — Explain the concept in plain, simple language as if teaching someone with no background knowledge.
3. **Identify gaps** — Where you struggle to explain simply, you have identified a gap in your understanding.
4. **Review and simplify** — Go back to the source material, fill the gaps, and refine your explanation.

## Why It Is Effective

- Forces **active recall** and **retrieval practice**.
- Exposes the **illusion of competence** — you may think you understand something until you try to explain it.
- Encourages **chunking** and **schema building** by requiring a coherent, simplified model.
- Aligns with the [[zone-of-proximal-development]] by pushing the learner to articulate knowledge at a foundational level.
