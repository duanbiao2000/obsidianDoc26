# Intercropping

Intercropping is a planting strategy that maximizes the use of space and resources by growing two or more crops simultaneously in the same bed. This technique is especially valuable during the establishment phase of long-term perennial crops, where the space between and around the main crop can be utilized for fast-growing annuals.

## Example from the Source

In the [[Little Spanish Farmstead]] homestead (Castellón, Spain), an asparagus bed (perennial, harvest expected starting 2028) is intercropped with:

- **Strawberries** — planted along the sides of the bed; their shallow roots do not compete with deep asparagus roots.
- **Lettuces** — planted in the back row, benefiting from shade cast by nearby straw bales.
- **Radishes** — planted in the front row; a fast crop that matures before the summer heat.

## Principles

- **Root depth differentiation** — choose companions with different root zones to avoid competition.
- **Light use** — place shade-tolerant crops in the shadow of taller plants or structures.
- **Seasonal succession** — plant fast crops that are harvested before the main crop requires full space.
- **Companion benefits** — some plants can deter pests, fix nitrogen, or provide ground cover.
