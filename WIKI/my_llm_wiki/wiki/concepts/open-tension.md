# Open Tension (Oral Strategy)

Open tension is a rhetorical technique in which a speaker ends a point or entire response with an unresolved question or a deliberately unanswered implication, rather than offering a tidy conclusion. In the context of high-stakes speaking assessments like [[IELTS]], it signals **boundary awareness** and **intellectual maturity** — the candidate shows they understand the limits of their own argument.

## Key Characteristics

- Ends with a problem that resists immediate resolution.
- Uses phrases like "But whether... — that's a question the evidence hasn't answered convincingly."
- Avoids safe, formulaic wrap-ups (e.g., "we might start to see a system that actually nurtures learners...").
- Demonstrates awareness of complexity and nuance.

## Why It Scores Higher

Band 8.0 criteria reward the ability to "develop topics fully and appropriately." An open-tension ending shows that development includes acknowledging counter‑arguments or deeper complications. It moves beyond the "two sides, then my opinion" template toward authentic critical thinking.

## Example from Source

> "Though whether institutions designed to sort people can ever be genuinely reformed to nurture them — that's a question the evidence hasn't answered convincingly."

The speaker does not offer a neat answer; instead, they leave the tension hanging, which implies a sophisticated understanding of systemic constraints.

## Relation to Other Concepts

Open tension is a specific instantiation of what [[口語高分底層邏輯]] calls "展示思維過程" — it makes the thinking visible rather than presenting a finished product. It contrasts with the [[導航式邏輯]] of "thinking aloud" in that it is a deliberate structural choice, not an incidental hesitation.
