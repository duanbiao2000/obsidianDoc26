# Assumption Competition

**Assumption Competition** is a mechanism in multi-agent systems where agents actively challenge each other's premises and hypotheses through structured dialogue. It is designed to embed **critical thinking** directly into the workflow, preventing groupthink and improving decision quality.

## Mechanism

In an assumption competition framework, agents do not simply accept previous outputs. Instead, they engage in hypothetical competitive dialogue:

1.  **Proposal**: An agent (e.g., Builder) proposes a solution or idea.
2.  **Challenge**: Other agents (e.g., Critic, Risk Analyst) identify potential flaws, risks, or alternative assumptions.
3.  **Refinement**: Agents (e.g., Improver) suggest modifications to address the challenges.
4.  **Synthesis**: A synthesizer agent integrates the diverse perspectives into a robust final output.

## Role in Agent Teams

In [[Agent Teams Skill]], assumption competition is facilitated by specific roles:

*   **Devil's Critic**: Explicitly tasked with finding flaws and challenging assumptions.
*   **Risk Analyst**: Focuses on potential negative outcomes and vulnerabilities.
*   **Issue Improver**: Proposes solutions to identified problems.
*   **New Feature Specialist**: Explores innovative alternatives.

These roles are configured with varying levels of skepticism and critical thinking styles, allowing users to tune the intensity of the competition.

## Benefits

*   **Avoids Groupthink**: By institutionalizing dissent, the system ensures that multiple viewpoints are considered.
*   **Stimulates Innovation**: Competitive dialogue encourages agents to explore non-obvious solutions.
*   **Improves Decision Quality**: Decisions are stress-tested against multiple criteria before finalization.

## Connection to Critical Thinking

Assumption competition operationalizes the concept of [[批判性思維]] (Critical Thinking) in AI systems. It moves beyond simple error checking to a deeper evaluation of underlying assumptions, logical consistency, and potential consequences.
