# Filler Transitionals

**Filler Transitionals** are generic connecting words or phrases (e.g., *also*, *furthermore*, *in conclusion*) that are used without adding logical progression or depth to an argument. They are considered "weak language" in the context of **IELTS** Band 9 performance.

## Problem

- They create the illusion of structure without actual logical flow.
- They are often overused (e.g., stacking *also*, *furthermore*, *additionally*).
- They do not advance the argument or clarify relationships between ideas.

## Examples

- **Weak**: "Also, furthermore, additionally, people are using phones more."
- **Strong**: "More critically, the ubiquity of smartphones has reshaped social interaction."

## Strong Alternatives

- *What follows from this is...*
- *The implications extend further...*
- *Conversely...*
- *In light of this...*

## Impact on Scoring

Excessive use of filler transitionals lowers scores in **Fluency and Coherence** (Speaking) and **Coherence and Cohesion** (Writing) by making the text or speech feel mechanical and lacking in sophisticated logical linking.
