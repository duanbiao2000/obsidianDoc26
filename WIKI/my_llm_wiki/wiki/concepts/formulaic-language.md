# Formulaic Language

**Formulaic Language** (or **Formulaic Sequences**) refers to fixed, pre-fabricated multi-word expressions that are stored and retrieved from memory as wholes, without online syntactic construction. These sequences are characterized by their rigidity and high frequency in native speaker usage.

## Characteristics

*   **Fixedness**: The components of the sequence rarely change (e.g., `by and large`, `kick the bucket`).
*   **Pre-fabrication**: They are learned and used as single units, bypassing grammatical assembly.
*   **Idiomaticity**: Often carry meanings that are not fully compositional (i.e., the meaning cannot be deduced solely from the individual words).

## Examples

*   **Social Routines**: `How are you?`, `See you later.`
*   **Discourse Markers**: `To be honest`, `Long story short`, `Anyway.`
*   **Idioms**: `Piece of cake`, `Break a leg.`

## Relationship to Lexical Chunks

Formulaic language is a specific type of **[[lexical-chunk]]**. While all formulaic sequences are chunks, not all chunks are strictly formulaic. Some chunks (like phrasal constraints: `a ___ ago`) allow for variability. Formulaic language represents the most fixed end of the chunk spectrum.

## Cognitive Processing

The use of formulaic language reduces cognitive load during speech production. By retrieving a whole phrase, the speaker frees up mental resources for planning subsequent content or monitoring pronunciation, leading to greater fluency.
