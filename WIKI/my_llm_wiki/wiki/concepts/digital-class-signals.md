# Digital Class Signals

Digital behavior has emerged as a new domain of class signaling, where both social awareness and social calibration are tested simultaneously. Both New York and London elite contexts penalize the same digital behaviors, though the reasoning may differ.

## Key Digital Behaviors and Elite Standards

| Rough Behavior | Elite Standard |
|---|---|
| Voice messages to people you barely know | Forces them to process your information on your timeline |
| Leaving someone on read for 24+ hours without explanation | A failure of basic courtesy |
| Mass-forwarding greetings — obviously templated | Worse than sending nothing |
| LinkedIn request with no message | A cold call is a cold call |
| "Did you see my message?" sent immediately after | Zero boundary awareness |
| Messy background on video calls | Your private space signals your standards |
| Camera off with no explanation | Unilaterally making yourself invisible is rude |
| Forwarding unverified information | Reveals a failure of judgment |

## Why It Matters

Digital behavior is a relatively new class signal, which means:
- Older generations may not have ingrained habits, making it a level playing field.
- It is highly visible and easily judged (video backgrounds, response times).
- It tests both **social awareness** (respecting others' time and attention) and **social calibration** (knowing the appropriate medium and tone for the relationship).

## Practical Implications

- Treat digital communication with the same care as in-person interaction.
- Respect others' time: don't send voice messages to strangers, don't follow up immediately.
- Maintain standards: keep video backgrounds tidy, send personalized messages.
- Verify before forwarding: judgment is on display.
