# Vague Quantifiers

**Vague Quantifiers** are non-specific words used to describe amount or frequency, such as *many*, *a lot*, *some*, *various*, or *few*. In **IELTS**, their overuse is a marker of **Band 7** or lower performance, as they reduce information density and precision.

## Why They Are Weak

- They lack specificity and fail to convey the scale or nature of the issue.
- They suggest the candidate lacks the vocabulary or knowledge to be precise.
- They contribute to "undisciplined thinking" by allowing generalizations without evidence.

## Examples

- **Weak**: "Many people believe that a lot of countries have some kind of problem with inequality."
- **Strong**: "Wealth concentration has intensified across OECD nations over the past four decades, leaving the bottom quintile structurally excluded."

## Strategy for Improvement

- Replace *many* with *a significant majority*, *numerous*, or specific demographics.
- Replace *a lot* with *substantial*, *considerable*, or *extensive*.
- Replace *some* with *certain*, *specific*, or *particular*.
- Ask: "Can I be more specific?" If yes, replace the vague quantifier.
