# Key Selection Criteria

Key Selection Criteria (KSC) are the essential skills, qualifications, and attributes listed in Australian job advertisements that applicants must demonstrate they possess. Addressing KSC is a critical step in the Australian job application process.

## Purpose

KSC help employers efficiently screen applicants by matching stated requirements against demonstrated experience. They serve as the primary filter before resume review in many organizations, particularly in government, education, healthcare, and non-profit sectors.

## How to Address Key Selection Criteria

- Read each criterion carefully and understand what the employer is seeking
- Provide specific examples using the [[STAR Method]] (Situation, Task, Action, Result) where possible
- Use the same language and terminology as the job advertisement
- Address each criterion separately, either in a dedicated statement or within the cover letter
- Be honest — claims may be verified during reference checks

## Common Types of Criteria

- **Essential Criteria**: Must-have qualifications or experience; applications not addressing these are typically rejected
- **Desirable Criteria**: Nice-to-have attributes that differentiate candidates
- **Behavioral Criteria**: Interpersonal skills, teamwork, communication, problem-solving
- **Technical Criteria**: Specific tools, software, methodologies, or certifications

## Relevance to Newcomers

For candidates with little local experience, the [[education-first-resume-strategy]] can help address KSC by highlighting transferable skills from non-professional contexts. The guide by [[Living Simply Australia]] recommends being strategic about which criteria to emphasize based on the applicant's background and the role's requirements.
