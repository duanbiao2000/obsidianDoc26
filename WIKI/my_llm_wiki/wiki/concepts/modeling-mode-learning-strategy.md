# Modeling Mode (Learning Strategy)

**Modeling Mode** is a metacognitive learning strategy designed to combat [[cognitive-load-management|Cognitive Load]] and prevent [[negative-transfer]]. It involves switching from passive information absorption to active structural mapping when a learner experiences "mental clutter" or "hits a wall."

## Workflow

1.  **Trigger:** The learner detects signs of overload (e.g., inability to retain new facts, feeling "fried").
2.  **Stop Cramming:** Cease the intake of new raw data.
3.  **Visual Mapping:** Externalize current understanding by creating visual aids such as:
    *   Timelines
    *   Flowcharts
    *   Mind Maps
4.  **Integration Test:** Attempt to connect new information to existing [[schema-learning|schemas]].
5.  **Diagnostic Check:** Evaluate the "fit" of the connections.
    *   **Natural Click:** Proceed with integration.
    *   **Awkward/Forced:** Suspect [[negative-transfer]]. Investigate counterexamples. Discard flawed models.

## Purpose

The primary goal of Modeling Mode is to ensure that new knowledge is accurately integrated into long-term memory without corrupting existing mental models. It emphasizes **quality of processing** over quantity of input, aligning with principles of [[active-recall]] and [[self-explanation]].

## Relation to Other Concepts

*   **[[Chunking]]:** Modeling Mode often involves breaking down complex systems into smaller, manageable chunks to visualize their relationships.
*   **[[Feynman Technique]]:** Both strategies require simplifying and explaining concepts to verify understanding.
*   **[[Second Brain]]:** Visual maps created in Modeling Mode can be stored in a [[Second Brain]] system for future reference and refinement.
