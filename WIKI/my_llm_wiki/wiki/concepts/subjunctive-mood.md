# Subjunctive Mood

The **subjunctive mood** is a grammatical mood used to express hypothetical, wished, commanded, or contrary-to-reality situations. It is characterized by a "tense backshift" mechanism: the more unreal the situation, the further back the tense moves.

## Core Principle: Tense Backshift

The unifying logic of the subjunctive is that the tense is shifted "one step back" from reality:

- **Should-type**: Base form (lightest backshift — expresses suggestion/command)
- **Wish/If present**: Past tense (expresses present unreality)
- **Wish/If past**: Past perfect (expresses past unreality)

## Three Categories

### 1. If-type (Conditional Subjunctive)

Used for hypothetical conditions contrary to reality.

| Type | If-clause | Main clause |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Present | if + past simple | would + base verb |
| Past | if + past perfect | would have + past participle |
| Mixed | if + past perfect | would + base verb |

**Examples:**
- If I **were** rich, I **would travel** the world. (present unreal)
- If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed**. (past unreal)
- If I **had saved** money, I **would be** free now. (mixed)

### 2. Wish-type (Wish Subjunctive)

Expresses regret or dissatisfaction about a situation.

| Target | Structure | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Present | wish + past simple | I wish I **knew** the answer. |
| Past | wish + past perfect | I wish I **had come** earlier. |
| Future (complaint) | wish + would | I wish it **would stop** raining. |

### 3. Should-type (Mandative Subjunctive)

Used after verbs of suggestion, demand, command, or recommendation. The that-clause uses the base form (or "should + base form").

**Trigger verbs:** suggest, recommend, insist, demand, require, order, propose

**Examples:**
- I suggest that he **(should) leave** immediately.
- It is important that she **(should) be** informed.

American English prefers the bare subjunctive (base form); British English often retains "should."
