# Hedging

**Hedging** is the use of linguistic devices to express uncertainty, caution, or politeness. In academic writing, it can be a tool for accuracy, but in **IELTS** contexts, excessive or weak hedging is often penalized as a lack of confidence or precision.

## Weak vs. Strategic Hedging

- **Weak Hedging**: Using words like *maybe*, *I think*, *sort of*, or *quite* to soften statements due to uncertainty or fear of being wrong. This lowers scores in **Lexical Resource** and **Fluency**.
    - Example: "I think pollution is quite bad."
- **Strategic Hedging**: Using cautious language to avoid overgeneralization when evidence is not absolute. This is appropriate in Band 9 writing but must be done with precision.
    - Example: "Evidence suggests that pollution may have long-term effects on respiratory health."

## Impact on IELTS Scores

The source [[8-journals--23-eliminate-weak-language--rtgasv]] argues that candidates should largely eliminate weak hedging to demonstrate **certainty** and **stance**. Examiners prefer clear, assertive arguments over tentative ones.

## Common Weak Hedges

- *I think...*
- *Maybe...*
- *It seems like...*
- *Perhaps...*
- *Quite / A bit / Somewhat*

## Strong Alternatives

- *It is evident that...*
- *Data indicates...*
- *Fundamentally...*
- *Must / Should* (for recommendations)
