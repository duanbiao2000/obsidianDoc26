# Cushioning Requests

Cushioning Requests is a pragmatic strategy in spoken English where requests are softened with modals, hedges, or polite frames rather than stated directly. It is Rule 1 of the [[natural-english-playbook]] framework.

## The Rule

Never start a request with "I want." Always add a modal or softener before the verb.

|❌ Direct|✅ Cushioned|
|---|---|
|I want water.|I'll have some water, thanks.|
|I want to know...|I was wondering...|
|I want you to help me.|Would you mind helping me out?|

## Why It Matters

Direct requests ("I want X") are grammatically correct but pragmatically marked in English. Native speakers perceive them as abrupt, demanding, or rude in most social contexts. Cushioning signals respect for the listener's autonomy and creates a cooperative rather than transactional tone.

## Common Cushioning Strategies

- **Modal verbs:** could, would, might, may
- **Past tense modals:** I was wondering, I was hoping
- **Hedges:** maybe, perhaps, possibly
- **Embedded questions:** Do you know if... / Could you tell me whether...
- **Softening frames:** Would you mind... / Is there any chance... / If it's not too much trouble...

## Relationship to Other Rules

Cushioning requests overlaps with [[certainty-downgrading]] (both involve softening) but serves a different function: downgrading certainty softens claims, while cushioning softens requests. Both are part of the broader [[esl-pragmatic-competence]] framework.
