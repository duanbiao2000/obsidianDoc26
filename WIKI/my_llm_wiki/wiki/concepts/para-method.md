# PARA Method

The PARA Method is a folder organization system for knowledge management that sorts information by what it means to you, not by date, source, or topic. It was developed by Tiago Forte and is recommended as the structural framework for organizing notes in [[Obsidian]] within a [[Second Brain]] system.

## The Four Folders

- **Projects:** Short-term efforts with deadlines (e.g., product launch, hiring plan, client delivery).
- **Areas:** Ongoing responsibilities without deadlines (e.g., team management, personal finance, career development).
- **Resources:** Topics of interest not currently active (e.g., industry trends, methodologies, tools).
- **Archives:** Everything that is finished or inactive.

## Key Principles

- **Sort by meaning, not by metadata.** A note's location depends on its current relevance to your life, not its creation date or source application.
- **Move notes when their "identity" changes.** When a project ends, it moves to Archives. When a resource becomes an active project, it moves to Projects.
- **Scales to thousands of notes.** The four-folder structure takes ~5 minutes to set up and remains manageable because it reflects your current priorities.

## Role in the Second Brain System

In the [[NotebookLM-Obsidian Pipeline]], PARA provides the organizational backbone. After rewriting insights from NotebookLM into Obsidian, each note is placed in the most appropriate PARA folder and linked to related notes across folders. This ensures that information is retrievable based on its current relevance rather than its origin.
