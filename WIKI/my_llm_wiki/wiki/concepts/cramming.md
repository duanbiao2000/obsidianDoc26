# Cramming

Cramming is last-minute intensive study before an exam. It is rated **C Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Why It Is Low-Rated

- **Poor long-term retention** — Information is quickly forgotten after the exam.
- **Stress** — Increases anxiety and reduces sleep quality.
- **Shallow processing** — Focuses on memorization rather than understanding.
- **Interference** — Overloading working memory reduces encoding quality.

## When It Might Help

- **Last resort** — Better than nothing for an imminent exam.
- **Familiar material** — Reviewing already-learned content.
- **Fact-based exams** — May help with rote memorization of facts.

## Relationship to Other Techniques

- [[spaced-repetition]] — The opposite of cramming; spaced practice is more effective.
- [[sleep]] — Cramming often sacrifices sleep, which is essential for consolidation.
- [[active-recall]] — Cramming can include active recall, but without spacing.
- [[study-planning]] — Cramming is a failure of planning.
