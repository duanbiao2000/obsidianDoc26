# Error-making Goal

An **Error-making Goal** is a deliberate learning strategy where the learner sets a high numerical target for making mistakes (e.g., 200 per day) rather than aiming for accuracy. This approach reframes mistakes from indicators of failure to indicators of activity and progress.

## Definition and Purpose

The primary purpose of an error-making goal is to shift the metric of success from *correctness* to *volume of attempt*. By setting a specific, high number, the learner transforms an abstract goal ("practice more") into a concrete, gamified daily task. This makes progress measurable and reduces the ambiguity that often fuels anxiety.

## Psychological Mechanism

*   **Reframing Failure:** Mistakes are viewed as proof of language usage. If one is not making mistakes, one is likely not pushing their linguistic boundaries.
*   **Anxiety Reduction:** By expecting and welcoming errors, the learner neutralizes the emotional cost of speaking. The goal acts as a cognitive shield against the fear of judgment.
*   **Process Orientation:** It exemplifies a "process-oriented" mindset where the act of trying is valued over the immediate outcome.

## Relationship to Learning Science

*   **[[desirable-difficulty]]:** The strategy is a practical application of desirable difficulty, intentionally introducing friction (social risk, correction) to enhance long-term retention.
*   **[[illusion-of-competence]]:** It counters the illusion of competence created by passive study. Active, error-prone speaking reveals true gaps in knowledge.
*   **[[active-recall]]:** Making a mistake in speech is a failed retrieval attempt. The strategy encourages high-frequency retrieval practice, where the "failure" serves as a data point for improvement.

## Implementation Considerations

*   **Feedback Loop:** Making mistakes is only useful if the learner receives corrective feedback. Without feedback, there is a risk of "practicing mistakes" or fossilizing errors.
*   **Environment:** Effective implementation often requires **[[public-practice]]** or high-interaction environments to generate sufficient opportunities for errors.
*   **Social Courage:** The strategy requires overcoming **[[overcoming-language-anxiety]]** and may be challenging for introverted learners or those in low-density language environments.
