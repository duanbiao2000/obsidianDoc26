# Jevons Paradox

**Jevons Paradox** is an economic theory stating that as technology increases the efficiency with which a resource is used, the total consumption of that resource increases rather than decreases.

## Application to AI and Productivity

In the context of Artificial Intelligence, Jevons Paradox suggests that AI-driven efficiency gains do not necessarily result in leisure time or reduced workload. Instead, the lowered cost of production (e.g., writing, coding, analysis) often leads to:

1.  **Increased Volume:** Expectations for output volume rise, absorbing the time saved.
2.  **Expanded Scope:** Tasks that were previously too expensive or time-consuming to attempt are now undertaken, increasing the total workload.
3.  **Efficiency Trap:** Individuals and organizations may find themselves working harder to keep up with the accelerated pace of production, rather than benefiting from the efficiency.

## Implications

*   **Productivity Optimization:** Strategies for [[Production Optimization]] must account for this paradox. Simply adding AI tools without intentional constraint setting or value re-evaluation may lead to burnout rather than liberation.
*   **Business Strategy:** Companies should anticipate that AI will enable them to do *more*, not just do the same things *faster*. This requires strategic planning to manage increased throughput and quality control.
