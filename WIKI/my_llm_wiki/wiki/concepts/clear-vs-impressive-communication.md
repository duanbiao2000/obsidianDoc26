# Clear vs. Impressive Communication

**Clear vs. Impressive Communication** is a theoretical framework that distinguishes between two modes of speech: one designed for accurate information transfer (Clear) and one designed for memorability and emotional impact (Impressive).

## Definitions

*   **Clear Communication:** Logical, informational, and unambiguous. Its primary goal is to ensure the listener understands the facts. It relies on abstraction and generalization.
    *   *Example:* "The project was delayed because of resource issues."
*   **Impressive Communication:** Sensory, emotional, and memorable. Its primary goal is to create a mental image or emotional response in the listener. It relies on specificity, contrast, and narrative.
    *   *Example:* "We ran out of people before we ran out of problems."

## The Hierarchy

The framework posits that these are not mutually exclusive but hierarchical. The optimal approach is **"Be clear first, then make it impressive."** Clarity ensures the message is received; impressiveness ensures it is remembered. Sacrificing clarity for style leads to confusion, while sacrificing impressiveness for clarity leads to forgettability.

## Psychological Basis

*   **Logical Processing:** Clear communication engages the brain's analytical centers. It is efficient but low-arousal.
*   **Sensory/Emotional Processing:** Impressive communication engages the brain's sensory and emotional centers. It triggers visualization and empathy, which are stronger drivers of memory retention.
