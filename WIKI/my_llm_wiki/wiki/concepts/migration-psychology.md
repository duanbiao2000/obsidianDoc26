# Migration Psychology

Migration psychology examines the emotional and cognitive processes individuals undergo when relocating to a new country. It highlights that migration is not just a logistical change but a profound psychological transition.

## Core Concepts

### Settlement Curve
The [[settlement-curve]] describes the typical emotional trajectory of migrants, often starting with honeymoon-phase excitement, followed by a dip into frustration and anxiety (culture shock), and eventually leading to adjustment and acceptance.

### Culture Shock
[[Culture-shock]] is the feeling of disorientation experienced by someone who is suddenly subjected to an unfamiliar culture, way of life, or set of attitudes. Symptoms include homesickness, irritability, and self-doubt.

### Impostor Syndrome
Many migrants experience [[impostor-syndrome]], feeling like frauds despite their qualifications. This is exacerbated by the need to "start from zero" in a new environment, where previous achievements may not be recognized.

### Resilience and Mental Health
The source emphasizes the importance of [[resilience]] and proactive mental health management. Neglecting mental health can have a massive impact on the migration experience. Seeking support from credential therapists or community groups is recommended to navigate the "roller coaster of emotions."

## Strategies for Coping

*   **Financial Cushion:** Reducing financial stress allows for better mental space to adapt.
*   **Social Connection:** Building a sense of belonging through local communities.
*   **Realistic Expectations:** Understanding that adaptation takes time and effort, and that "rainbows and butterflies" are not guaranteed.
