# Discourse Management in Speaking

**Discourse Management** in the context of IELTS Speaking refers to the ability to control the flow of conversation, manage thinking time, and signal logical relationships without relying on fillers. This is a key differentiator between Band 7 and Band 9 in the Speaking exam, particularly in Part 3.

## Band 7: Hiding Behind Fillers
Band 7 candidates often use fillers to buy time while they search for words or ideas. This makes their thinking process invisible and can disrupt fluency scores.
*   **Common Fillers:** "Um," "Uh," "You know," "I mean," "Well."
*   **Example:** "Hmm, that's a good question… I think… um… maybe… people should… you know… care more about the environment."

## Band 9: Making Thinking Visible
Band 9 candidates use **discourse markers** to manage time while simultaneously signaling their logical structure. This keeps the examiner engaged and demonstrates higher-level control.
*   **Functional Replacements:**
    *   Instead of "Um/Uh": Use "The way I'd frame this is…"
    *   Instead of "That's a good question": Start directly or say "There are two ways to approach this."
    *   Instead of "I'm not sure but maybe": Use "The evidence points in different directions, but…"
*   **Example:** "That's a tension worth unpacking — on one hand X, but the more I think about it, the stronger case is actually Y, because…"

## Key Strategies

1.  **Use Functional Phrases:** Replace empty sounds with phrases that add value to the response.
2.  **Signal Logic:** Use markers like "That said," "To put it another way," "What’s often overlooked is," and "I’d argue that" to guide the listener.
3.  **Natural Pausing:** Allow brief pauses (0.5 seconds) for thought, but avoid filling them with "uh/um/like."
4.  **Intonation and Stress:** Use stress on content words and new information to convey attitude and logical weight, rather than speaking in a flat monotone.

## Connection to Shadowing
This concept complements [[Shadowing]] techniques, where learners practice reproducing not just words, but the rhythm, pacing, and functional use of discourse markers found in native speech.
