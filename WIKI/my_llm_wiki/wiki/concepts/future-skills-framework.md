# Future Skills Framework

The **Future Skills Framework**, proposed by [[Bernard Marr]], posits that success in a digital world requires a balanced portfolio of 20 specific skills. These skills are categorized into four domains: Technical, Personal, Interpersonal, and Career.

## Core Thesis

The framework argues that while technical skills (such as [[Digital Literacy]] and [[Data Literacy]]) are necessary hygiene factors, they are increasingly commoditized by AI and automation. Therefore, the primary differentiators for professional value are "unique human skills": the abilities to think critically, feel empathy, adapt to change, and connect with others.

## Categories

1.  **Technical Skills**: Foundational competencies including [[Digital Literacy]], [[Data Literacy]], [[Technical Skills]], and [[Digital Threat Awareness]].
2.  **Personal Skills**: Internal capabilities such as [[Critical Thinking]], [[Judgment and Complex Decision Making]], [[Creativity]], [[Adaptability and Flexibility]], [[Ethical Awareness]], [[Time Management]], [[Curiosity and Continuous Learning]], [[Embracing and Celebrating Change]], and [[Looking After Yourself]].
3.  **Interpersonal Skills**: Social capabilities including [[Emotional Intelligence & Empathy]], [[Collaboration & Working in Teams]], [[Interpersonal Communication]], and [[Cultural Intelligence and Diversity Consciousness]].
4.  **Career Skills**: Professional orientations such as [[Working in Gigs]], [[Leadership Skills]], and [[Brand of "You" and Networking]].

## Application

This framework is particularly relevant for technical professionals (e.g., [[Data Scientist]], [[ML Engineer]], [[Staff Engineer]]) who must balance deep technical expertise with the soft skills required for leadership, ethical decision-making, and effective collaboration in complex, automated systems.
