# Landauer's Principle

**Landauer's Principle**（兰道尔原则）is a physical law linking information theory and thermodynamics. Proposed by Rolf Landauer in 1961, it serves as the physical foundation principle of information erasure and, within this Wiki, acts as the theoretical root of cognitive-thermodynamic analogies.

## Definition

The principle states that any logically irreversible manipulation of information—such as the erasure of a bit or the merging of two computation paths—must be accompanied by a corresponding increase in entropy of the non-information-bearing degrees of freedom of the information-processing apparatus or its environment. This means:

- **Information erasure has a physical cost**: Deleting information is not a "free" operation; it necessarily generates heat (physical entropy increase).
- **Unification of information and physics**: Information processing is constrained by physical laws.

## Key Formula

Erasing one bit of information dissipates at least $k_B T \ln 2$ of heat, where:
*   $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant.
*   $T$ is the absolute temperature of the heat bath.

## Significance: Resolving Maxwell's Demon

Landauer's Principle resolves the paradox of [[Maxwell's-Demon]]. While the act of measurement can be thermodynamically reversible, the demon must eventually reset (erase) its memory to prepare for subsequent operations. This erasure process generates the heat and entropy required to preserve the Second Law of Thermodynamics, perfectly offsetting the apparent entropy decrease created by the demon's filtering, thereby proving that information processing is not free.

## Implications for Computing, Biology, and Cognition

*   **Computational Limits**: It sets a fundamental lower bound on the energy consumption of computing devices.
*   **Neurothermodynamics & Cognitive Compression**: Emerging research explores whether biological neural networks obey generalized Landauer-like bounds during memory encoding and decision erasure, suggesting that cognitive limits may have physical information-theoretic roots. This implies that cognitive compression mechanisms—such as information filtering and discarding during decision-making—might have thermodynamic foundations; the brain's process of "forgetting" or "ignoring" information carries a metabolic cost.
