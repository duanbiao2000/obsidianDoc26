# Divergent-Convergent Cognitive Cycle

The **Divergent-Convergent Cognitive Cycle** describes the mental processes involved in creative problem-solving, particularly in product development. It consists of two phases:

1.  **Divergent Thinking (Design):**
    -   Exploring multiple possibilities.
    -   Embracing ambiguity and open-endedness.
    -   Generating ideas without immediate judgment.
    -   Focus: "What could it be?"

2.  **Convergent Thinking (Development):**
    -   Narrowing down options to feasible solutions.
    -   Applying constraints (technical, budget, time).
    -   Executing specific paths with precision.
    -   Focus: "How do we build it?"

## Strategic Advantage
Individuals who master both phases (Hybrid Builders) can complete the full cognitive loop internally. This reduces the friction of handing off divergent ideas to convergent executors, ensuring that creative vision is aligned with technical reality from the start.
