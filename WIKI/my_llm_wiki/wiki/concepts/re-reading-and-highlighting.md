# Re-reading and Highlighting

Re-reading and highlighting are passive review methods that involve going over material again and marking key passages. They are rated **D Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List" (Table 2). This is the same concept as [[rereading]].

## Why They Are Low-Rated

- **Illusion of competence** — Rereading feels like learning but produces shallow encoding.
- **Passive processing** — No active retrieval or elaboration.
- **Fluency effect** — Familiarity with the text is mistaken for understanding.
- **Minimal transfer** — Does not improve ability to apply knowledge in new contexts.

## Evidence

Research consistently shows that rereading and highlighting are among the least effective learning techniques (Dunlosky et al., 2013).

## Alternatives

- [[active-recall]] — Retrieving information from memory.
- [[feynman-technique]] — Teaching concepts in simple terms.
- [[practice-problems]] — Applying knowledge to new situations.
- [[spaced-repetition]] — Reviewing at increasing intervals.
