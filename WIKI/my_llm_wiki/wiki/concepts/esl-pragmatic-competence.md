# ESL Pragmatic Competence

ESL Pragmatic Competence refers to the ability to use language appropriately in social contexts — understanding not just what words mean, but how to use them to achieve communicative goals in a way that sounds natural to native speakers. It encompasses discourse management, register awareness, conversational routines, and the strategic use of hedges, fillers, and reaction tokens.

## The Gap in Traditional ESL Instruction

Traditional ESL curricula emphasize:
- **Grammatical competence** (syntax, morphology)
- **Lexical competence** (vocabulary breadth and depth)
- **Phonological competence** (pronunciation, intonation)

However, they often neglect **pragmatic competence** — the knowledge of how to:
- Soften requests and disagree politely
- Use discourse markers to structure conversation
- Signal engagement through backchanneling and reaction tokens
- Calibrate formality and certainty to context
- Navigate turn-taking and topic management

## The Natural English Playbook Contribution

The [[natural-english-playbook]] framework operationalizes pragmatic competence into 10 teachable behavioral rules. This is significant because it transforms an abstract competence (often acquired only through immersion) into a **systematic, learnable skill set**.

## Key Components

| Component | Description | Example Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Request mitigation | Softening directness | Cushioning requests |
| Discourse management | Structuring spoken narratives | Narrative structure |
| Listener engagement | Signaling attention and reaction | Verbal reactivity, listener check-ins |
| Certainty modulation | Hedging and downgrading | Certainty downgrading |
| Register shifting | Matching formality to context | Energy matching, spoken vocabulary |
| Syntactic register | Using spoken rather than written syntax | Spoken grammar |

## Relationship to Communicative Competence

Pragmatic competence is one component of the broader [[communicative-competence]] framework (Canale & Swain, 1980), which also includes grammatical, sociolinguistic, and strategic competence. The Natural English Playbook primarily addresses the intersection of pragmatic and sociolinguistic competence.

## Open Questions
