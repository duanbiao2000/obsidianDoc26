# Latent Demand in AI Products

**Latent Demand in AI Products** is a product development strategy that identifies feature opportunities by observing how users "abuse" or use a tool in unintended ways to solve real problems. Introduced by [[Boris Cherny]], this approach treats user behavior—especially non-design-compliant usage—as the strongest signal for product iteration.

## Principles
*   **User Behavior as Signal:** If users hack or misuse a product to achieve a useful outcome, it indicates an unmet need that the product should formally support.
*   **Iterative Form Factors:** Start with minimal resources (e.g., a terminal interface) and iterate on the user interface based on how people actually use the tool.
*   **Validation through Abuse:** "Abuse" is not negative; it is evidence of strong demand for a capability that the current product design does not explicitly provide.

## Application in AI
In the context of AI agents like [[Claude Code]], users may push the model beyond its intended scope (e.g., using it for complex multi-step workflows not initially supported). Observing these patterns allows developers to build native support for these workflows, enhancing user satisfaction and retention.

## Contrast with Traditional Research
Unlike traditional market research which often relies on surveys or focus groups, latent demand analysis relies on actual usage data and behavioral patterns, providing a more accurate reflection of user needs.
