# Interleaving (Language Learning)

A study strategy where learners mix different topics or skills during practice, rather than focusing on one topic at a time (block learning). The source "How to Get Fluent" contrasts interleaving with rote memorization of word lists: "Block learning you are not going to learn them very well. Interleaving... you learn them better."

## Mechanism

Interleaving forces the brain to constantly retrieve and discriminate between different types of knowledge, strengthening long-term retention and transfer. It is well-supported by cognitive science research (though the source does not cite it).

## Application to Language Learning

- Instead of studying one grammatical structure or vocabulary set per session, mix multiple topics.
- For vocabulary, encounter words in varied contexts (reading, listening, speaking) rather than in isolated lists.
- Combine with [[Comprehensible Input (Language Learning)]] for natural interleaving through diverse content.
