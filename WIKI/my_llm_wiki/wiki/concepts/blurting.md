# Blurting / Brain Dump

Blurting (also called brain dump) is a learning technique where the learner writes down everything they know about a topic from memory, without cues or notes. It is rated **C Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Mechanism

Blurting is a form of **free recall** — it forces the brain to retrieve information without prompts. It reveals gaps in knowledge and provides a baseline for further study.

## Process

1. **Set a timer** (e.g., 5-10 minutes).
2. **Write everything** you can remember about a topic.
3. **Review** — compare your dump against notes or source material.
4. **Identify gaps** — note what was missing or incorrect.
5. **Repeat** — do another dump after studying to track progress.

## Limitations

- **Low structure** — The output is often disorganized.
- **No feedback** — Without comparison to source material, errors go uncorrected.
- **Shallow retrieval** — May only access surface-level knowledge.
