# Channel Naming Strategy

## Overview

Channel Naming Strategy is a systematic methodology for naming content channels, derived from a 100-name brainstorming exercise for an AI/technology/cognition channel. The framework organizes naming options into 10 thematic directions, each representing a distinct value proposition and audience positioning.

## The 10-Direction Framework

1. **Signal/Noise** — Positions the channel as a filter that extracts meaningful signals from information noise. Names: SignalLab, KeepSignal, TrueSignal, SNR Station.
2. **Depth Series** — Emphasizes deep analysis and going beyond surface-level understanding. Names: DeepDive, DeepThink, DeepRead, DeepLogic.
3. **Insight/Cognition** — Directly addresses cognitive upgrade and thinking methods. Names: InsightLab, MindEvolve, ThinkClear, MetaMind.
4. **AI Frontier** — Technology-oriented, emphasizing AI developments and practical tools. Names: AI Unpacked, AI Era, AI Lab Notes, ToolsOfTomorrow.
5. **Distillation/Refinement** — Emphasizes removing fluff and keeping core value. Names: Distilled, NoFluff, ExtractMind, HighDensity.
6. **Companionship/Growth** — Community-oriented, emphasizing shared growth. Names: AI Pacer, LevelUpTogether, GrowthSignal, SlowBetter.
7. **Clarity/Light** — Uses light and lens metaphors for cognitive clarification. Names: OneRay, SeeClearly, TheLens, ClarityGuide.
8. **Future Outpost** — Positions ahead of trends. Names: Outpost, TomorrowNotes, OneStepAhead, FutureNow.
9. **Curiosity/Questioning** — Centers on questioning as a method for discovery. Names: WhyThough, CuriosityLab, KeepAsking, SlowQuestions.
10. **Bilingual/Persona** — Lighter, brand-IP-oriented naming with personality. Names: AI Institute, SignalSquad, MindDev, CogLab.

## Application

This framework can be used to:
- Evaluate existing channel names against multiple positioning dimensions
- Generate new naming options by mixing elements from different directions
- Align channel naming with content strategy and audience expectations
- Test language dominance (English vs. native language) for global vs. local audiences
