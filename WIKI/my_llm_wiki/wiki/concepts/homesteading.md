# Homesteading

Homesteading refers to a lifestyle of self-sufficiency and subsistence agriculture, typically involving small-scale food production, animal husbandry, and the use of low-cost, regenerative methods. The concept has evolved from historical frontier settlement to a modern movement focused on resilience, food sovereignty, and ecological stewardship.

## Key Practices

Homesteading encompasses a wide range of techniques, including:

- **Soil preparation methods** — such as [[Straw Bale Gardening]] for passive weed suppression without plastic.
- **Perennial crop establishment** — planting long-term crops like asparagus, strawberries, fruit trees, and nut trees that yield for years.
- **Intercropping** — planting fast-growing annuals in perennial beds during the establishment phase to maximize use of space (see [[Intercropping]]).
- **Raised bed construction** — using locally sourced or bartered materials (e.g., logs) to create manageable growing areas.
- **Livestock integration** — using animals (chickens for weed control, donkeys for grazing) as part of the system.
- **Crop planning** — planting a mix of early and main crop varieties to stagger harvests and reduce storage pressure (see [[Potato Varieties (Determinate vs. Indeterminate)]]).

## Philosophy

Modern homesteading is increasingly framed as a response to global uncertainty. [[Food Self-Sufficiency]] is seen not just as a personal lifestyle choice but as a community and civilisational necessity. As documented in the [[Little Spanish Farmstead]] channel, the motivation shifts from "joy and privilege" to a deeper sense of responsibility in uncertain times.
