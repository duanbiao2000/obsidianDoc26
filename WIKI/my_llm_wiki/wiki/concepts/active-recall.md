# Active Recall

Active recall is a learning technique that involves actively retrieving information from memory without cues or prompts. It is rated **A Tier** (with the author noting it could easily be upgraded to S) in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Mechanism

Active recall strengthens memory traces by forcing the brain to reconstruct information. This process, known as the **testing effect**, leads to better long-term retention than passive review methods like rereading or highlighting.

## Examples

- **Practice tests** — answering questions without looking at notes.
- **Flashcards** — looking at a prompt and recalling the answer before flipping.
- **Free recall** — writing down everything you remember about a topic.
- **Feynman Technique** — teaching a concept from memory.

## Relationship to Other Techniques

- [[retrieval-practice]] — Active recall is the core mechanism of retrieval practice.
- [[spaced-repetition]] — Active recall is most effective when combined with spaced scheduling.
- [[interleaving]] — Mixing topics during active recall sessions enhances discrimination.
- [[desirable-difficulty]] — Active recall creates desirable difficulty, which improves learning.

## Evidence

Active recall is one of the most robust findings in cognitive science (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006; Dunlosky et al., 2013).
