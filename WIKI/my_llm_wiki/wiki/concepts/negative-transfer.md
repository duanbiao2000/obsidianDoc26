# Negative Transfer

**Negative Transfer** is a psychological and educational phenomenon where prior knowledge or existing mental models interfere with the acquisition of new information. Instead of facilitating learning, the learner's attempt to apply familiar frameworks to a new context leads to misunderstandings or errors.

## Mechanism

In the context of [[schema-learning]], learners naturally try to map new information onto existing structures. However, if the analogy is flawed or the contexts are sufficiently different, this mapping "backfires." The learner may feel a sense of awkwardness or cognitive dissonance when the old model fails to explain new phenomena.

## Identification and Mitigation

According to [[8-journals--35-how-to-actually-make-new-info-stick--cxmvk1|How to Actually Make New Info Stick]], negative transfer can be identified through **"Modeling Mode"**:
1.  **Visual Mapping:** Create timelines, flowcharts, or mind maps to visualize connections.
2.  **Detect Awkwardness:** If the fit between new info and old framework feels forced or unnatural, it is a signal of potential negative transfer.
3.  **Counterexample Testing:** Ask, "If my understanding is correct, what else should make sense?" If glaring counterexamples exist, the model is flawed.
4.  **Discard and Replace:** Immediately abandon the conflicting model and seek a new one.

## Relation to Other Concepts

*   **[[Illusion of Competence]]:** Negative transfer often creates a false sense of understanding because the learner recognizes familiar patterns, even if those patterns are misapplied.
*   **[[Chunking]]:** Breaking information into smaller chunks can help isolate where negative transfer occurs, preventing systemic confusion.
*   **[[Desirable Difficulty]]:** Recognizing and resolving negative transfer is a form of desirable difficulty that leads to deeper, more accurate schema construction.
