# Sneaky Plagiarism

Sneaky plagiarism is a learning technique that involves closed-book summarization where the learner completely restructures the content using their own mental model. The goal is to produce a version so reorganized that the original author would not recognize it as derived from their work.

## Why It Works

The technique simultaneously satisfies both criteria for effective learning:
1.  **High-quality processing:** It forces the learner to reorganize information, build new connections, and extract a personal model.
2.  **Retrieval:** It requires free recall from memory without external cues.

## How to Do It

1.  Study the material.
2.  Close the book or source.
3.  Write a summary (e.g., 150-200 words) that:
    - Uses a different structure than the original.
    - Rearranges the order of concepts and examples.
    - Explains relationships using your own framework.
    - Does not copy phrases or sentence structures.

## Application to IELTS

For IELTS reading and listening practice:
- After studying a passage, close it and write a "new article" on the same topic.
- Ensure the structure, example arrangement, and argument flow are different from the original.
- This serves as integrated speaking and writing training.

## Contrast with Traditional Summarization

Traditional summarization (deleting words, making bullet points) is often low-quality processing — it is editing, not thinking. Sneaky plagiarism is a form of deep processing that builds durable, flexible knowledge.
