# Interleaving

Interleaving is a learning technique where different topics or types of problems are mixed together during study sessions, rather than studying one topic in a block (blocked practice). It is rated **S Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Mechanism

Interleaving forces the learner to constantly identify which strategy or formula applies to each problem, strengthening **discrimination** and **retrieval** skills. This creates [[desirable-difficulty]] — the extra cognitive effort during practice leads to better long-term retention and transfer.

## Comparison with Blocked Practice

| Aspect | Blocked Practice | Interleaving |
|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| Structure | One topic at a time | Mixed topics |
| Short-term performance | Higher (feels easier) | Lower (feels harder) |
| Long-term retention | Lower | Higher |
| Transfer ability | Lower | Higher |

## Evidence

Interleaving is one of the most robust findings in cognitive science, supported by research in mathematics, motor learning, and category learning (e.g., Rohrer & Taylor, 2007; Kornell & Bjork, 2008).
