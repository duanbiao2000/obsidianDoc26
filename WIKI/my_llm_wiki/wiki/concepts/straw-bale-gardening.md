# Straw Bale Gardening

Straw bale gardening is a method of ground preparation and weed suppression using straw bales placed directly on the soil. Unlike plastic mulch, which can bake the soil and destroy soil biology in hot, dry climates, straw bales cool and protect the ground, leaving it soft, moist, and biologically active when removed.

## Technique

The process, as documented by [[Little Spanish Farmstead]] in Castellón, Spain:

1. Place straw bales on the target area for **4–6 months**.
2. After this period, remove the bales. The vegetation underneath is killed, and the soil remains alive and workable.
3. Move the bales to the edge of the newly prepared bed to continue weed suppression.
4. When fully rotted, use the decomposed bales as mulch. If still intact, move to a new area and repeat.

## Advantages Over Plastic

- **Does not bake the ground** — in hot climates, plastic traps heat and can reach temperatures that kill soil microorganisms and harden the soil surface.
- **Soil remains alive** — after bale removal, the ground is spongy, moist, and biologically active, ready for planting.
- **Transitions to mulch** — the straw decomposes into organic matter that can be incorporated back into the soil.

## Limitations

- **Slow process** — requires months of waiting.
- **Limited area per cycle** — bales cover a small footprint, so it is best for sequential preparation.
