# Chunking

Chunking is a learning technique that involves breaking complex information into smaller, manageable units (chunks). It is rated **A Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Mechanism

Chunking reduces **cognitive load** by grouping individual pieces of information into meaningful units. Working memory has limited capacity (approximately 7±2 items), but chunking allows each "item" to contain more information.

## Examples

- **Phone numbers** — grouped as (555) 123-4567 instead of 5551234567.
- **Language learning** — learning phrases rather than individual words.
- **Programming** — understanding a function as a single unit rather than individual lines.
- **Music** — learning a chord as a unit rather than individual notes.

## Relationship to Other Techniques

- [[interleaving]] — Chunking helps create the building blocks that can be interleaved.
- [[spaced-repetition]] — Chunks can be reviewed as units.
- [[active-recall]] — Retrieving a chunk is more efficient than retrieving individual items.
- [[feynman-technique]] — Teaching requires chunking information into digestible pieces.
- [[pomodoro-technique]] — Chunking tasks into Pomodoro-sized units.
