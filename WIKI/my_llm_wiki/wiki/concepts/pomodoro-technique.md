# Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method that involves working in focused intervals (typically 25 minutes) followed by short breaks (typically 5 minutes). It is rated **A Tier** in the source "27 Learning Techniques and Productivity Tools Tier List."

## Core Structure

1. **Choose a task** to work on.
2. **Set a timer** for 25 minutes (one "Pomodoro").
3. **Work** on the task until the timer rings.
4. **Take a short break** (5 minutes).
5. After four Pomodoros, take a **longer break** (15-30 minutes).

## Why It Is Effective

- Reduces **procrastination** by making tasks feel less daunting.
- Creates **urgency** and **focus** through the time constraint.
- Provides **regular breaks** to prevent mental fatigue.
- Helps with **time awareness** and **study planning**.

## Relationship to Other Techniques

- [[study-planning]] — Pomodoro sessions can be scheduled as part of a study plan.
- [[chunking]] — Breaking work into Pomodoro-sized chunks aligns with cognitive load management.
- [[sleep]] and [[exercise]] — Regular breaks and physical movement support cognitive function.

## Criticisms

- The fixed interval may not suit all tasks or cognitive styles.
- Some tasks require longer uninterrupted focus (e.g., deep work, creative flow).
- The technique may be less effective for tasks with high setup/teardown overhead.
