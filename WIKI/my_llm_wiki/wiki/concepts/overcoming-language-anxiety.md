# Overcoming Language Anxiety

**Overcoming Language Anxiety** is the psychological process of desensitizing oneself to the fear of judgment, embarrassment, or failure when speaking a non-native language. It is a critical component of achieving fluency, particularly in spontaneous social contexts.

## Nature of Language Anxiety

Language anxiety often stems from:
*   Fear of making mistakes.
*   Fear of negative evaluation by others.
*   Perfectionism and high self-expectations.
*   Lack of confidence in linguistic ability.

## Strategies for Overcoming Anxiety

*   **Reframing Mistakes:** Using strategies like the **[[error-making-goal]]** or **[[200-mistakes-a-day-strategy]]** to view mistakes as positive indicators of progress rather than failures.
*   **Exposure Therapy:** Engaging in **[[public-practice]]** to gradually increase comfort with social interaction in the target language.
*   **Process Orientation:** Shifting focus from the outcome (perfect speech) to the process (communication and effort).
*   **Self-Compassion:** Accepting that errors are a natural and necessary part of learning.

## Connection to Mindset

This concept is closely linked to **[[mindset]]** and **[[psychological-resilience]]**. A growth mindset allows learners to view challenges and errors as opportunities for development, reducing the emotional impact of anxiety.
