# 200 Mistakes a Day Strategy

The **200 Mistakes a Day Strategy** is a specific heuristic for language acquisition that involves setting a daily goal to make at least 200 mistakes. This quantitative target serves as a proxy for high-volume language usage and active engagement.

## Core Principle

The core principle is that mistakes are positive feedback signals. As stated in the source material:

> "I actually have a goal to make at least 200 mistakes a day because then I know I'm getting somewhere, I'm using the language!"

This shifts the focus from avoiding errors to maximizing attempts. The number 200 is arbitrary but significant enough to require sustained effort and interaction throughout the day.

## Methodology

1.  **Set the Target:** Define a high numerical goal for errors (e.g., 200).
2.  **Seek Interaction:** Intentionally seek out public spaces or contexts where interaction with native speakers or other learners is likely (**[[public-practice]]**).
3.  **Embrace Errors:** View each mistake as a successful data point indicating that the language is being used and boundaries are being pushed.
4.  **Monitor Progress:** Use the count of mistakes as a metric for daily activity and engagement levels.

## Benefits

*   **Gamification:** Transforms language practice into a measurable, game-like challenge.
*   **Anxiety Reduction:** Reduces the fear of judgment by normalizing and even celebrating errors.
*   **Fluency Development:** Prioritizes volume and flow over immediate accuracy, which can help build confidence and automaticity.

## Risks and Mitigations

*   **Fossilization:** Without corrective feedback, repeated mistakes may become ingrained. Mitigation: Pair with **[[shadowing]]**, **[[self-correction]]**, or regular feedback sessions with teachers/native speakers.
*   **Social Stress:** High-volume public interaction can be stressful. Mitigation: Start with lower targets or safer environments before scaling up.
