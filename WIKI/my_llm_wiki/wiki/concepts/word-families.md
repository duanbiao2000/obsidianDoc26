# Word families

Word families are the noun, verb, adjective, and adverb forms of a word. Mastering them is described as a neglected but important tool for C1→C2 learners.

## Example from the Source

| Form | Word |
|------|------|
| Verb | imply |
| Noun | implication |
| Adjective | implicit |
| Adverb | implicitly |

## Why They Matter

- They give the speaker **flexibility** to express the same idea in different grammatical structures
- They enable **precise expression** — choosing the exact form needed for the context
- They support [[style-shift drills]] by providing vocabulary options for different registers

## How to Learn Them

- When learning a new word, actively learn all its forms
- Practice using each form in a sentence
- Notice word families in authentic input and record them together

## Relationship to Other Concepts

Word families are a tool for achieving [[effortless clarity]] at C2. They complement [[collocations for natural English]] (which focus on word combinations) by focusing on word forms.
