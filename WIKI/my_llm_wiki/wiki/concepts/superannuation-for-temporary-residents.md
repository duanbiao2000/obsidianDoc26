# Superannuation for Temporary Residents

Superannuation is Australia's compulsory retirement savings system. Employers are required to contribute a percentage of an employee's ordinary earnings into a superannuation fund. For temporary residents (including international students and working holiday makers), specific rules apply regarding accessing these funds.

## How It Works

- Employers contribute a minimum percentage (currently 11.5% in 2024–25, gradually increasing to 12%) of an employee's ordinary earnings
- Contributions are paid into a super fund chosen by the employee (or a default fund if none is selected)
- Temporary residents can use the [[Australian Taxation Office]]'s Super Comparison Tool to compare fund performance and fees

## Departing Australia Superannuation Payment (DASP)

Temporary residents who have left Australia can apply for a Departing Australia Superannuation Payment (DASP) to withdraw their accumulated superannuation. Key points:

- The application is made through the ATO website
- Tax is deducted from the payment (generally 35% for most temporary residents, or higher for working holiday makers)
- The application must be made after departing Australia and the visa has ceased
- Funds cannot be accessed while still living and working in Australia

## Relevance to Job Seekers

Understanding superannuation is important for temporary residents working in Australia. The compulsory nature of super means it is not optional or negotiable. Job seekers should plan for super contributions as a component of their total compensation, and understand the process for reclaiming these funds when their Australian work period ends.
