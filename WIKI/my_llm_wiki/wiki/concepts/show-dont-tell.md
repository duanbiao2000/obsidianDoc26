# Show, Don't Tell

**Show, Don't Tell** is a narrative technique central to **US holistic admissions** essays. It involves using vivid, specific details, scenes, and actions to allow the reader to infer the writer's values, character, and growth, rather than explicitly stating these traits.

## Application in Admissions

In the context of elite US university applications (e.g., Harvard, Yale, Stanford), this technique is used to create an emotional connection and demonstrate authenticity. Instead of saying "I am resilient," a writer might describe the process of failing a math competition, the initial disappointment, and the subsequent decision to practice daily, thereby *showing* resilience through action.

## Contrast with UK Style

This technique stands in direct contrast to the UK academic style's principle of **"State, Support, Discuss,"** which prioritizes explicit thesis statements and logical argumentation over implicit narrative inference. While "Show, Don't Tell" relies on emotional resonance and subjective experience, the UK approach relies on objective analysis and evidence.
