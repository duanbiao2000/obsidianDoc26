# Genuine Override Authority

**Genuine Override Authority** is a design principle for AI-integrated workflows that ensures humans retain actual decision-making power rather than serving as nominal approvers. It distinguishes between tokenistic "human-in-the-loop" systems and robust oversight mechanisms.

## Core Components

*   **Cognitive Space**: Humans must have the time and mental bandwidth to thoroughly review AI outputs. High-volume approval rates (e.g., 10,000 decisions a day) indicate a lack of genuine authority, reducing humans to mere signers.
*   **Organizational Permission**: The culture must explicitly support and protect the right to say "no" to AI recommendations. Fear of bottlenecks or inefficiency often undermines this permission.
*   **Contextual Access**: To exercise override authority effectively, humans need access to the same context and data used by the AI to generate its output.

## Failure Modes

When override authority is nominal rather than genuine, humans become **"review machines."** They rubber-stamp outputs at speed, leading to:
1.  **Complacency**: Reduced vigilance due to trust in automation.
2.  **Accountability Gaps**: When errors occur, it is unclear whether the fault lies with the AI's generation or the human's approval.
3.  **Skill Erosion**: Lack of critical engagement prevents humans from refining their judgment over time.

## Implementation

To establish genuine override authority, organizations must:
1.  Limit the volume of AI decisions requiring human review to manageable levels.
2.  Provide clear guidelines on when and how to reject AI outputs.
3.  Reward careful scrutiny over rapid approval.
