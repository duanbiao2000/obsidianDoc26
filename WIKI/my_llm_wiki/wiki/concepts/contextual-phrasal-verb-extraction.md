# Contextual Phrasal Verb Extraction

**Contextual Phrasal Verb Extraction** is a language learning methodology that involves identifying and categorizing high-frequency verb phrases (phrasal verbs and idioms) within natural, unscripted conversational contexts, rather than relying on isolated textbook examples.

## Core Principles

1.  **Authenticity**: The primary value lies in using verbatim examples from native speakers (e.g., podcasts, interviews) to demonstrate real-world application, tone, and collocation.
2.  **Thematic Grouping**: Vocabulary is organized by semantic fields (e.g., "Relationships," "Life Experiences," "Resilience") to facilitate cognitive chunking and improve long-term retention.
3.  **Nuance Annotation**: Special attention is paid to pronunciation (IPA transcriptions) and non-literal meanings (Chinese glosses or synonyms) for phrases that are difficult to infer from context alone (e.g., *miss out on*, *put up with*).

## Application in Language Learning

This method supports [[IELTS]] speaking preparation and general oral fluency by providing learners with reusable, high-utility lexical chunks. It complements techniques like [[Shadowing]] and [[Active Recall]] by offering a structured dataset of authentic language use. By focusing on phrases like *pick up skills* or *stand up for*, learners can move beyond basic vocabulary to more nuanced, native-like expression.
