# Potato Varieties: Determinate vs. Indeterminate

Potato varieties are classified by growth habit as either determinate or indeterminate, which affects planting and hilling practices.

## Determinate Varieties

- **Growth habit**: Produces tubers in a single layer just below the soil surface. Once this set is formed, no further tubers develop up the stem.
- **Hilling requirement**: Minimal. After planting in a trench, cover with soil, and add straw around stems as they grow to prevent light exposure. No need for repeated mounding.
- **Example**: [[Alouette]] (purple skin, yellow flesh).

## Indeterminate Varieties

- **Growth habit**: Continues producing tubers progressively up the stem as the plant grows.
- **Hilling requirement**: Repeated hilling (mounding soil around the base) is necessary to protect developing tubers from light and encourage additional yield.
- **Examples**: Many traditional main-crop varieties.

## Practical Implications

- Determinate varieties are easier to manage for small-scale growers, as they require less labor for hilling.
- Planting a mix of early (determinate) and main-crop (indeterminate) varieties staggers harvests and reduces storage burden.
- Spacing: Determinate varieties like Alouette are typically planted 30–32 cm apart.
