# Frequency-Based Grading

Frequency-based grading is a principle in language learning and corpus linguistics where learning materials are prioritized according to how frequently they occur in natural language use. The assumption is that high-frequency items (words, phrases, grammatical structures) offer greater communicative utility and should be learned first.

## Application in PlayPhrase.me

[[PlayPhrase.me]] displays a frequency count for every phrase — the number of video clips available in its database. For example:

- "Nice to meet you." — 14,683 clips (very high frequency)
- "I'll see you later." — 3,910 clips (high frequency)
- "Just trying to say thank you." — 6 clips (low frequency)

These counts serve as a practical prioritization signal: learners can focus on phrases with many clips, which are likely more common and useful in everyday communication.

## Relationship to Corpus Linguistics

Frequency-based grading aligns with established corpus linguistics resources such as:

- **BNC/COCA frequency lists**: Word frequency lists derived from the British National Corpus and Corpus of Contemporary American English.
- **Nation's vocabulary levels**: Paul Nation's framework for prioritizing high-frequency vocabulary.

## Limitations

- Frequency in a media database may not perfectly reflect real-world spoken frequency.
- Low-frequency phrases may still be important for specific contexts or advanced learners.
- Counts are relative to the tool's underlying media library, which may have biases (e.g., overrepresentation of certain genres).
