# Food Self-Sufficiency

Food self-sufficiency is the practice of producing enough food to meet one's own needs, either individually or as a community. In the context of modern homesteading, it has evolved from a lifestyle choice into a form of resilience against global supply chain disruptions, economic uncertainty, and climate instability.

## Philosophical Shift

As articulated by [[Harriet and Mauro]] of [[Little Spanish Farmstead]]:

> "The world is going to need many, many more people working small plots of land growing food for themselves and their immediate community."

This represents a move from personal satisfaction ("joy and privilege") to a broader sense of responsibility in "these uncertain times." Food self-sufficiency is framed as both personal resilience and community contribution.

## Practical Strategies

- **Long-term perennial crops** — asparagus, fruit trees, nut trees that produce for years with establishment effort upfront.
- **Intercropping** — filling space in perennial beds with fast annuals while awaiting maturity (see [[Intercropping]]).
- **Low-input methods** — straw bale ground clearing instead of plastic mulch (see [[Straw Bale Gardening]]).
- **Crop diversification** — mixing early and main crop varieties to spread harvests (see [[Potato Varieties (Determinate vs. Indeterminate)]]).
- **Barter economies** — exchanging produce for materials (e.g., vegetables for logs).
- **Animal integration** — chickens and livestock for weed control and fertility.
