# Transient Hypofrontality

**Transient Hypofrontality**（暂时性前额叶功能低下）is a neurological hypothesis proposed by Arne Dietrich, suggesting reduced activity in the prefrontal cortex during states of deep engagement, such as [[flow-state]]. As the core neural mechanism explaining flow, this reduction temporarily silences self-referential thought, executive control, and self-monitoring, allowing for intuitive performance and the loss of reflective self-consciousness characteristic of flow.

## Key Features Explained by Transient Hypofrontality

The temporary suppression of the prefrontal cortex—which is responsible for executive function, self-reflection, and time perception—explains several key features of the flow experience:

- **丧失自我意识**: The self-monitoring function of the prefrontal cortex is weakened, leading to the loss of reflective self-consciousness.
- **时间扭曲**: Activity in time perception areas is lowered.
- **无焦虑的高效产出**: Executive control shifts from explicit cognition to an implicit automated mode.

## Evidence

Recent EEG studies with expert musicians support this hypothesis, showing reduced frontal lobe activity during high-flow improvisation, indicating a release of conscious control as expertise takes over.

## Contrasts with Related Concepts

- **vs. Synchronization theories**: Synchronization theories posit enhanced cross-regional communication without necessarily reducing frontal activity, whereas transient hypofrontality specifically posits reduced frontal activity.
- **vs. [[Hyperfocus]]**: Hyperfocus is an over-locking of attention, usually accompanied by anxiety and self-consciousness; Transient Hypofrontality is an ordered suppression of the prefrontal cortex, accompanied by inner pleasure.
- **vs. 正念**: Mindfulness strengthens prefrontal monitoring; flow temporarily removes this monitoring.
