# Five-to-Seven Person Golden Rule

The **Five-to-Seven Person Golden Rule** is a guideline for optimizing team size in multi-agent systems, particularly for medium-sized projects. It suggests maintaining between 5 and 7 agents to balance communication efficiency, decision speed, and coverage.

## Theoretical Basis

This rule is derived from two well-established management heuristics:

*   **The Two Pizza Rule**: Popularized by Jeff Bezos, it states that a team should be small enough to be fed with two pizzas. Larger teams tend to suffer from communication breakdowns and reduced accountability.
*   **Dunbar’s Number**: Anthropological research suggesting that humans can maintain stable social relationships with approximately 150 people, but effective collaborative groups are much smaller (often cited as 5-9 for close cooperation).

## Application in Agent Teams

In [[Agent Teams Skill]], this rule is applied to configure optimal agent teams:

*   **5-Person Team**: Ideal for fast delivery. Typically consists of 2 Builders, 1 Critic, 1 Improver, and 1 Synthesizer.
*   **6-Person Team**: Recommended for balanced innovation. Adds a second Critic or Improver to enhance critical thinking and refinement.
*   **7-Person Team**: Suitable for deep exploration. Maximizes role diversity but increases coordination complexity.

## Trade-offs

*   **Smaller Teams (<5)**: May lack sufficient diversity of perspective or specialized skills.
*   **Larger Teams (>7)**: Experience diminishing returns due to increased communication overhead and potential for conflicting inputs. For larger projects, the "Team of Teams" strategy is recommended, where multiple 5-7 person teams collaborate.

## Benefits

*   **Communication Efficiency**: Minimizes the number of interactions required for consensus.
*   **Decision Speed**: Faster alignment and execution.
*   **Role Coverage**: Ensures all critical functions (building, critiquing, improving, synthesizing) are represented.
