# IELTS Topic Scenarios and Corresponding Materials

This source provides a systematic analysis of the scenario overlap across the four IELTS skills (Listening, Speaking, Writing, Reading). Its central thesis is that the four skills share significant scenario overlap, but differ in emphasis: Listening focuses on "life service scenarios," while Speaking, Writing, and Reading focus on "social issue topics." This framework enables a cross-skill vocabulary reuse strategy for more efficient test preparation.

## Core Claims

1. **High scenario overlap, different emphasis.** The same vocabulary bank can be reused across skills, but depth requirements vary significantly.
2. **A unified vocabulary strategy is more efficient** than independent preparation for each skill. The recommended path: build a core vocabulary bank around Speaking+Writing social issue topics, supplement with life service words for Listening, and academic words for Reading.
3. **Each skill has exclusive scenarios** that cannot be cross-trained: Listening (renting, shopping, dining, banking), Reading (history, astronomy, linguistics, evolution), Speaking Part 1/2 (personal experiences, relationships, childhood memories).

## Cross-Skill Reuse Strategy

The source classifies vocabulary domains by reusability:

- **High reusability (four skills universal):** Education, Healthcare, Environment, Technology, Social Culture
- **Medium reusability (Speaking + Writing shared):** Urbanization, Globalization, Mental Health, Crime, Media
- **Low reusability (skill-specific):** Listening life scenario words, Reading academic science words, Speaking personal experience words

## Resource Recommendations

The source recommends specific video channels for each skill domain:

- **Universal (four skills):** TED-Ed, TED Talks, Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell
- **Speaking + Writing (social issues):** DW News/DW Documentary, BBC Ideas, Vox, The School of Life
- **Listening (life scenarios):** English in Action (BBC Learning English), 6 Minute English (BBC Learning English), Rachel's English
- **Reading (academic):** SciShow, PBS Eons, National Geographic, Crash Course

## Evidence Strength

- **Strong:** The scenario comparison table is comprehensive and internally consistent, based on observable IELTS test patterns.
- **Moderate:** Resource recommendations are based on qualitative assessment of content fit, not empirical testing.
- **Weak:** No citation of official IELTS test specifications or frequency analysis. The "high/medium/low reusability" classification is intuitive but not quantified.
