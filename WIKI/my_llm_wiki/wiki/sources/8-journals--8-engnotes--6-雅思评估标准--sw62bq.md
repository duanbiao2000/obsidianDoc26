## Key Entities
- **IELTS (International English Language Testing System)**: The central subject of the document; a standardized test for English proficiency.
- **FC (Fluency & Coherence)**: A scoring dimension for IELTS Speaking.
- **LR (Lexical Resource)**: A scoring dimension for both IELTS Speaking and Writing.
- **GRA (Grammatical Range & Accuracy)**: A scoring dimension for both IELTS Speaking and Writing.
- **P (Pronunciation)**: A scoring dimension for IELTS Speaking.
- **TA (Task Achievement)**: A scoring dimension for IELTS Writing Task 1.
- **CC (Coherence & Cohesion)**: A scoring dimension for IELTS Writing.
- **TR (Task Response)**: A scoring dimension for IELTS Writing Task 2.
- **PF (Prediction & Focus)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Listening errors, not an official IELTS metric.
- **PR (Paraphrase Recognition)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Listening errors.
- **DH (Distractor Handling)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Listening errors.
- **DA (Detail Accuracy)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Listening errors.
- **FS (Form & Spelling)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Listening errors.
- **LS (Locating Skill)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Reading errors.
- **PM (Paraphrase Mapping)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Reading errors.
- **I (Inference)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Reading errors.
- **QTS (Question-type Strategy)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Reading errors.
- **TM (Time Management)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Reading errors.
- **VT (Vocabulary Tolerance)**: A proposed "ability dimension" for analyzing Reading errors.

## Key Concepts
- **Official vs. Analytical Dimensions**: The source distinguishes between official IELTS scoring criteria (for Speaking/Writing) and analytical frameworks (for Listening/Reading). Official criteria determine band scores; analytical dimensions help identify specific skill gaps for training.
- **Paraphrasing/Synonym Replacement**: Highlighted as a critical skill across all four modules (Speaking LR, Writing LR, Listening PR, Reading PM). It is identified as a core mechanism for avoiding repetition in production tasks and recognizing meaning in comprehension tasks.
- **Distractor Handling**: Specifically in Listening, the ability to recognize when a speaker corrects themselves (e.g., using "actually," "but") is crucial for accuracy.
- **Inference (Not Given)**: In Reading, distinguishing between explicit information and logical inference is key, particularly for True/False/Not Given questions.
- **Production vs. Comprehension Logic**: The document implicitly contrasts productive skills (Speaking/Writing), which are judged on range and accuracy against rubrics, with receptive skills (Listening/Reading), which are binary (right/wrong) but rely on underlying cognitive strategies like scanning and prediction.
