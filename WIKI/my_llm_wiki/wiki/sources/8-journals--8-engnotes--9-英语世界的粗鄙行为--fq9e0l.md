# Rough Behavior in the English-Speaking World: New York vs. London Elite Social Codes

This source is an observational guide to social behavior that is perceived as "rough" (粗鄙) in elite contexts in New York and London. It provides a contrastive analysis of how each city defines, detects, and penalizes social missteps, covering language patterns, social behavior, table manners, appearance, digital conduct, and conversation topics.

## Core Insight

The fundamental definition of "rough" differs between the two cities:

- **New York Elite:** Rough = failure of **social awareness** — disrespecting others' time, space, and boundaries.
- **London Elite:** Rough = failure of **social calibration** — not knowing one's place, the room's register, or the appropriate level of formality.

## Key Sections

### I. Language Patterns
The source identifies absolute statements, filler words, volume, pace, and pitch as the fastest class/background signals. It provides substitution tables for both cities, e.g., replacing "like" with "I mean" or a pause, and "whatever" with "fair enough."

### II. Social Behavior
Lists behaviors each city finds unforgivable. New York penalizes lateness, phone use during meetings, and name-dropping. London penalizes direct questions about salary or schooling, rudeness to waitstaff, and over-familiarity.

### III. Table Manners
Described as London's "class X-ray machine." Specific rules include cutlery grip, napkin placement, soup scooping direction, and not placing phones on the table.

### IV. Appearance
New York reads fit and intention (logos, creased clothes, overpowering cologne). London reads new money vs. old money (too-new shoes, over-fitted suits, watch worn outside the cuff).

### V. Digital Behavior
A new class signal: voice messages to strangers, leaving messages on read, mass-forwarded greetings, messy video backgrounds, and forwarding unverified information are all penalized.

### VI. Conversation Topics
Both cities agree on taboo topics (salary, age, politics, personal complaints) but differ in reasoning. New York-specific taboos include "I'm so busy" as status and blaming external factors. London-specific taboos include agreeing too eagerly and asking "Are you posh?"

### VII. Hardest-to-Fake Tells
Signals that cannot be quickly trained: knowing when to stop talking (NY), genuine curiosity (NY), knowing which fork without looking (London), delivering devastating opinions gently (London).
