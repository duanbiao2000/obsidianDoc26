# 生硬变自然: Politeness and Softening Strategies for Natural Spoken English

This source is a practical linguistic reference guide that provides a systematic framework for transforming blunt, rigid English expressions into natural, polite, and socially appropriate spoken alternatives. It is directly relevant to [[雅思]] speaking preparation and advanced English communication skills.

## Core Argument

The central claim is that natural spoken English is not achieved by making language more complex, but by systematically shifting the focus away from the speaker's or listener's perceived deficiency and toward the situation or possibility. This creates "room for both sides" (给双方都留台阶).

## Key Strategies

The source identifies four core softening strategies:

1. **Adding Vague Qualifiers:** Using modifiers like *not really*, *not quite*, *not exactly*, *not necessarily* to buffer direct negation.
2. **Shifting to State-Description:** Replacing direct statements of inability or deficiency with descriptions of one's current state (e.g., *I'm running on empty* instead of *I'm tired*).
3. **Using Indirect/Roundabout Phrasing:** Replacing direct refusals with offers, questions, or alternative suggestions (e.g., *Let me see what I can do* instead of *I can't do it*).
4. **Using Rising Intonation/Open-Ended Endings:** Keeping the conversation alive by adding open-ended clauses after negative statements (e.g., *I'm not sure… but let me find out*).
