# Coherence & Cohesion: A Practical Guide for IELTS Writing

This source provides a detailed pedagogical framework for understanding and applying the IELTS writing assessment criteria of **Coherence** and **Cohesion**. It argues that these are fundamentally different constructs—coherence is a thinking problem, cohesion is a language problem—and that most learners mistakenly equate more connectives with higher scores.

## Core Distinction

The source establishes a clear contrast between coherence (logical flow and reasoning quality) and cohesion (linguistic devices that bind sentences together). It emphasizes that examiners evaluate the overall "gluing mechanism," of which connectives are only one part.

## Key Techniques

The source details three primary cohesion mechanisms:

1.  **Lexical Cohesion**: Using vocabulary itself to create semantic links, including lexical chains (repeated appearance of related words), superordinate/hyponym relations, and antonym pairs. This is presented as what examiners truly reward.
2.  **Reference Cohesion**: Using pronouns, demonstratives (this/these/that/those), and substitution to link sentences. The source provides extensive examples of clear vs. ambiguous reference.
3.  **Precise Connective Use**: A detailed table of connectives with their precise semantic functions (e.g., *furthermore* vs. *moreover* vs. *in addition*; *however* vs. *nevertheless* vs. *nonetheless*), along with common error patterns.

## Paragraph Structure

The source presents the **PEEL structure** (Point → Explanation → Evidence → Link) as the most stable framework for paragraph-level coherence. It provides a before/after comparison of a low-score vs. high-score paragraph on free higher education, with structural breakdowns.

## Essay-Level Cohesion

The source demonstrates three methods for paragraph transitions: (1) ending a paragraph with a preview of the next, (2) opening the next paragraph by echoing a key word from the previous one, and (3) using a consistent comparative framework (e.g., individual vs. societal level) throughout the essay. A full essay demonstration on health responsibility is provided with an annotated cohesion mechanism table.

## Key Findings

- Connectives have precise semantic functions; treating them as interchangeable is a systematic error.
- Lexical cohesion (chains, superordinate/hyponym, antonym pairs) is what examiners truly reward.
- PEEL structure provides the most reliable paragraph-level coherence.
- Paragraph transitions should use logical echoing, not "firstly/secondly."
- Reference cohesion (this/these/such) is the safest and most sophisticated linking mechanism.
