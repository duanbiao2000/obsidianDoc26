## Key Entities
- **Herodotus (希罗多德)**: Central figure; Ancient Greek historian, author of *The Histories*. Role: Primary subject of analysis. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Croesus (克洛伊索斯)**: King of Lydia. Role: Central character in the opening narrative arc illustrating the fragility of happiness. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Solon (梭伦)**: Athenian statesman and sage. Role: Advisor to Croesus; voice of wisdom regarding the definition of happiness and the unpredictability of fate. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Cyrus the Great (居鲁士)**: Persian King. Role: Conqueror of Croesus; represents the shift in power and the instrument of divine balance. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Thales (泰鲁斯)**: An ordinary Athenian citizen mentioned by Solon. Role: Example of "true happiness" (prosperity, good children, honorable death). Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Cleobis and Biton (克列奥比斯和比顿)**: Brothers mentioned by Solon. Role: Second example of happiness due to their painless, glorious death. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Megabyzus (美伽比佐斯)**: Persian general. Role: Advocate for monarchy/tyranny in the political debate within *The Histories*. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Amasis (阿玛西斯)**: Egyptian King. Role: Source of the "bow" analogy regarding leadership and moderation. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Darius (大流士)**: Persian King. Role: Conducts the cultural experiment on burial customs. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Spartans (斯巴达人)**: Representing martial discipline and contempt for commerce/deception. Role: Respondents to Persian demands. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **The Histories (《历史》)**: The source text. Role: Primary artifact being analyzed. Status: Not in wiki index.

## Key Concepts
- **Happiness as a Terminal State (幸福作为终点状态)**: The idea that one cannot be called happy until they have died, because fortune can reverse at any moment. Matters as the core philosophical thesis of Book 1. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Divine Envy / Divine Balance (神运/神的嫉妒)**: The concept that gods actively punish human prosperity to maintain cosmic balance, often by granting a "shadow of happiness" before destruction. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **The Wheel of Fortune (命运车轮)**: A metaphor for the cyclical nature of luck and power; no one enjoys good fortune forever. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Custom is King (习俗乃是万民之主)**: The principle that cultural norms are the ultimate authority for individuals, leading to cultural relativism. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Cultural Relativism (文化相对主义)**: Herodotus’s approach of observing foreign customs without inherent judgment, highlighting that what is "normal" is culturally specific. Status: Not in wiki index.
- **Freedom vs. Wealth/Power (自由 > 财富与权力)**: The argument that political freedom generates greater collective strength and effort than mere material wealth or autocratic power. Status: Not in wiki index.
