## Key Concepts

| Concept | Definition | Why It Matters | In Wiki? |
|---------|------------|----------------|----------|
| **Subjunctive Mood** | Verb forms for hypothetical, counterfactual, or wished situations (were, had known, would be) | Highest-priority error area; Chinese lacks morphological mood marking entirely | ❌ |
| **Article System** | Use of a/an/the/zero article for noun reference tracking | Chinese has no article system; learners rely on guessing | ❌ (partial: covered in teaching sources peripherally) |
| **Tense/Aspect System** | Verb inflection to encode time relationships (present perfect vs. past, sequence of tenses, future perfect) | Foundational conflict: Chinese marks time contextually, not morphologically | ❌ |
| **Preposition System** | Fixed collocations for time (at/in/on), location logic, verb+prep and adj+prep pairs | Cannot be translated literally from Chinese; systematic mismatch | ❌ |
| **Subject-Verb Agreement** | Verb number matching subject through intervening phrases, indefinite pronouns, collective nouns, proximity | High error rate in long sentences due to distraction by intervening words | ❌ |
| **Non-finite Verbs** | Gerunds, infinitives, participles as different syntactic roles | Chinese verbs don't change form for syntactic role; foundational mismatch | ❌ |
| **Inversion/Emphasis** | Subject-auxiliary inversion after negative adverbs, only+adverbial, so/such...that | Rarely used by Chinese learners; marks advanced writing when used correctly | ❌ |
| **Comma Splice** | Joining independent clauses with only a comma | Chinese writing convention allows comma-joined independent clauses; direct transfer error | ❌ |
| **Contrastive Analysis Root Cause** | The meta-claim that 90% of Chinese learner grammar errors stem from two sources: (1) features absent in Chinese (tense inflection, articles, case), (2) literal translation of Chinese logic (word order, collocation, clause structure) | Central thesis of the document | ❌ (but aligns with the "底层逻辑" approach in [[口语高分底层逻辑]]) |
