# Source: Journals/Engnotes/雅思场景下的风格选择(写作).md

## Key Entities
- **IELTS (International English Language Testing System)**: The subject of the analysis; specifically its Writing module scoring criteria.
- **"New York Style"**: A metaphorical construct used in the source to represent direct, concise, active, and sharp writing (associated with higher scores if applied correctly, but penalized if it becomes colloquial).
- **"London Style"**: A metaphorical construct representing formal, academic, objective, and nuanced writing (the baseline for IELTS, but prone to pitfalls like verbosity or lack of stance if misapplied).
- **Task 1 & Task 2**: The two specific components of the IELTS Writing test analyzed separately.

## Key Concepts
- **Style Matrix / Spectrum**: The core theoretical framework of the document, positing that high-scoring IELTS writing is not purely "academic" but a hybrid of "London" formality and "New York" directness.
- **Hybrid Style ("London Skeleton + New York Sharpness")**: The recommended optimal approach for Task 2, combining structural rigor with argumentative clarity.
- **Colloquial Leakage**: The primary risk in Task 1 and a major pitfall in Task 2, where spoken-language features (e.g., "kind of," "basically") degrade the score.
- **Empty Verbosity**: A specific failure mode of "London Style" where excessive nominalization, double negatives, and redundant modifiers obscure meaning rather than enhance it.
- **Stance Clarity**: The requirement in Task 2 to take a clear position, contrasting with the "neutral" tendency often associated with overly cautious academic writing.
- **Paraphrasing vs. Repetition**: The distinction between effectively rewriting the prompt (high score) and mechanically repeating it (low score).

## Main Arguments & Findings
- **Writing and Speaking Have Opposite Risks**: While speaking allows for natural pauses and tolerates some informality, writing has zero tolerance for colloquialisms ("New York risks are high" in writing). Conversely, speaking penalizes overly formal language, whereas writing rewards educated formality.
- **Task 1 Requires Pure Objectivity**: The ideal style is "London Academic Variant." It demands zero personal opinion, precise data language, and layered trend descriptions. Examples show that simply listing numbers is low-scoring; interpreting trends (e.g., "warrants closer examination") is high-scoring.
- **Task 2 Requires a Hybrid Approach**:
    - **Introduction**: Paraphrase the topic formally (London), present both sides neutrally (London), but state a clear thesis sharply (New York).
