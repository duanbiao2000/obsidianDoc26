# C1 Proposal or Report Writing Guide

A pedagogical guide from an unnamed video instructor (referred to as "老师" in the source notes) covering the writing of Reports and Proposals for the Cambridge C1 Advanced exam. The guide provides step-by-step templates, planning strategies, and language upgrading techniques.

## Key Concepts

- **[[c1-command-words]]**: Parsing task verbs (evaluate, outline, comment, suggest) to determine required content. Missing any command word loses content marks.
- **[[c1-report-vs-proposal]]**: Reports describe current situations; proposals argue for future action. Temporal orientation is the key differentiator.
- **[[c1-communicative-achievement]]**: Writing for a higher-status reader requires formal tone, distancing, passive voice, and indirect suggestions.
- **[[c1-language-techniques]]**: Deliberate post-writing language upgrading — adding precise adverbs, topic-specific vocabulary, and nominalization to lift the language score.

## Four Grading Dimensions

The guide references the four C1 Advanced scoring criteria:

1. **Content** — Covering all task requirements with specific details
2. **Communicative Achievement** — Appropriate register and tone for the audience
3. **Organization** — Clear paragraph structure with headings and subheadings
4. **Language** — Grammatical accuracy and lexical range including topic-specific vocabulary

## Core Recommendations

- Plan for 10–15 minutes before writing (planning time should equal writing time)
- Use headings and subheadings for reports and proposals
- Apply the "lazy alignment" method: each suggestion directly addresses a previously identified problem
- Perform a dedicated language upgrade pass after drafting
- Avoid direct "you should" commands; use polite indirect structures (e.g., "Should these suggestions be implemented...")

## Example Topics

- Public transport in Seville (report model)
- Preserving an old building (proposal model)
- Online seminar feedback (instructor's model answer)

## Limitations

- The guide focuses exclusively on Reports and Proposals within C1 Advanced; it does not cover Essays, Reviews, or Articles from the same exam.
- Recommendations are based on one instructor's experience rather than empirical validation.
- The source video's title and instructor name are not recorded in the available notes.
