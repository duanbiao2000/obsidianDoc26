## Key Concepts
- **Precision vs. Generality**: The core theoretical framework. It argues that vague language ("people," "society," "affects") signals a lack of critical thought, while specific language demonstrates depth and argumentative strength.
- **The "Big Word" Trap**: A phenomenon where learners substitute simple words with complex but generic ones (e.g., using "facilitates" without specifying *how*), resulting in low information density.
- **Mechanism Clarity**: The requirement to explain *how* and *why* things happen (causal chains) rather than just stating that they do. This moves beyond correlation to causation.
- **Information Density**: The metric for sentence quality. A precise sentence retains its meaning even if isolated; a vague sentence loses all value when removed from context.
- **Subject-Verb-Object Specificity**: A structural technique for rewriting sentences by replacing generic nouns/verbs with concrete entities/actions.
- **Cliché Deconstruction**: Identifying and replacing formulaic IELTS essay openings/closings (e.g., "In today's fast-paced world") with substantive, context-specific statements.

## Main Arguments & Findings
- **Core Claim**: Chinese IELTS candidates often lose points not because of grammar errors, but because their arguments are too vague. Examiners interpret vagueness as a lack of genuine opinion or critical thinking.
