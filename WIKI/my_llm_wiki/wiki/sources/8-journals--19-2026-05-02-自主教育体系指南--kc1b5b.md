# Self-Curriculum Generator: A Framework for Self-Directed Education

This source presents a dialogue between an AI assistant and a user, where the AI infers the user's intellectual profile by answering seven self-assessment questions on their behalf. The core contribution is the proposal of these seven questions as a **curriculum generator** — a framework for mapping one's knowledge gaps, interests, and goals to design a personalized learning path.

## The Seven Questions (The Curriculum Generator)

1. **What domain do you frequently encounter but wish to understand more deeply?** — Identifies recurring knowledge gaps.
2. **What type of knowledge has the greatest impact on your life?** — Prioritizes areas with high leverage.
3. **What practical skills do you want to master?** — Distinguishes actionable skills from passive knowledge.
4. **In what field do you want to be regarded as an authority?** — Defines long-term expertise goals.
5. **What do you find yourself thinking about persistently?** — Surfaces intrinsic curiosity and obsession.
6. **What are your obvious knowledge blind spots? What shortcomings make you uncomfortable?** — Forces honest self-assessment of weaknesses.
7. **What is your grand goal? What themes are stepping stones?** — Connects daily learning to a larger purpose.

## Key Insights

- **Metacognition as a Strength:** The source notes that the user demonstrates strong metacognitive awareness ("knowing what you don't know"), which is a prerequisite for effective self-directed learning.
- **Language-Thought Relationship:** A recurring theme is the connection between quality of expression and clarity of thinking. The source suggests that improving writing and speaking is not merely a technical skill but an external manifestation of cognitive clarity.
- **Structured Skill Acquisition:** The framework is built on the premise that skills like "English argumentative writing" can be systematically trained rather than being innate talents. This aligns with a growth mindset.

## Tensions

- **Self-Directed vs. Externally Generated:** The framework is designed for *self*-directed education, yet the source immediately provides *externally* generated answers (the AI's inferences). This creates a tension between autonomous self-discovery and using an AI as a guide.

## Limitations

- The "evidence" in the source is entirely speculative — the AI's own inferences about the user. No empirical data, case studies, or external validation of the framework's effectiveness is provided.
- The source ends with an open question ("do you want to go deeper?"), leaving the implementation phase unexplored.
