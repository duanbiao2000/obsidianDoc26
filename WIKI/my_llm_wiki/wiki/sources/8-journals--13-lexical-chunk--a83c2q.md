# Lexical Chunk: Terminology and Application in Oral Fluency

This source clarifies the precise terminology for "small, reusable, pragmatic language units" used to improve oral fluency in English. It distinguishes between several related linguistic concepts, identifying **Lexical Chunk** as the most accurate and practical term for learners aiming to sound native-like and reduce cognitive load during speech.

## Key Terminology Hierarchy

The source organizes terms by their fit for describing reusable oral units:

1.  **Lexical Chunk / Chunk**: The core concept. Defined as the smallest, meaningful, multi-word unit (typically 1–4 words) that is memorized and retrieved as a whole. Examples include `make sense`, `in a nutshell`, and `grab a cuppa`. This is the mainstream concept in modern English teaching for rapid fluency improvement.
2.  **Formulaic Sequence / Formulaic Language**: Refers to fixed, pre-fabricated expressions stored and retrieved without online syntactic processing. Examples: `to be honest`, `long story short`. Best for highly routine or idiomatic expressions.
3.  **Pragmatic Chunk**: Focuses on communicative functions such as transitioning, hedging, or emphasizing, rather than just semantic content. Examples: `actually`, `basically`, `not necessarily`.
4.  **Collocation**: Describes habitual co-occurrence of words (e.g., `strong tea`, `pay attention`). Considered too narrow for the broader concept of functional chunks.
5.  **Multi-word Unit**: A broad umbrella term encompassing collocations, phrasal verbs, idioms, and chunks.

## Distinctions

*   **Vs. Grammar**: Chunks are not derived from grammatical analysis but are natural units used by native speakers.
*   **Vs. Morphemes**: Unlike morphemes (the smallest grammatical units like prefixes/suffixes), chunks are multi-word units suitable for immediate oral application.
*   **Vs. Cognitive Chunking**: Distinct from the cognitive study technique of breaking information into smaller units (see [[chunking]]).

## Practical Application

Learning via **Lexical Chunking** involves memorizing and retrieving multi-word units as single items, bypassing word-by-word sentence construction. This reduces cognitive load, increases speech speed, and enhances naturalness. The source emphasizes the value of **Pragmatic Chunks** (discourse markers) for managing social interaction and tone, which is critical for high scores in exams like [[IELTS]].
