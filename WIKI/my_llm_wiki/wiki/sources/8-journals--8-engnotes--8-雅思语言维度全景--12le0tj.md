## Key Concepts
- **Collocation (搭配)**: The habitual juxtaposition of words in a language. The source argues this is more critical than vocabulary size because examiners quickly identify "Chinglish collocations." It matters because it is a primary differentiator between native-like and non-native usage.
- **Precision (精确性)**: The ability to use specific, concrete language rather than vague generalizations. It matters because vagueness is a common扣分点 (deduction point) for Chinese candidates. Principle: "Be specific if possible, quantify if possible."
- **Nominalization (名词化)**: Converting verbs/adjectives into noun structures. It is identified as a hallmark of academic writing, shifting focus from actions to concepts.
- **Syntactic Variety (句式多样性)**: Using varied sentence structures (simple, concessive, conditional, emphatic, inverted, participle phrases) rather than just difficult words. It matters for demonstrating grammatical range.
- **Hedging vs. Asserting (态度标记)**: The strategic use of modal verbs and qualifiers to soften claims (hedging) versus making strong statements (asserting). Essential for academic tone and avoiding over-generalization.
- **Coherence & Cohesion (连贯与衔接)**: Logical flow of ideas. The source distinguishes between mechanical linking words (e.g., "furthermore") and lexical chains (semantic repetition), advocating for the latter for better flow.
- **Register (语域)**: Mentioned briefly as a dimension alongside the others, implying appropriateness of language to context.

## Main Arguments & Findings
- **Core Claim**: IELTS success depends less on obscure vocabulary and more on mastering specific linguistic dimensions: Collocation, Precision, Nominalization, Syntax, Hedging, and Cohesion.
- **Evidence**: The source provides comparative tables showing "Wrong/Vague" vs. "Correct/Precise" examples for each dimension (e.g., *do a crime* vs. *commit a crime*; *There are many problems* vs. *Unemployment and pollution are the most pressing concerns*).
- **Prioritization Strategy**: The source identifies **Collocation** as having the highest ROI (Return on Investment) for practice because errors are most visible and corrections yield quick results.
