# How to Actually Make New Info Stick

This source provides a pedagogical framework for managing [[cognitive-load-management|Cognitive Load]] during intensive learning, specifically tailored for [[IELTS]] speaking preparation. It argues that pushing through mental fatigue ("hitting a wall") is counterproductive and introduces the concept of **"Modeling Mode"** as a metacognitive strategy to integrate new information.

## Core Arguments

### 1. Cognitive Load Management
The text identifies "mental clutter" as a signal to stop cramming. It recommends **shrinking focus** and breaking complex information into **bite-sized chunks** (2-3 concepts at a time) to prevent brain shutdown. This aligns with existing wiki concepts on [[Chunking]] and [[desirable-difficulty]].

### 2. Modeling Mode & Negative Transfer
To avoid the [[illusion-of-competence]], learners should switch to "Modeling Mode" when overwhelmed. This involves visualizing connections (timelines, flowcharts) to test if new info fits existing frameworks. If an analogy feels forced, it indicates **Negative Transfer**, where prior knowledge interferes with new learning. Learners are advised to discard flawed models immediately.

### 3. Lexical Balance in Speaking (80/20 Rule)
For [[IELTS]] Speaking Band 8+, the source proposes an **80/20 rule**:
*   **80% Natural Idioms/Phrasal Verbs:** Focus on fluency and naturalness (e.g., *hit a wall*, *fried*, *fall back on*).
*   **20% Precise Lexical Items:** Use less common, precise terms for scoring points (e.g., *negative transfer*, *mental clutter*, *jargon*).

This supports the wiki's existing content on [[口语高分底層邏輯]] and [[spoken-to-written-style-transformation]], emphasizing that true understanding is demonstrated by simplifying complex ideas rather than using excessive jargon.

## Key Vocabulary Highlights

The source curates specific lexical items for high-level spoken English:

*   **Natural Idioms:** *hit a wall*, *fried*, *here's the catch*, *drowning in information*, *nailed*, *airy-fairy*, *cook up*, *at the end of the day*.
*   **Precise Terms:** *mental clutter*, *backfiring*, *negative transfer*, *shrink your focus*, *bite-sized chunks*, *intimidated*, *jargon*, *ground it*.

These terms are recommended for integration into [[进阶英语词汇库]] and [[IELTS Speaking Lexical Strategy]].

## Methodology

The suggested workflow for internalizing new information:
1.  **Detect Overload:** Recognize signs of "brain fried" or "mental clutter."
2.  **Switch to Modeling Mode:** Stop passive absorption. Create visual maps (flowcharts/mind maps).
3.  **Test Frameworks:** Check if new info clicks with existing knowledge.
4.  **Identify Negative Transfer:** If analogies feel awkward, look for counterexamples. Discard flawed models.
5.  **Chunk Information:** Break down large sets of concepts into small, manageable groups (2-3 items).
6.  **Ground Concepts:** Translate academic jargon into plain English or real-world analogies.
