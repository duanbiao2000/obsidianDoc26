# IELTS Speaking Rephrase Techniques: A Tactical Guide

A tactical guide reframing the act of rephrasing (self-correction) in the IELTS Speaking test from a sign of error to a demonstration of active thinking. The document provides a complete toolkit for displaying a thinking process under exam conditions, targeting Band 8+ performance.

## Core Argument

The single most important reframe for IELTS Speaking candidates: **rephrase should not be used to correct errors, but to externalize a thinking process.** Low-scoring candidates apologize for mistakes; high-scoring candidates display their real-time cognitive engagement.

## Key Techniques

The guide organizes techniques into seven categories, each with example phrases and full dialogue demonstrations:

1. **Direct Replacement** — Neutral phrases like "Let me rephrase that" and "To be more specific"
2. **Upgrade Expressions** — Deepening an idea mid-utterance: "Actually, a better way to put it"
3. **Buying Time** — Natural pauses that sound like reflection: "How do I put this", "Bear with me"
4. **Angle Shift** — Restructuring the entire framing approach: "Let me come at this from a different angle"
5. **Partial Correction** — Revising only part of the statement: "The first part stands, but..."
6. **Contrast Upgrade** — Seamless rephrase via "Not X, but Y" structures
7. **Complete Scenarios** — Full Part 1, Part 2, and Part 3 demonstrations

## Prohibitions

| Action | Reason |
|--------|--------|
| "Sorry, I made a mistake" | Turns a language issue into an apology — double penalty |
| Repeating the error then the correction | Forces the examiner to hear the mistake twice |
| Rephrasing without substantive change | Exposes limited vocabulary |
| Over-rephrasing every sentence | Signals lack of control |
| "How do you say... in English?" | Reveals translation process, not thinking process |

## Three-Scenario Taxonomy

The guide identifies three distinct situations where rephrase is appropriate:

1. **Imprecise word** — The candidate feels the first word choice is not accurate
2. **Unclear expression** — The candidate senses the examiner may not have followed
3. **Idea upgrade** — The candidate formulates a better argument mid-sentence

Each scenario has a different trigger and recommended strategy.
