# PlayPhrase.me — Phrase-Based Video Learning Tool

PlayPhrase.me is a web-based language learning tool that allows users to search for English phrases and instantly retrieve short video clips from movies and television shows where those phrases are spoken naturally. The tool is designed to provide contextual, authentic exposure to spoken English.

## Key Features

- **Phrase search**: Users type any English phrase and receive a paginated list of video clips.
- **Frequency indicator**: Each phrase displays a count of available clips (e.g., "Thank you very much" has 13,179 clips; "Nice to meet you" has 14,683).
- **CEFR level tagging**: Every phrase is tagged with a difficulty grade from A1 (beginner) to C2 (proficient).
- **Filters**: Users can filter by CEFR level, emotion, polarity, and topic. Additional toggles include "Only idioms" and "Only questions."
- **Video playback controls**: Skip forward/backward, theater mode, and navigation through search results.

## Example Content

The source document shows the tool's interface for the root phrase "count." Results include:

- "Can count on one hand." (14 clips) — B2 grade
- "I knew I could count on you." (112 clips) — B1 grade
- "I could be bounded in a nutshell and count myself a king of infinite space." (6 clips) — C2 grade
- "I'm going to count down from." (10 clips) — A2 grade

The default landing page displays common A1 phrases such as "Thank you. Have a good day." (7 clips), "Thank you very much." (13,179 clips), and "Nice to meet you." (14,683 clips).

## Pedagogical Significance

PlayPhrase.me embodies a **search-based, on-demand** paradigm for language learning. Unlike structured courses or push-based content (e.g., YouTube channels), the learner actively searches for specific phrases they want to hear in context. This complements existing wiki resources on [[phrase-based-video-learning]], [[影子跟读法 (Shadowing)]], and [[主题化词汇列表]].

## Limitations

- The tool is a reference resource, not a structured curriculum.
- CEFR grading may show minor inconsistencies (e.g., "Can count on one hand" tagged B2 while "I can count on one hand" tagged B1).
- No explicit user studies or efficacy data are provided on the site.
