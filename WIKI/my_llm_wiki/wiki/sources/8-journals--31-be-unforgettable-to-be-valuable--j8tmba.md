# Be Unforgettable to Be Valuable

This source captures the core thesis and framework from a TEDx talk by [[Yasir Khan]], titled "How to Speak so People Remember You." The central argument is that professional and personal value is contingent on memorability: "It’s not what you say—it’s what they remember." If a message is forgotten, it holds no utility, regardless of its accuracy or volume.

## Core Thesis

The source posits that communication effectiveness is measured by retention, not transmission. Key principles include:
*   **Memorability as Value:** You cannot be valuable if you are not memorable.
*   **Action follows Memory:** People cannot follow or act upon information they have forgotten.
*   **Contrast over Sameness:** The brain ignores routine and sameness, filtering out generic responses (e.g., "I'm fine") while retaining contrast.

## The SHARE Framework

Khan introduces the **[[SHARE Framework]]**, a five-pillar heuristic for structuring memorable speech:

1.  **S - Stand Out:** Break routine conversations. Use contrast to capture attention, as the brain habituates to sameness.
2.  **H - Highlight What Matters:** Distill the message to its core value. "Give the gift, not just the wrapping." Cut extraneous details to ensure the listener knows exactly what to remember.
3.  **A - Address Them:** Personalize the communication. Use "you" and focus on topics relevant to the audience, leveraging the psychological tendency for people to care most about themselves.
4.  **R - Repeatable:** Make the message easy to recall and share. Use stories, metaphors, analogies, and sticky phrases.
5.  **E - Emotion:** Leverage emotional arousal to cement memory. Emotional words (positive or negative) leave a lifelong impact because "people believe what they feel."

## Key Anecdotes

*   **The Trainee:** A trainee forgot 17 tips immediately after leaving the stage, illustrating the fragility of unstructured information.
*   **The Cashier:** A grocery cashier’s comment ("You have a beautiful voice") changed Khan’s career trajectory, demonstrating the power of specific, emotional positive feedback.
*   **The Bully:** A childhood comment about teeth persisted in memory, showing how negative emotional encoding also strengthens retention.

## Implications

The source suggests shifting the goal of communication from "accuracy" or "volume" to "retention." It implies that generic communication is invisible and that emotional resonance drives belief and action. This framework can be applied to professional presentations, everyday social interactions, and potentially adapted for [[Prompting / ChatGPT]] to generate more engaging AI outputs.
