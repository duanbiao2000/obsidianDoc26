# 承认不确定：高级别考生的秘密武器 (Acknowledging Uncertainty: The Secret Weapon of High-Scoring Candidates)

This source argues that the key to high IELTS speaking scores is **acknowledging uncertainty** — using hedging language, buffer phrases, and colloquial markers — rather than projecting false confidence or memorizing "advanced vocabulary." It uses Greg Brockman's TED talk as a case study to illustrate how native speakers naturally soften their claims in spontaneous speech.

## Core Argument

The document's central thesis is that Band 7+ speaking requires softening claims rather than asserting them absolutely. It presents the counterintuitive insight that "not knowing" is a strength, not a weakness, in speaking assessment. The author argues that authenticity — including visible hesitation and uncertainty — is more valuable than polished, memorized responses.

## Key Strategies

1. **Hedging Language**: Using words like `feel like`, `tend to`, `generally speaking`, `to some extent`, `it depends`, and `or something like that` to soften assertions.
2. **Buffer Phrases**: Time-buying phrases like "Let me think about that for a second" to avoid silence when faced with difficult questions.
3. **The `It's not just X, it's Y` Structure**: A comparative/progressive sentence pattern that naturally provides contrast and depth.
4. **Phrasal Verbs as Authenticity Markers**: Using multi-word verbs (e.g., `push back on`, `come up with`) instead of formal single-word equivalents to signal genuine fluency.

## Tensions

The document contains an internal tension: it argues that authenticity is the most valuable quality, yet the entire document is a highly structured, strategic guide. This reflects a known paradox in language testing literature — the tension between "being authentic" and "strategically performing authenticity."
