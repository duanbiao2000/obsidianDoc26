## Main Arguments & Findings

### Core Claims

1. **Register adaptability is the hidden, decisive differentiator between Band 7 and Band 9** — not vocabulary sophistication or grammatical complexity alone.

2. **Band 7 = "one register for everything"** (typically either too casual or too formal throughout). Band 9 = "natural switching" based on context.

3. **Register switching operates across three simultaneous dimensions**: vocabulary, sentence structure, and personal distance (I/my → society/one).

4. **Writing tasks also require register differentiation**: Task 1 demands neutral, data-driven language (no subjective judgments); Task 2 demands academic argumentation (no casual markers).

5. **Three common failure scenarios**: Part 3 sliding into Part 1 casualness; Task 2 using Task 1's neutral tone (no stance); Part 1 sounding overly academic ("can't speak like a normal person").

### Evidence Strength

- **Strong**: The source provides extensive, concrete examples across 5 topics (Technology, Education, Environment, Health, Culture) with explicit before/after comparisons.
- **Strong**: The "four signal words" framework (trigger → switch) is actionable and testable.
- **Moderate**: The claim that register switching is "the most critical differentiator" is asserted but not empirically validated against other factors (e.g., coherence, lexical resource).
- **Strong**: The "communication purpose" mechanism provides a principled explanation for *why* register matters, not just *what* to do.
