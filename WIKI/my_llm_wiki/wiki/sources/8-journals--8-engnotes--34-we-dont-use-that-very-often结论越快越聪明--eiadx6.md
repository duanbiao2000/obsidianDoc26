# We don't use that very often: 结论越快越聪明

A pedagogical reference document that systematically identifies common Chinglish (中式英语) patterns used by Chinese ESL learners and provides native English replacements, organized by communication domain. The document goes beyond surface-level vocabulary errors to diagnose the underlying cultural-psychological traps that produce systematic pragmatic failures.

## Core Thesis

Many common English expressions used by Chinese learners are not isolated mistakes but **systematic errors rooted in L1 transfer** — Chinese grammatical structure, vocabulary choice, and cultural logic producing unnatural or non-native output. The correct replacements follow identifiable rules: drop redundant prepositions, replace "very + adjective" with strong single words, use implicit conversational connectors instead of overt logical ones, and avoid literal translations of Chinese politeness formulas.

## Key Content

### Verb Corrections
Common preposition errors (discuss about → discuss, emphasize on → emphasize, approach to → approach) and redundant constructions (return back → return, repeat again → repeat).

### Domain-Specific Replacement Tables
- Expressing opinions and viewpoints
- Time expressions
- Degree/emphasis (very tired → exhausted, very happy → thrilled)
- Workplace/professional language
- Social/daily conversation
- Describing people
- Quantity/comparison
- Transition/logical connectors
- Common Chinese sentence patterns

### Seven Cultural Traps (文化陷阱)

The document identifies seven cultural-psychological patterns that produce systematic communication errors:

| Trap | Chinese Logic | NYC Logic |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| Modesty (谦虚) | Self-deprecation | Confident self-presentation |
| Face-saving (面子) | Indirect refusal | Direct rejection |
| Collectivism (集体主义) | "We" to diffuse responsibility | "I" to own positions |
| Authority worship (权威崇拜) | Deference to superiors | Value-driven pushback |
| Over-explanation (过度解释) | Background before conclusion | Conclusion first |
| Over-apology/thanks (感谢/道歉过度) | Excessive humility | Brief, sincere acknowledgment |
| Relationship-orientation (关系导向) | Build relationship before business | Value creation before relationship |

### Additional Traps
- Numbers/achievement expression (big numbers vs. specific impact)
- Emotional control (suppression vs. appropriate expression)
- Academic/written register in spoken English

## Deep Pattern Summary

| Chinese Cultural Logic | NYC Elite Logic |
|------------------------|-----------------|
| Modesty is virtue | Confidence is competence |
| Vague to save face | Direct to save time |
| Collective diffuses responsibility | Individual owns outcomes |
| Relationship before business | Value before relationship |
| Hidden emotion = professional | Appropriate authenticity = trustworthy |
| More preamble = more thoughtful | Faster conclusion = smarter |

## Pedagogical Value

The document provides concrete, actionable substitution tables that directly support the principles outlined in [[口语高分底层逻辑]] — specifically the need for "conversational feel" (对话感) and "navigational logic" (导航式逻辑) in spoken English. It extends the [[进阶英语词汇库]] by adding a cultural-pragmatic competence dimension beyond thematic word lists.

## Limitations

- The "纽约精英" (NYC elite) reference group is narrow and informal; many replacements are casual and inappropriate for formal writing or conservative workplaces
- The document implicitly positions NYC communication as universally superior without acknowledging contexts where indirectness or relationship-building is appropriate
- Register boundaries are not marked — replacements span from casual (beats me, not gonna lie) to professional (critically important, highly capable) without distinction
- Anecdotal rather than corpus-based evidence
