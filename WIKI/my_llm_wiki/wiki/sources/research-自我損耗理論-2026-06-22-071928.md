## Key Entities
- **Roy Baumeister**: Central figure; social psychologist who formalized the Ego Depletion Theory in the late 1990s.
- **Michael Inzlicht & Brandon Schmeichel**: Peripheral figures; proponents of the alternative "Process Model" which challenges the resource-based view.
- **Hagger et al. (2016) & Vohs et al. (2021)**: Peripheral entities; cited as key studies in the replication controversy, reporting null findings.

## Key Concepts
- **Ego Depletion (自我損耗)**: The core concept; a state of reduced effectiveness in voluntary actions following the consumption of finite psychological resources through self-control or decision-making.
- **Strength Model**: The theoretical framework positing that self-regulation relies on a shared, limited resource analogous to muscle fatigue.
- **Conservation Hypothesis**: A refinement suggesting the brain downshifts exertion to preserve energy for high-priority demands, rather than running out completely.
- **Process Model**: An alternative theory arguing depletion is a strategic shift in attention and motivation toward immediate gratification, not energy loss.
- **Decision Fatigue**: A specific manifestation of ego depletion where prolonged choice-making leads to status-quo bias and passive acceptance of defaults.
- **Blood Glucose Metabolism**: Initially proposed physiological correlate; now viewed as contributory but insufficient to explain all effects.
- **Mindset (Implicit Beliefs)**: Moderating factor; belief in unlimited willpower can attenuate depletion effects.
- **WEIRD Sample Bias**: Critique noting most data comes from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic populations.

## Main Arguments & Findings
- **Core Claim**: Self-control draws from a finite pool of executive resources, leading to temporary impairment in subsequent unrelated tasks (Cross-domain transferability).
    - *Evidence*: Synthesized findings from stress tolerance, emotion suppression, and impulse control studies [1][4][6].
    - *Strength*: Historically influential but currently contested due to replication failures.
- **Moderation by Motivation**: High incentives or salient goals can temporarily restore performance by tapping into conserved reserves.
    - *Evidence*: Studies showing restored performance under strong external motivators [7][8][10].
    - *Strength*: Moderate; effect is temporary and does not refute the underlying conservation mechanism.
- **Replication Crisis**: Large-scale multi-lab replications have failed to reproduce original effect sizes, attributing earlier positives to publication bias.
