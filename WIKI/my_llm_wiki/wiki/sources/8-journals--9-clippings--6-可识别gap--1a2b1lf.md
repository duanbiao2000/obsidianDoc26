# Identifiable Gaps Between IELTS Speaking Band 7.5 and 8.0

This source analyzes an IELTS Speaking sample answer that scores approximately 7.0–7.5, identifying three specific gaps that prevent it from reaching Band 8.0: **depth of thought**, **structural variety**, and **oral naturalness**.

## Core Diagnostic

The central insight: the answer demonstrates knowledge of vocabulary and structures, but does not fully show the examiner that the candidate is *thinking with them*. The gaps cluster around conclusions and transitions rather than lexical range.

## Gap 1 — Depth of Thought (Part 1)

The response ends at the feeling level (e.g., "it stays with you") without explaining *why* the experience lingers. The upgrade pushes to an analytical layer:

> "It stays with you — not because of anything you were taught, but because it's one of the rare times you were asked to think rather than perform."

## Gap 2 — Structural Variety (Part 1, second question)

The filler "It's funny, actually" is replaced with the more sophisticated contrast opener:

> "The irony is that enrichment is universally valued in principle and systematically defunded in practice."

This shift from a conversational placeholder to a rhetorical structure signals conscious control of discourse.

## Gap 3 — Oral Naturalness & Derived Insight (Part 2)

The climactic sentence — "not all valuable learning can be measured — and that is precisely the problem with reducing education to test scores" — arrives abruptly, feeling pasted on rather than derived from the narrative. The solution adds a transitional sentence that lets the insight emerge organically:

> "And I think that's when it crystallised for me — that the most formative experiences resist measurement almost by definition. Which is precisely why systems built around test scores are structurally incapable of valuing them."

A secondary issue: overuse of "thinking aloud" hesitation (e.g., "well, on one level") dilutes the impact of genuine reflective pauses.

## Gap 4 — Clichéd Phrasing & Tidy Conclusion (Part 3)

- "absent from the equation" is replaced with a more specific, less formulaic expression: "yet they leave almost no trace in how curricula are designed."
- The final recommendation ("we might start to see a system that actually nurtures learners rather than just sorting them") is replaced with an [[open-tension]] ending that signals boundary awareness:

> "Though whether institutions designed to sort people can ever be genuinely reformed to nurture them — that's a question the evidence hasn't answered convincingly."
