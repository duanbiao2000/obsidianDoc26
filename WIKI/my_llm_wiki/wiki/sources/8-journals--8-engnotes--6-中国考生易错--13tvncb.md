# 中国考生易错: Mapping Social Rudeness to IELTS Speaking Assessment

This source provides an original analysis of common errors made by Chinese candidates in the IELTS Speaking test. It reframes language errors as **social faux pas** — behaviors that make the examiner feel uncomfortable having an extended conversation with the candidate. The core thesis is that the primary driver of low scores is not grammatical accuracy but a **"performance vibe"** (表演感), where the candidate appears to be executing a test procedure rather than having a genuine conversation.

## Key Framework

The document argues that the IELTS Speaking examiner's core question is **social**, not linguistic: *"Would I be comfortable having an extended conversation with this person in a professional setting?"* This reframes the entire assessment. Errors are categorized not by their linguistic type (e.g., grammar, vocabulary) but by their **social impact** — how they make the examiner feel.

## Error-to-Dimension Mapping

The document provides a detailed mapping of specific candidate behaviors to the four IELTS scoring criteria (Fluency, Lexical Resource, Coherence, Nuance, Intelligibility) and to the overall impression:

| Behavior | Impacted Dimension | Social Interpretation |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| "I am fine, thank you, and you?" | Fluency + Overall Impression | Mechanical, no personality |
| "Very very very" | Lexical Resource | Vocabulary poverty |
| "First, Second, Third" | Coherence | No logical connection between points |
| Repeating the examiner's question | Fluency + Overall Impression | Avoidance strategy |
| Abandoning position when pushed | Nuance + Overall Impression | Cannot hold a position |
| "Thank you" at the end | Overall Impression | Not required |
| "Both sides have their points" | Nuance | Avoids commitment |
| Written language in spoken context | Range | Not natural |
| Rising intonation on every statement | Intelligibility | Lacks confidence |
| "Sorry sorry sorry" | Overall Impression | Signals lack of confidence |

## Top 10 Most Frequent Errors

The document identifies the ten highest-frequency errors in the Chinese candidate population:

1. "I am fine thank you and you?" — Fluency + Overall Impression
2. "Very very very" stacking — Lexical Resource
3. "First second third" mechanical listing — Coherence
4. Repeating the examiner's question to buy time — Fluency + Overall Impression
5. Abandoning position when pushed — Nuance + Overall Impression
6. Saying "Thank you" at the end — Overall Impression
7. "Both sides have their points" — Nuance
8. Written language in spoken context — Range
9. Rising intonation on every statement — Intelligibility
10. "Sorry sorry sorry" — Overall Impression

## Core Thesis

The document's central claim is that **Chinese candidates' primary error is not grammar but "performance vibe"** (表演感). The candidate feels like they are executing a test procedure, not having a conversation. The document argues that **eliminating this performance vibe is more effective than memorizing any template**.

## Limitations

The document is **prescriptive** (it tells candidates what to do) but **not descriptive** (it does not prove that these behaviors are actually the most common or most damaging). The "Top 10" list is the author's judgment, not a data-driven finding. The document does not cite official IELTS scoring rubrics, published research, or empirical data.
