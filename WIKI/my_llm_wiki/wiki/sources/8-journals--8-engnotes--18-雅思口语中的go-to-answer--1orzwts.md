## Key Concepts
- **Go-to Answer**: A pre-prepared, habitual response structure or angle that a candidate can instantly recall when facing a specific category of questions. It is not a rote memorized script but a flexible framework ("angle + structure").
    - *Why it matters*: It reduces cognitive load during the exam, ensuring fluency and coherence by providing an immediate starting point.
    - *Wiki Status*: New concept. Relates to [[沟通技巧]] (Communication Skills) and [[批判性思维]] (Critical Thinking) but is distinct as a test-specific tactical heuristic.
- **Template vs. Framework**: The source distinguishes between "dead memorization" (bad) and "fixed angle + structure" (good).
    - *Why it matters*: Ensures the answer remains relevant to the specific question while maintaining structural integrity.
    - *Wiki Status*: New nuance within language learning strategies.
- **Universal Angles (The "Cheat Angles")**: Specific thematic pivots that apply to many questions, such as "Technology/Social Media," "Pace of Life," and "Values."
    - *Why it matters*: Allows candidates to adapt to diverse prompts using a limited set of high-yield arguments.
    - *Wiki Status*: New concept.

## Main Arguments & Findings
- **Core Claim**: Success in IELTS Speaking Part 3 can be significantly enhanced by preparing "go-to answers"—pre-structured mental frameworks for common question types rather than memorizing specific content.
- **Evidence/Support**: The source provides five specific question patterns and corresponding universal angles:
    1.  **"How has X changed?"**: Pivot to Technology/Social Media, Pace of Life, or Values.
    2.  **"Why is X important?"**: Use a three-layer structure: Individual -> Society -> Future.
    3.  **"Advantages/Disadvantages"**: Contrast Efficiency/Opportunity with Health/Psychological/Social risks.
    4.  **"Should X be...?"**: State opinion -> Pros -> Cons -> Balanced conclusion (regulation/moderation).
    5.  **"What will X be like in the future?"**: Pivot to Advanced Tech, Eco-friendliness, or Personalization.
- **Strength of Evidence**: High practical utility for the target audience (IELTS candidates). It is a heuristic based on observed examiner expectations and common topic overlaps.
