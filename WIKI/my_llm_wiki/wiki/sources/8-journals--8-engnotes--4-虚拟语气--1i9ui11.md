# Subjunctive Mood: Core Logic and T-Voicing Notes

This source is a pedagogical grammar reference covering the English subjunctive mood and American English T-voicing (flapping). It presents a unified "tense backshift" framework for understanding the subjunctive across three categories: If-type conditionals, Wish-type expressions, and Should-type mandative subjunctives. The document also includes a separate section on American English T-voicing, organized by phonetic environment.

## Key Concepts

- **Subjunctive Mood (虚拟语气)**: A grammatical mood expressing hypothetical, wished, or commanded situations.
- **Tense Backshift (时态后移一格)**: The core principle that the more unreal a situation, the further back the tense moves.
- **If-type Conditional (If型条件虚拟)**: Hypothetical conditions contrary to reality, with present, past, and mixed subtypes.
- **Wish-type (Wish型愿望虚拟)**: Expressing regret or dissatisfaction about present, past, or future situations.
- **Should-type (Should型虚拟命令)**: Mandative subjunctive after verbs of suggestion, demand, or command.
- **T-voicing / Flapping (T浊化/闪音化)**: American English phonological rule where /t/ becomes [ɾ] between vowels.

## Key Claims

- The subjunctive mood is fundamentally "tense backshift by one step" — a unified cognitive rule rather than memorization of formulas.
- Should-type uses the base form (or "should + base form"), representing the "lightest" backshift.
- T-voicing occurs when /t/ is between a stressed vowel and an unstressed vowel in American English.

## Limitations

- Examples are invented teaching materials, not empirical findings.
- The framework is conventional and widely taught; no novel claims are made.
