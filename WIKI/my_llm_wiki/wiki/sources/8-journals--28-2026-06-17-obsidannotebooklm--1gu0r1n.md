# Second Brain with NotebookLM + Obsidian: Workflow and Methodology

This source is a dialogue-based discussion of a YouTube video (as7rV87pwdw) that presents a methodology for building a "second brain" by combining [[NotebookLM]] (Google's AI research tool) with [[Obsidian]] (a knowledge management app). The core argument is that traditional note-taking apps are "passive storage" — they hold information but require you to remember to search. A true second brain must actively surface relevant information when needed.

## Key Arguments

1. **The core problem is retrieval, not capture.** Traditional note apps are passive storage. A second brain must actively surface relevant information.
2. **NotebookLM + Obsidian form a complementary system.** NotebookLM processes raw information into insight (thinking); Obsidian stores and links insights permanently (remembering); feeding Obsidian back into NotebookLM enables meta-thinking (pattern discovery across your entire knowledge base).
3. **The [[PARA Method]] structure scales because it sorts by meaning, not by date or source.** Four folders (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) take ~5 minutes to set up and remain manageable at thousands of notes.
4. **Rewriting AI output in your own words is essential.** Copy-pasted AI text becomes "something you skim and forget"; self-written notes remain understandable and usable months later.
5. **The system's value is delayed but compounds.** Week 1 feels like extra work; month 1 shows emergent connections; month 3 becomes a searchable knowledge base.

## Workflow: The NotebookLM → Obsidian Pipeline

1. Upload sources related to a project into a focused NotebookLM notebook.
2. Ask strategic questions (disagreements, contradictions, evidence strength) rather than "summarize this."
3. Rewrite valuable insights in your own words as concise notes in Obsidian.
4. Use insight-titled notes (e.g., "Retention drops when onboarding exceeds three steps") rather than source-based titles.
5. Link new notes to relevant Projects, Areas, and Resources using [[wikilinks]].

## The Hidden Trick: Obsidian → NotebookLM Feedback Loop

Export Obsidian notes as markdown, upload them as sources into a new NotebookLM notebook, and ask meta-questions about patterns across your entire knowledge base. The source describes how this technique uncovered an unconnected pattern between two AI trends that became the thesis of a presentation.

## Delayed Value Curve

- **1 week (10-20 notes):** Feels like extra work; connections are sparse.
- **1 month (50-100 notes):** System starts working for you; unexpected connections emerge.
- **3 months:** Vault becomes a searchable knowledge base reflecting how you think.

## NotebookLM Free Tier Limits

- 50 sources per notebook
- 50 daily chats
- 3 audio overviews per notebook
