# IELTS Speaking vs Writing: 100 Substitution Pairs and Lexical Resource Strategies

This source is a comprehensive IELTS preparation resource focused on improving the **Lexical Resource** scoring criterion. Its central thesis is that **register appropriateness** (using spoken vocabulary in Speaking, written vocabulary in Writing) and **collocation accuracy** are more important for a high score than vocabulary size or rare words.

## Structure and Content

The document is organized into several sections:

1. **100 Spoken → Written Substitution Pairs** — A table of 100 direct word-for-word replacements (e.g., "get" → "obtain", "thing" → "factor/aspect/element"), covering verbs, adjectives, adverbs, conjunctions, and nouns. This is the most basic section.

2. **Advanced Substitution Categories** — Five targeted upgrade lists:
   - Phrasal Verb → Formal Verb (15 pairs, e.g., "go up" → "surge/escalate")
   - Weak → Precise Words (15 pairs, e.g., "say" → "assert/contend/argue")
   - Generic → Specific Adjectives (15 pairs, e.g., "very big" → "enormous/immense/substantial")
   - Vague → Precise Nouns (15 pairs, e.g., "problem" → "dilemma/predicament/constraint")
   - Discourse Marker Upgrades (15 pairs, e.g., "but" → "however/nonetheless/conversely")

3. **Topic-Specific Vocabulary** — Targeted word banks for Environment, Education, Technology, and Society topics.

4. **Idioms for Speaking** — A curated list of idioms for Band 7+ Speaking, organized by function (expressing opinions, describing trends, daily life).

5. **Collocations for Writing** — Extensive lists of verb+noun, adjective+noun, and adverb+adjective collocations, with common error examples (e.g., "~~make a conclusion~~" → "draw a conclusion").

6. **Writing Argumentation Templates** — Fixed expressions for introducing arguments, giving examples, conceding, and concluding.

7. **Vlog Phrase List** — A context-specific list of phrases from a vlogger's IELTS experience, covering exam preparation, test-day process, results, and self-evaluation.

## Key Arguments

- **Register is the primary scoring lever:** Using spoken words (e.g., "I think", "thing", "a lot") in Writing Task 2 directly harms the Lexical Resource score. The document provides the written alternatives.
- **Collocation errors are more damaging than simple vocabulary:** Incorrect pairings (e.g., "do a role") lose more marks than using a basic word correctly.
- **De-subjectification is critical for Writing:** Replace personal opinion markers ("I think", "in my opinion") with impersonal academic constructions ("it is argued that", "evidence suggests").
- **Idioms must be natural and sparse:** For Speaking Band 7+, use 1-2 idioms per answer naturally; forced or memorized delivery is counterproductive.
- **Precision over rarity:** The scoring key is precision and collocation naturalness, not vocabulary size.

## Usage Notes

The document contains an internal tension: it provides 100 "Spoken → Written" substitutions but advises that in Speaking, natural fluency comes first and written words should not be forced. The lists are best used as a "danger awareness" tool for Writing, not a Speaking upgrade guide.
