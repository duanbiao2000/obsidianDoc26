## Key Concepts
- **Disaster Recovery (DR)**: Structured framework to minimize downtime and data loss during catastrophic events.
- **Failover**: The process of switching to a redundant system when the primary fails.
    - *Planned vs. Unplanned*: Distinction between scheduled maintenance and unexpected outages.
- **Synchronous vs. Asynchronous Replication**: Technical methods for data copying; synchronous ensures zero loss but requires low latency, while asynchronous allows lag for distance/throughput.
- **Active-Passive / Active-Standby**: DR architecture where one site is active and another is ready to take over.
- **Hot/Warm/Cold Standby**: Tiers of standby readiness based on cost and speed of activation.
- **Infrastructure as Code (IaC)**: Method for automating infrastructure provisioning to reduce human error during recovery.
- **Immutable Backups**: Backup copies that cannot be altered, crucial for protection against ransomware.
- **Split-Brain Scenario**: A failure mode where two nodes believe they are the primary, leading to data corruption; mitigated by strict failover sequences.
- **Business Continuity Planning (BCP)**: Broader framework that includes DR, focusing on overall business operations.

## Main Arguments & Findings
- **DR is Critical for Survival**: Resilient recovery capabilities directly correlate with organizational survival, especially given rising ransomware threats [1, 7].
- **RTO/RPO Define Strategy**: The choice of DR strategy (e.g., Hot Site vs. Cold Site) is dictated by the organization's specific RTO and RPO requirements [2, 3].
- **Automation is Essential**: Manual execution increases error rates and delays; modern DR relies on IaC and automated orchestration [1, 3].
