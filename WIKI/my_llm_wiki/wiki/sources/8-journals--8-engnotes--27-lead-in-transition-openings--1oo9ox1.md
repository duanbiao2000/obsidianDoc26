# Lead-in Transition Openings: Prompt Templates for Extracting Transition Sentences

This source provides a taxonomy of English prompt templates designed to extract **lead-in / transition openings** (sentences and phrases that signal movement from one idea to the next) from any text or transcript. It synthesizes classification approaches from established writing and presentation resources including Grammarly, VirtualSpeech, Scribbr, Harvard Writing Center, Cambridge, and the University of Wisconsin-Madison Writing Center.

## Core Taxonomy

The document distinguishes between two primary use cases:

1. **Speech / Presentation Transitions** — verbal cues used in spoken contexts (e.g., "Now let's...", "First, I will...", "To get started...")
2. **Written Text Transitions** — linguistic bridges within and between paragraphs in written articles or essays

## Transition Functions

The source identifies five functional categories of transitions:

- **Addition** — introducing additional supporting points
- **Contrast** — signaling a shift or opposing viewpoint
- **Cause–Effect** — showing causal relationships
- **Sequence** — ordering ideas chronologically or logically
- **Example** — introducing illustrative cases

## Prompt Template Categories

### Direct Extraction Prompts
Templates that ask the AI to identify and list lead-in sentences verbatim, such as:
- "Extract the opening sentences that lead into the next part of the video/article."
- "Identify the sentences that introduce the next section or main point."

### Speech-Specific Prompts
Templates tailored for transcripts and spoken content:
- "From the transcript, find sentences the speaker uses to move from the introduction to the first main point."
- "List phrases like 'Now let's…', 'First, I will…', 'To get started…' that signal the beginning of a new section."

### Written Text Prompts
Templates for paragraph-level and within-paragraph transitions:
- "Extract transition sentences within and between paragraphs that link the previous idea to the next one."
- "Identify all transitional words and phrases (such as 'however', 'in addition', 'therefore', 'for example') and give the full sentences they appear in."

### Pattern-Building Prompts
Templates designed to build a reusable transition phrase library:
- "For each transition sentence, also output the core transition phrase (e.g. 'however', 'as a result', 'in addition') so I can build a pattern list."
- "Group the extracted transitions by function: addition, contrast, cause–effect, sequence, example."

## Significance

This document is a **methodology/instruction** resource — it provides prompt engineering templates for extracting linguistic features from source materials. It does not contain primary content but rather describes how to process content. Its value lies in enabling systematic extraction of transition patterns from any text or transcript, supporting language learning and writing skill development.
