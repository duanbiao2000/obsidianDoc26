# How to go from B1 to C2 without the pain!

A video from the [[Keith Speaking Academy]] (English Speaking Success) YouTube channel presenting a complete roadmap from B1 to C2 using the [[Speaking Success System]]: discover language, practice it, and review it. The core claim is that progress requires studying smarter with a level-appropriate system, not studying harder.

## Summary

The video walks through what to focus on and what to avoid at each CEFR level transition (B1→B2, B2→C1, C1→C2), applying the same three-step system differently at each stage. It emphasizes mindset shifts, activation of existing knowledge, and the progression from clear English to natural English to near-native effortless clarity.

## Key Concepts Introduced

- [[Speaking Success System]] — The discover-practice-review framework applied differently at each level
- [[High-frequency chunks]] — Multi-word units treated as single sound units for fluency
- [[Fossilised errors]] — Long-standing mistakes that must be actively corrected at B2→C1
- [[Collocations for natural English]] — Native-like word combinations
- [[Effortless clarity]] — The C2 goal of near-native automaticity and appropriateness
- [[Style-shift drills]] — Practicing the same content in different registers
- [[Word families]] — Noun, verb, adjective, adverb forms for flexible expression
- [[Repair strategies]] — Techniques to rephrase, clarify, and restart after communication breakdowns

## Level-by-Level Breakdown

### B1 → B2: Activation and Fluency
- **Challenge**: Can understand a lot but cannot speak fluently; feels stuck
- **Goal**: Activate existing vocabulary and grammar; build spoken fluency
- **Focus**: High-frequency chunks as sound units; thinking in English; speaking more, not learning more
- **Key mindset**: "Speak more, not learn more"
- **Don't**: Over-correct mid-sentence (kills fluency)

### B2 → C1: Naturalness and Flexibility (The Plateau)
- **Challenge**: Sounds okay but cannot communicate naturally with native speakers; progress feels flat
- **Goal**: Move from "student of English" to proficient/native-like speaker
- **Focus**: Collocations, lexical and grammatical patterns, discourse control, authentic native input
- **Key mindset**: Shift from "it's good enough" to wanting to communicate naturally
- **Don't**: Memorize isolated word lists without context
- **Tool**: [[ozdic.com]] collocation dictionary

### C1 → C2: Effortless Clarity
- **Challenge**: Small but noticeable mistakes in nuance, style, cultural references
- **Goal**: Near-native automaticity and appropriateness across contexts
- **Focus**: Fine-tuning lexical choice, rhetoric, repair strategies, word families, style-shift drills
- **Key mindset**: Language as a tool for deeper connection and persuasion, not just communication
- **Don't**: Over-focus on slang or accent eradication; aim for clarity

## Methodology

The framework is based on the instructor's teaching experience and anecdotal examples. No empirical data or controlled studies are cited. The pedagogical approach is coherent but not scientifically validated.
