# How to Get Fluent

A personal opinion piece on language learning, arguing that vocabulary acquisition through mass listening and reading (comprehensible input) is the primary driver of fluency, far more important than grammar study or exam techniques. The author defines fluency as CEFR B2 level — the ability to converse on diverse topics despite making errors.

## Key Claims

- **Vocabulary over grammar** — Lexical knowledge is the biggest factor in fluency; grammar is secondary.
- **Mass input** — Extensive listening and reading naturally build passive then active vocabulary.
- **Interleaving beats block learning** — Mixing topics during study improves retention over rote memorization of word lists.
- **Fluency = B2** — The author's personal threshold for "fluent" is CEFR B2.
- **Accent irrelevant** — IELTS does not evaluate accent; natural phrasing and word choice matter more.
- **Age not a barrier** — Motivation, not age, determines success.
- **IELTS success depends on general English ability** — Vocabulary size and reading speed outweigh exam technique.
- **Self-directed learning** — Learners must take initiative and seek content of personal interest.

## Evidence Strength

**Low.** The source is entirely anecdotal — no empirical studies, data, or citations are provided. Claims are plausible but unsupported.
