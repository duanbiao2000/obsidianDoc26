# St. Scholastica Day Riot

The **St. Scholastica Day Riot** was a three-day uprising in Oxford that began on **10 February 1355** (the feast day of St. Scholastica). It is the most famous and violent episode in the [[Town and Gown]] conflict.

## The Incident

- **Trigger**: Two students at the Swindlestock Tavern on Carfax complained about the quality of the wine. The argument escalated into a brawl.
- **Spread**: Violence quickly spread through the town. Locals armed with bows and arrows attacked students and scholars.
- **Death toll**: 93 people were killed — 63 students and 30 townspeople.
- **Aftermath**: The university gained greater legal privileges and the town was fined; the riot deepened the divide between town and gown.

## Historical Context

The riot was a flashpoint in a long-standing structural tension. It occurred roughly 150 years after an earlier lynching of students had prompted scholars to flee to Cambridge, leading to the founding of the [[University of Cambridge]].
