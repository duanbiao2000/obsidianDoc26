# Oxford Castle

**Oxford Castle** is a Norman medieval castle built on the orders of **William the Conqueror** in **1071**. It was one of the largest motte-and-bailey castles in England at the time.

## Historical Roles

- **Military fortress**: Played a role in the Siege of Oxford (1142), the First Barons' War, and the English Civil War (1642–1646) where it served as Royalist headquarters.
- **Prison**: After the Civil War, the castle was used as a prison (Oxford Prison), which remained operational until **1996**. It has since been redeveloped as a hotel.
- **Debtors' Tower**: 18th-century prison tower for debtors.
- **St. George's Tower**: The oldest surviving part of the castle complex; one of the oldest pieces of military architecture in England.

## Notable Events

- Captured by Parliamentarian forces in 1646 after a two-month siege, ending Oxford's role as the Royalist capital.
- The prison's closure in 1996 ended nearly 900 years of penal use.
