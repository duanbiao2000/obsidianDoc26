# John Donne

John Donne (1572–1631) was an English poet and cleric in the Church of England. He is widely recognized as the pre-eminent representative of the metaphysical poets.

## Influence on Literature

Donne is best known in the context of modern literature for his *Devotions upon Emergent Occasions* (1624), specifically Meditation XVII, which contains the famous passage:

> "No man is an island, entire of itself; every man is a piece of the continent... therefore never send to know for whom the bell tolls; it tolls for thee."

This passage inspired the title of [[Ernest Hemingway]]'s novel *For Whom the Bell Tolls*. Hemingway adopted Donne's metaphor of human interconnectedness to frame the novel's central theme: that individual suffering and death are shared by all of humanity.
