# Bodleian Library

The **Bodleian Library** is the main research library of the [[University of Oxford]] and one of the oldest working libraries in Europe. It was re-founded by **Sir Thomas Bodley** (a former fellow of Merton College) and opened in **1602**.

## Key Facts

- **Holds over 13 million printed items**, making it the second largest library in the UK after the British Library.
- **Non-lending library**: Books may only be read on the premises. This rule famously caused it to refuse a book request from **King Charles I** in 1645.
- **Partially housed in the [[Radcliffe Camera]]**, a landmark circular reading room built in the 1740s and absorbed into the Bodleian in 1927.
- **Also incorporates the [[Clarendon Building]]** (opened 1715), originally home to [[Oxford University Press]].

## Historical Significance

The Bodleian is a cornerstone of Oxford's intellectual heritage. Its non-lending policy ensured the preservation of its collection, making it a model for research libraries worldwide.
