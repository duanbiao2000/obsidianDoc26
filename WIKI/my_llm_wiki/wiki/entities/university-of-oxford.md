# University of Oxford

The **University of Oxford** is the oldest university in the English-speaking world, with teaching believed to date back to **1096**. It is located in Oxford, England, approximately 60 miles from London.

## Key Facts

- **Royal Charter**: Granted in **1248**.
- **Catalyst for growth**: King Henry II's **1167** ban on English students attending the University of Paris caused Oxford to become England's foremost centre of learning.
- **College system**: The university comprises **39 self-governing colleges**, each with its own statutes, traditions, and identity. The system was formalised by [[Merton College]]'s statutes in 1264.
- **Academic terms**: Michaelmas, Hilary, and Trinity — named after historic religious calendar dates.
- **Town and gown**: Historical tension between the university (the "gown") and local townspeople (the "town") shaped the institution's development and led to the founding of the [[University of Cambridge]].
- **Nickname**: "City of Dreaming Spires" — referring to the university-dominated architecture.

## Cultural Significance

Oxford has deep literary connections: it inspired *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (Lewis Carroll), *Through The Looking-Glass*, the *Inspector Morse* TV series, and allegedly the visual design of Harry Potter's scar (a stonecutter's mark beside the Sheldonian Theatre).

## Notable Landmarks

- [[Bodleian Library]] — one of Europe's oldest working libraries, with over 13 million items, famously a non-lending library.
- [[Radcliffe Camera]] — iconic circular reading room, part of the Bodleian.
- [[Sheldonian Theatre]] — designed by Christopher Wren; used for graduations and ceremonies.
- [[Christ Church College]] — the largest college; produced 13 UK Prime Ministers.
- [[Merton College]] — founded 1264; template for the college system.
- [[Magdalen College]] — known for its May Morning tradition (500+ years).
- [[All Souls College]] — a research-only college with no undergraduates.
- [[Oxford Castle]] — built 1071 by William the Conqueror; later a prison until 1996.
- [[Ashmolean Museum]] — first public museum in Britain (1683).

## Historical Events

- [[St. Scholastica Day Riot]] (1355) — three-day town-gown riot with 93 killed.
- [[Oxford Martyrs]] (1555) — three Protestant bishops burnt at the stake under Queen Mary I.
- English Civil War — Oxford served as the Royalist capital from 1642 to 1646.
