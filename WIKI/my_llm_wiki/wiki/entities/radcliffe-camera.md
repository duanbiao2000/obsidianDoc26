# Radcliffe Camera

The **Radcliffe Camera** (often called the "Rad Cam") is a iconic circular reading room in Oxford, built in the late **1740s**. It is part of the [[Bodleian Library]] and one of the most photographed buildings in the city.

## Key Facts

- **Funder**: Dr. **John Radcliffe**, royal physician to William III and Mary II, who left £40,000 in his 1714 will for its construction.
- **Original purpose**: An independent library for science and medicine.
- **Absorbed into the Bodleian** in 1927, now serving as an additional reading room.
- **Architecture**: Neoclassical design by James Gibbs; its circular shape and dome make it a landmark.

## Cultural Significance

The Radcliffe Camera is synonymous with the "City of Dreaming Spires" image of Oxford. Its best view is from the tower of the **University Church of St. Mary the Virgin** (accessible to tourists for a fee).
