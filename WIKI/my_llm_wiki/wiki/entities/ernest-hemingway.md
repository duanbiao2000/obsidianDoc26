# Ernest Hemingway

Ernest Hemingway (1899–1961) was an American novelist and short-story writer, known for his distinctive, minimalist writing style. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1954.

## Literary Style: The Iceberg Theory

Hemingway is famous for the **Iceberg Theory** (or Theory of Omission), a technique where the deeper meaning of a story is not explicitly stated but implied through surface-level details. This approach relies heavily on [[show-dont-tell]] principles, trusting the reader to infer the emotional subtext from actions and dialogue.

## Major Works

*   ***For Whom the Bell Tolls*** (1940): A novel set during the Spanish Civil War, exploring themes of sacrifice and interconnectedness.
*   ***The Old Man and the Sea*** (1952): A novella featuring Santiago, another iconic example of the [[code-hero]] archetype.

## Influence

Hemingway's work has had a profound impact on modern literature, particularly in the development of concise, direct prose. His portrayal of the [[code-hero]]—a character who faces adversity with grace and dignity—has influenced both literary criticism and cultural perceptions of masculinity and resilience.
