# A-level (Advanced Level)

A-level (Advanced Level) is the high school graduation qualification system for England, Wales, and Northern Ireland. It is typically completed between ages 16-18 and serves as the primary entry requirement for UK universities. It is the UK equivalent of university entrance exams, but with fundamentally different logic.

## Structure & Philosophy

Students choose **3-4 subjects** to study in depth for two years. Subjects are self-selected, allowing early specialization. This contrasts with systems like China's [[Gaokao]] (6 compulsory subjects, 3-day exam) and offers more student choice and depth per subject.

## Comparison with China's Gaokao

| Dimension | China's Gaokao | UK A-level |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| Subjects | 6 compulsory | 3-4 self-selected |
| Depth | Breadth-first | Depth-first |
| Exam timeline | 3 consecutive days | Distributed over 2 years |
| Student choice | Minimal | Extensive |

The core logic: **what you choose declares who you are and what path you will take.**

## Subject Categories

Over 50 subjects are available, broadly categorized into:

- **Traditional academic** (most popular for university entry): Mathematics, Further Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, History, Geography, Economics, English Literature, Psychology
- **Vocational**: Business, Law, Media Studies, Sports Science, Health & Social Care
- **Arts & Creative**: Fine Art, Music, Drama, Photography, Design & Technology

STEM students typically select Mathematics + Physics + Chemistry; humanities students select History + Economics + English.

## Significance for Personal Development

1. **Cognitive: Deep thinking training** — Fewer subjects means each is studied to near-university depth. Students write argumentative essays, analyze primary sources, and construct their own interpretations rather than memorizing timelines.
2. **Identity: Early specialization** — Subject choices force students to answer "Who am I and what do I want to do?" two to three years earlier than Chinese students.
3. **Social screening: University admissions** — A-level grades are the core metric for UK university admissions. Oxford/Cambridge require A\*AA or A\*A\*A for competitive courses.

## Self-Study Strategy

The recommended priority order for self-study is: **past papers > textbooks > supplementary videos**. Many learners waste time on videos without understanding the exam format and mark scheme logic. Practice with past papers and mark schemes is essential.

## Exam Boards

Three main exam boards set syllabi and exams: [[AQA]], [[Edexcel]], and [[OCR]]. Self-learners must confirm their target exam board before selecting resources.
