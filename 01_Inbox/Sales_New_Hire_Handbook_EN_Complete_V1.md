### Chapter 1 | Sales Responsibilities and Authority Boundaries

> Source: English Volume 1, page 3; complete PDF combined page 7.

#### 1.1 What does this role need to accomplish?

The supplied job description includes domestic and international market research, needs analysis, market development and maintenance, and customer reception. The common thread is to identify potential opportunities, understand requirements, coordinate company resources and keep work moving. English is a tool for part of the work, not the whole job.

#### 1.2 How the four types of work connect

Market research asks, “Where should we look, and whom should we approach?” Customer development asks, “How do we establish contact?” Needs analysis asks, “What exactly does the customer require?” Maintenance and reception ask, “How do we sustain communication and advance the next step?” A list of companies is not a portfolio of customers, and a reply is not an order.

#### 1.3 Shared foundations of domestic and international business

Both require customer verification, confirmation of products and commercial conditions, communication records and internal coordination. International business may also involve language, cross-border delivery, currencies, documents and export requirements. Actual procedures depend on the company and transaction; this chapter assumes no universal payment method or trade term.

#### 1.4 Track the process as well as results

Results may include orders, delivery and collection of payment. Process records include relevant leads, clarified requirements, sample feedback and confirmed next actions. Confirm how the company defines performance measures after joining. Do not repeatedly contact unsuitable prospects merely to increase activity counts, and do not report unsupported forecasts as confirmed achievements.

#### Exercise 1

In your own words, explain the problem addressed by market research, customer development, needs analysis and account maintenance. Give one reviewable deliverable for each. Passing criterion: the four functions are distinct, and their outputs can be inspected or verified.

### Chapter 1 | From Lead to Customer: The Overall Process

> Source: English Volume 1, page 4; complete PDF combined page 8.

#### 1.5 Suggested learning workflow—not an existing company procedure

Find a lead → check its background → make first contact → clarify requirements → conduct internal review → provide a quotation or sample → confirm commercial terms → review the contract and order → coordinate delivery → track payment → provide after-sales support and pursue repeat business. Real opportunities may loop back, pause or end. Do not force an unsuitable opportunity forward.

#### 1.6 Keep evidence at every stage

Lead: company website and reasons for fit. Requirements: the customer’s original request and confirmation record. Quotation: approved version and sending date. Sample: sample identity, dispatch and feedback. Delivery: confirmed milestones and explanations of exceptions. After-sales: the issue, outcome and closure status.

#### 1.7 Minimum follow-up record

Record at least the company and contact; date and channel; the customer’s words or a faithful needs summary; confirmed information; open questions; next action; owner; and agreed completion time. Do not invent confirmation of timing, quantity or purchase intent that the customer has not given.

#### Training case—not an actual transaction

A company asks whether a high-purity material can be supplied for evaluation. Rather than immediately quoting or promising stock, the salesperson first asks about the material identity, target specification, form, quantity, application, quality documents, delivery region and timing. The appropriate team then reviews supply feasibility and other applicable requirements.

#### Exercise 2

Write a follow-up record for this case. If purity and quantity have not been stated, mark them “awaiting customer confirmation” instead of inserting estimates. The next step must specify who will contact the customer and what information they will request.

### Chapter 1 | Internal Coordination and Commitment Limits

> Source: English Volume 1, page 5; complete PDF combined page 9.

#### 1.8 Identify the question, then the responsible role

Product suitability and technical parameters: the technical or designated product lead. Testing and quality documents: quality staff or another authorized role. Production scheduling and lead time: production and planning. Receipt of funds and bank details: finance. Contract and compliance matters: the designated approval route. Confirm actual department names and authority after joining.

#### 1.9 What sales can take responsibility for

Learn and use approved external materials; record requirements accurately; organize questions and coordinate replies; track agreed tasks; and tell the customer the current status. Sales is responsible for communication quality but must not replace technical judgment or company approval with personal guesses.

#### 1.10 What must not be promised without authorization

Unconfirmed prices or discounts; payment periods or credit limits; stock or delivery times; technical suitability; free samples or compensation; proprietary processes or non-public customer details; or broad claims such as “we can export to every country.” This is a learning checklist, not the company’s authority policy.

#### 1.11 Five elements of an internal request

Customer background and application; product and specification; the exact question requiring confirmation; original supporting material; and the requested response time with its reason. Replace “The customer is urgent—please look” with a request someone can act on.

#### Example: request for internal confirmation

The customer is planning a material evaluation. Please review whether the attached specification can be supplied and which quality documents may be available. Quantity and end use are awaiting customer confirmation. First identify any missing information. Once it is complete, please assess quotation and timing. Please advise an expected response time so that we can update the customer.

### Chapter 1 | Exceptions and Daily Work

> Source: English Volume 1, page 6; complete PDF combined page 10.

#### 1.12 Reply structure: verify first, commit second

Acknowledge the question; distinguish known facts from items requiring confirmation; identify the next step; and agree an update you can deliver. If the review completion time is itself unknown, agree when to report progress rather than promising a final answer by an unsupported deadline.

#### Example: delivery timing has not been verified

I understand that you need the material as soon as possible. We need to confirm the specifications, quantity and production schedule before committing to a delivery date. I will check internally and update you at the agreed time.

#### 1.13 Four steps for handling exceptions

Record facts without rushing to assign blame; assess effects on the customer and internal work; notify the authorized owner and discuss options; maintain a consistent external message and continue updating. Do not skip necessary checks concerning safety, law, payments or sensitive information merely to advance a sale.

#### 1.14 Suggested daily order of work

First check promised replies and exceptions; then handle clear requirements and internal coordination; schedule prospecting; update records; and review unfinished actions before ending the day. This is a study recommendation and may be adapted to actual company arrangements.

#### Chapter completion criteria

Explain the overall process in five minutes; produce a complete follow-up record; avoid inventing technical, pricing or delivery answers; and know whom to consult and how to return the confirmed information to the customer.

### Chapter 2 | Company Introductions and Audience Focus

> Source: English Volume 1, page 8; complete PDF combined page 12.

#### 2.3 One-minute introduction—from an applicant’s perspective

Based on the company website I have reviewed, Zhuzhou Keneng was established in 2001 and works with minor metals and related materials, including indium, gallium, bismuth, tellurium and tin. The applications described include semiconductors, displays and solar energy. My understanding is that this role requires not only English communication but also market research, an understanding of customer needs and consistent follow-up. I would use company-confirmed information for detailed product parameters and commercial conditions.

#### 2.4 Why applicants should not say “our company”

At the interview stage, state your status clearly. Wording such as “based on the information I have reviewed” does not imply that you are already employed or authorized to speak for the company. Even after joining, your authority depends on your role and approval requirements.

#### 2.5 Structure for a three-minute introduction

Part 1: who the company is and what it does. Part 2: one product category and application relevant to the audience, rather than the entire catalogue. Part 3: ask what matters to the customer, then decide whether to discuss specifications, documents, samples or commercial terms. An introduction should establish relevance, not say everything at once.

#### Adapt to the person you are speaking with

Purchasing: learn the product, buying cycle and supplier-evaluation requirements. Technical staff: learn the application, specifications and testing needs. Management: confirm cooperation goals and the decision process. These are practice prompts, not assumptions that each role has only one concern.

#### Exercise 3

Record a 60–90-second introduction. The Chinese source asks for Chinese delivery; for this English edition, practise it in English, retaining the same content checks. Label every sentence as public fact, personal interpretation or information requiring confirmation. Delete unsupported absolutes such as “all,” “guaranteed” and “world number one.”

### Self-Assessment and Reference Answers

> Source: English Volume 1, page 11; complete PDF combined page 15.

#### Scenario 1: “Can you ship next week?”

Background: specification, quantity and stock are unconfirmed. Write a reply and list the necessary internal checks. Reference: clarify the specification, quantity, delivery location and whether the requested timing means shipment or arrival. Then verify supply, scheduling and applicable procedures. Do not immediately say yes. Explain when you will update progress; do not invent a confirmed date.

#### Scenario 2: “If you have management-system certification, the material suits our application, right?”

Reference: certification alone does not establish suitability. Obtain the application, specification and acceptance requirements for the appropriate review. Management-system certification and product testing are different. Confirm the current certificate version and scope before sending it.

Reference: no. These are leads first. Relevance, demand and whether contact is appropriate still need validation. Record sources and fit; do not count a company list as customers won.

Reference: use the public information in Chapter 2 and speak as an applicant. For unconfirmed capacity, stock or customer details, explain the information boundary. Do not present study exercises as actual reception experience.

### Chapter 3 | 3.1 Product Identity and Classification

> Source: English Volume 2, page 3; complete PDF combined page 19.

#### Ask “What is it?” before “How pure is it?”

Identify at least the material name, element or chemical formula, product form and specification version. Indium and indium oxide are not the same product; neither are gallium and gallium oxide. Sharing an element does not make products interchangeable. If a customer requests “gallium material,” clarify whether this means elemental gallium, gallium oxide or another gallium-containing material.

#### Basic identification

Indium: In. Gallium: Ga. Bismuth: Bi. Tellurium: Te. Tin: Sn. Indium oxide commonly refers to In2O3; gallium oxide to Ga2O3; bismuth oxide to Bi2O3. Confirm the actual composition, crystal form and specification in formal documentation.

#### Catalogue listing versus supply

The company catalogue lists items including high-purity gallium granules, indium ingots, high-purity indium, indium sheets, ultra-pure indium, indium oxide and indium beads. [K1] A listing is a starting point for inquiry, not proof that every size is in stock or every grade is immediately available.

#### Material versus finished device

Supplying a raw material for semiconductor preparation is not the same as supplying wafers or chips. A listed application does not mean a customer’s process has been validated. State whether the discussion concerns a raw material, a material used in preparation, or an end product.

#### Exercise 3-A

A customer asks for “gallium powder.” Clarify material identity, application and form. Do not immediately recommend gallium oxide powder from the website. Record the original wording and verify the exact product.

> Source: English Volume 2, page 4; complete PDF combined page 20.

#### Common N-grade notation

In high-purity materials, 5N commonly means 99.999% purity. Supplier 5N Plus explains 5N as 99.999% and describes 5N, 6N and 7N materials. [R1][R2] On the same reporting basis, 4N is 99.99%, 6N is 99.9999%, and 7N is 99.99999%. These explain notation; they are not confirmed Keneng supply grades.

#### Conversion on a mass-fraction basis

If purity is a mass fraction and the entire remainder is counted as impurity: 4N corresponds to a total impurity ceiling of 100 ppm; 5N to 10 ppm; 6N to 1 ppm; and 7N to 0.1 ppm. ppm means parts per million; ppb means parts per billion. On a mass-fraction basis, 1 ppm = 1 mg/kg and 1 ppb = 1 µg/kg. State the basis before converting.

#### Calculation

Remainder percentage = 100% − purity percentage. For 99.999%, the remainder is 0.001%, or a mass fraction of 0.00001: 10 ppm. Do not confuse 0.001% with 0.001 ppm.

#### 4N5 and grade boundaries

4N5 is commonly used for 99.995%, but an abbreviation alone is not a formal supply commitment. Confirm the parties’ definitions, elements tested and exclusions. A historical company article mentions 4N5–5N indium for targets; this does not establish a current order specification. [K5]

#### Necessary limitation

Remainders can be compared directly only when the purity basis, testing scope and data rules align. A critical individual impurity may matter more than the overall grade. A higher grade does not automatically satisfy every process requirement.

#### Unit-rendering note

The extracted Chinese PDF text can omit the micro prefix. This edition explicitly renders micrograms as µg/kg and micrometres as µm; it does not interpret the extracted “g/kg” as the intended ppb conversion.

### 3.3 Impurity Bases: Equal Numbers May Not Be Comparable

> Source: English Volume 2, page 5; complete PDF combined page 21.

#### Overall grade and individual limits

A customer may require both an overall purity grade and a maximum for a particular impurity. The former is a summary; the latter is a specific constraint. Check each requirement rather than saying “our grade is higher.” Any example limit must be identified as a customer condition or simulation, not inserted into company specifications without evidence.

#### What “trace metals basis” means

The expression indicates a defined scope for purity reporting; do not assume every substance is included. MilliporeSigma product information contains explicit exclusions: for example, a potassium hydroxide trace-metals purity excludes sodium, while composition and water are listed separately. [R3] Read the complete specification, not only the percentage in the title.

#### Special point for oxides

Oxygen is part of the intended oxide compound, not an impurity to subtract. Compound content, metal-basis purity and elemental content can be different measures. Ask technical or quality staff to confirm the basis; do not rewrite the customer’s requirement yourself.

#### Detection limits and “not detected”

ND, below the detection limit, or below the quantification limit does not mean an absolute zero concentration. Preserve the inequality and unit in a result such as “< value,” and confirm the method and definition. Abbreviations and symbols may differ between reports; ask the issuer when unclear.

#### Exercise 3-B

A report states only “5N; Fe <0.2 ppm.” Can you say there is no iron? No. Can you assert that total impurities, counting all elements, must be below 10 ppm? No: these two statements alone do not establish the calculation scope or method.

### 3.4 Form, Dimensions and Packaging Matter

> Source: English Volume 2, page 6; complete PDF combined page 22.

#### Form is part of the use conditions

Common forms include ingots, granules, sheets or foils, rods, beads and powders. Even with the same material and purity, form, dimensions, unit weight, surface condition and packaging may affect handling and processing. Ask the customer rather than deciding for them.

#### Questions about metals

What form is required? What unit weight, dimensions and tolerances? Any surface requirements? Will the package be opened in a clean environment? Are there container-material, opening or sampling requirements? These are questions to confirm, not claims that every option is available.

#### Questions about powders

Besides purity, clarify the particle-size metric and test method. Does “2 microns” mean a statistic, a distribution or an upper limit? Are crystal form, specific surface area or other properties specified? Do not add unsupported promises; forward actual requirements accurately for technical review.

#### A size range is not a distribution statistic

The company gallium oxide page gives a general particle-size range of 2–20 µm. [K4] This is not D50 = 2 µm and does not establish D90 or a maximum size. Confirm requirements and available testing information before a formal quotation.

#### Packaging levels

Distinguish net weight, the quantity in each inner package, outer-package count and gross weight. Website examples are public historical descriptions; current quantities, containers, labels and transport arrangements need confirmation. Do not translate “1 kg per bottle” into “1 kg net per carton.”

### 3.5 Product Documents, Batches and Acceptance

> Source: English Volume 2, page 7; complete PDF combined page 23.

#### Specification: the required characteristics

A specification normally identifies the material, parameters and limits. It may be a standard document or a mutually confirmed version. Record its number, version and date. A promotional page is not automatically an agreed acceptance specification.

#### CoA: check product and batch

Certificate of Analysis. Formats vary by issuer. Check product, batch, test items, results, units, methods and dates. MilliporeSigma’s official document search uses a lot number to locate a CoA, illustrating the importance of batch matching. [R4] Its practices do not establish Keneng’s document format.

#### SDS and TDS serve different purposes

SDS: Safety Data Sheet, covering hazards and information relevant to handling, storage, emergencies and transport. TDS: Technical Data Sheet, describing technical and use-related information. An SDS is not a batch purity report; a TDS is not automatically batch-specific test evidence. Confirm document identity, applicable region and version through the company.

#### Suggested reading order

First match product and batch; then specification version, items and limits; then results, symbols and units; finally methods, dates and issuer information. A sample CoA used to illustrate capability must be labelled “example—not the supplied batch.” Never change its batch number to pass it off as another report.

#### Acceptance requires more than one document

Clarify whether the customer accepts the supplier’s report, uses a third party, and has agreed sampling and discrepancy procedures. Do not promise that every test method will produce exactly the same results.

### 3.8 Product Learning Example 3: Gallium Oxide

> Source: English Volume 2, page 10; complete PDF combined page 26.

Listed applications include LEDs, semiconductor materials for electronics, insulating layers for gallium-based semiconductor materials, ultraviolet filters and optoelectronic materials. [K4]

### 3.9 Safety, Packaging and Supply Confirmation

> Source: English Volume 2, page 11; complete PDF combined page 27.

#### Safety limits for sales

Do not recommend melting, grinding, acid washing, mixing or recovery treatment based on personal experience. Use the applicable SDS and approved guidance through personnel with the appropriate responsibilities. This handbook does not give chemical-handling procedures.

#### “Metal” is not a single risk category

Elements, compounds and forms may have different hazards and transport requirements. Experience with one ingot does not establish that a powder or another compound is equally safe. Absence of a warning on a website is not proof that transport classification needs no verification.

#### Suggested pre-supply checks

Material and specification identified; document version confirmed; packing and quantity confirmed; technical and quality questions reviewed; price and delivery conditions approved; customer and use information meet applicable review needs; relevant licensing, transport and compliance matters checked by designated personnel. This is neither legal advice nor an existing company procedure.

#### The gap between website and current supply

A catalogue, historical article, sample report, oral discussion and formal order basis are not interchangeable. When they conflict, stop sending unsupported conclusions and ask an authorized owner to confirm the current version.

#### Information-management exercise

Create a record card for indium, gallium and gallium oxide: page title, URL, access date, verified public facts and current unknowns. Remove unsupported claims such as “low price” or “best quality.”

### Chapter 4 | 4.1 From Material to Application

> Source: English Volume 2, page 13; complete PDF combined page 29.

#### Build a needs chain

Material identity → specification and form → customer preparation or processing stage → downstream product → application. Identify where the customer operates before asking questions; do not send the same information simply because “semiconductor” appears in its business description. [M]

#### Practice map: product, application and customer type

High-purity indium → compound semiconductor, high-purity alloy and ITO powder uses listed on the website → research relevant material manufacturers, but do not assume any specific company buys indium externally. [K2] High-purity gallium → III–V-related uses → research relevant manufacturing stages and verify the actual gallium source used. [K3] Gallium oxide → semiconductor and optoelectronic uses → research companies or R&D units that actually use the powder or a related precursor; still verify specification and process fit. [K4]

#### Do not skip intermediate stages

A chipmaker may not buy elemental gallium directly. A display end-product manufacturer may not buy indium ingots. Upstream suppliers may purchase them, or the process may use other forms or compounds. Industry classification is a lead, not purchase evidence.

#### Three research questions

What does the company make? Could that process use this type of material? Is there public evidence of the material used or the buying role? If not, label the proposition a hypothesis and design an economical validation question rather than recording it as fact.

### 4.2 Customer Types and Buying Roles

> Source: English Volume 2, page 14; complete PDF combined page 30.

#### Manufacturers and processors

Learn the actual application, production or experimental stage, specifications, quality conditions, supplier-evaluation process and purchasing stage. A large company does not automatically have a large requirement for your material.

#### Traders and channel customers

Understand territories served, target applications, typical specifications, order requirements and documents. Determine the necessary end-user and end-use information through applicable compliance procedures. Do not skip review because the buyer is a trader, or assume a trader is inherently unreliable.

#### R&D and evaluation organizations

Clarify experimental goals, evaluation quantity, timing, documentation and possible next steps. A small sample request does not guarantee mass production and does not remove quality or safety requirements.

#### Roles within one organization

Purchasing may coordinate price and supply; technical staff may assess process fit; quality staff may focus on testing and acceptance; management may approve. Responsibilities vary. Ask who sets the specification, evaluates samples, approves suppliers and places orders.

#### Example question

Who will confirm the technical specification, and what approval steps follow the sample evaluation?

### 4.3 Needs Interviews: Make an Inquiry Reviewable

> Source: English Volume 2, page 15; complete PDF combined page 31.

#### Layer 1 | Identify the material

Which material or compound is required? Is there a specification, current material name or product number? Is this replacement sourcing or a new-project evaluation? Resolve ambiguity before quoting the wrong product.

#### Layer 2 | Technical and acceptance requirements

Purity and calculation basis; critical impurities; form, dimensions or particle size; required documents; test method and acceptance criteria. Mark unclear items unknown rather than pushing the customer to invent a number.

#### Layer 3 | Commercial and delivery context

Evaluation quantity, expected purchasing volume and certainty; shipment versus arrival target; delivery region; packing; communication and decision roles; use information and applicable reviews. A forecast is not a confirmed order.

#### Layer 4 | Agree the next action

Should the next step be specification review, document exchange or sample assessment? Who supplies missing information? Who reviews internally? When will progress be updated? Write an action, not just “keep in touch.”

#### Ask in a sensible sequence

In the first reply, prioritize questions that affect material identity and supply assessment. Do not send a long questionnaire unnecessarily. Explain why application or end-user details are needed for suitability or applicable checks; do not request unrelated personal information. [M]

### 4.4 Internal Technical and Quality Confirmation

> Source: English Volume 2, page 16; complete PDF combined page 32.

#### Retain the customer’s original wording

When a customer requires a grade, retain the email or attachment. An internal summary must say “customer requirement,” not “company can already meet it.” Do not lose minimum, maximum, less than, or inequality symbols in translation.

#### Separate reviews

Technical: correct material, specification feasibility and further testing. Quality: documents and methods supporting assessment. Commercial: quantity, price, lead time and terms. Compliance: additional review needed. Actual responsibilities belong to designated company personnel.

#### Internal handover structure

Background: customer, application, project stage. Requirement: material, specification, form, quantity. Attachments: original specification and version. Questions: each item needing confirmation. Constraints: desired timing and unknowns. Requested output: an available specification, differences or additional questions—not merely “can we make it?”

#### Manage differences

When requested and available specifications differ, list the differences for the customer to assess. Do not alter its limits. Explain assumptions and unvalidated elements in an alternative. Do not promise “exactly the same result” without authority.

#### Exercise 4-A

The customer says “6N gallium oxide, as fine as possible.” Clarify at least the purity basis and critical impurities; particle metric and method; application; acceptance criteria; quantity and timing. Turn “as fine as possible” into defined requirements, not an arbitrary smallest number.

### 4.6 Sample Evaluation: Give the Trial a Purpose

> Source: English Volume 2, page 20; complete PDF combined page 36.

#### Before sending a sample

Confirm identity and specification version; quantity and packing; what will be assessed; customer test method and intended conditions; documents; delivery information; fees, dispatch and approvals. Internal confirmation and applicable compliance checks are needed; small quantity is not automatic permission to send.

#### One sample does not represent every future batch

Record batch and dispatch information. Customer approval of one sample does not approve every future batch or arbitrary parameter changes. Agree whether final specifications, supplier qualification or documents still need confirmation before production purchasing.

#### Questions after evaluation

Was the sample received and testing started? Which items passed or differed? What methods and conditions were used? Can necessary results be shared? Is the next step more information, retesting, an adjusted proposal or ending the evaluation? Avoid only asking “How was it?”

#### Lack of fit versus nonconformity

Failure in an application is not automatically product nonconformity; specifications may not align or methods may differ. Establish facts and agreed conditions for the relevant team to assess. Do not immediately promise compensation or declare the customer’s method wrong.

#### Exercise 4-B

The sample passes but no order follows. List information to clarify: approval, budget, project schedule, other tests and commercial conditions. Do not record “sample passed” as “order confirmed.”

### Chapter 5 | 5.1 Define the Research Question First

> Source: English Volume 3, page 3; complete PDF combined page 47.

#### Turn an instruction into a question

“Research overseas markets” is too broad. Reframe it: “For a specified material and specification, within a defined region and application stage, identify possible buyers and needs requiring validation, then recommend development actions.” Research should support decisions, not merely collect industry news. The International Trade Administration emphasizes demand, competition and market suitability rather than size figures alone. [S1]

#### Six fields in a research assignment

Product boundary: what is included and excluded. Geographic boundary: where to research. Customer boundary: the manufacturing or purchasing stage targeted. Time boundary: data coverage and access date. Decision question: continue research, prioritize validation or defer. Resource limits: time, information and authority available.

#### Teaching example

Topic: prospects related to high-purity indium. Do not target every semiconductor company. First identify businesses publicly describing relevant materials preparation; then verify whether they buy elemental indium externally, the required specification and the purchasing method. This is a research plan, not a verified market conclusion.

#### Common errors

Deciding the market must be attractive and seeking only supporting evidence; substituting a macro report for product-level needs analysis; confusing materials, downstream devices and end-use industries; or expanding the scope without recording the change.

#### Exercise 5-A

Rewrite “help the company find foreign customers” as one executable assignment. Include product, region, customer stage, expected output and time boundary. Mark unresolved items “to be confirmed with the supervisor.”

### 5.2 Information Sources and Evidence Grades

> Source: English Volume 3, page 4; complete PDF combined page 48.

#### Primary and secondary research

Primary research gathers information through interviews, questionnaires or direct contact. Secondary research uses trade statistics, public reports and existing material. [S1] Primary information is not automatically correct, nor is secondary information automatically outdated. Assess source, method, coverage and relevance.

#### Suggested source layers

Company websites and formal product documents: verify company statements and products. Official registers and statistics: check entities, statistical scope and dates. Associations, exhibitions and technical literature: discover companies and process clues. News and directories: generate leads, then verify important conclusions. Sales discussions: record the speaker’s role and words.

#### Separate fact, inference and unknown

Fact: a website lists a product. Inference: its production stage may use a material. Unknown: whether that material is bought externally. Incorrect: the company makes a related product, so it definitely buys a stated quantity every month. Do not attach unsupported numbers to an inference.

#### Suggested evidence grades

E1: traceable original information with a clear source, date and basis. E2: traceable information with some scope or timing details missing. E3: directory entries, second-hand statements or unverified leads. Grades describe the current evidence state, not absolute truth. Website publicity may also need cross-checking.

#### Evidence-register fields

Conclusion ID; statement supported; source organization; title and link; publication date; access date; quotation; applicability; evidence grade; open verification items. Important judgments should lead back to the original, not only a search snippet.

### 5.3 Searching, Records and Information Management

> Source: English Volume 3, page 5; complete PDF combined page 49.

#### Combine material, process and document terms

Material terms: indium, gallium, gallium oxide. Process or business terms: manufacturer, materials, target, compound semiconductor. Document terms: specification, technical data, annual report. Identify the material before combining terms. Search results are candidate leads, not purchasing facts.

#### Search patterns

“material name + application”; “downstream material + manufacturer”; “company name + official website”; “company name + product”; “company name + annual report.” Once the official domain is known, use “site:domain product term.” Do not invent contact names or email addresses.

#### Work in rounds

#### Suggested folder structure

#### AI boundaries

Require original sources for important information and check them yourself. Do not upload customer specifications, quotations, personal contact information or internal files without permission.

### 5.4 Data Scope: Why Market-Size Figures Can Mislead

> Source: English Volume 3, page 6; complete PDF combined page 50.

#### Ask what the figure measures

“Market size” may refer to raw metal, a compound, wafers, devices or final equipment. Downstream device revenue is not upstream metal purchasing value. Before quoting a number, specify product scope, region, year, currency and whether it measures value or quantity.

#### Trade-data limitations

A commodity code may combine purities or forms and may not isolate the target product. Classification and scope should be checked by appropriate specialists. This volume assigns no HS code. Import value, export value, production, apparent consumption and end-market size are different measures.

#### Units and periods

Distinguish kilograms, tonnes, units and money; calendar versus fiscal years; full years versus partial months; nominal values versus changes adjusted for prices. Value or volume can rise for different reasons. Do not assert demand growth without supporting evidence.

#### Teaching calculation

Simulated data: 100 units last year and 120 this year means a 20% increase on the same basis. If product coverage differs, direct comparison is inappropriate. When reports conflict, check scope, period and definition instead of averaging unlike figures.

#### When information is insufficient

“Comparable product-level statistics are not yet available; this work currently covers company leads and application stages” is more useful than an invented market size. Explain the decision limitation and which interviews could fill the gap.

### 5.5 Compare Markets Separately from Eligibility to Proceed

Are product and application clear? Is the company’s relevant supply capability confirmed or still unknown? Have applicable reviews and delivery feasibility been assessed? Where compliance or essential eligibility is unresolved, write “awaiting confirmation; no transaction commitment.” A market score cannot cancel a hold.

### 5.6 Competitor Analysis: Compare Like with Like

> Source: English Volume 3, page 8; complete PDF combined page 52.

#### What to examine

Suppliers of comparable materials; different forms and service proposals; and the customer’s option of keeping its current source. Alternative materials or processes require technical assessment, not unilateral sales confirmation.

#### Align the conditions

Material identity, purity basis, individual impurities, form and dimensions, quantity, documents, packing, delivery place, timing, payment and validity. Different conditions make a simple per-kilogram price comparison insufficient.

#### What public records can support

Website products, public specifications, markets served, publicly disclosable credentials and channels. Record source dates. Do not pretend to know competitors’ current quotations, costs, customer lists or actual stock.

#### Improper practices

Impersonating a customer or concealing identity to obtain sensitive material; spreading unverified negative claims; treating website omissions as proof of absence; or turning “we can check the documents” into “no competitor has documents.”

#### Exercise 5-B

### 5.7 Interviews and Validation: Seek More Than Confirmation

> Source: English Volume 3, page 9; complete PDF combined page 53.

#### State a hypothesis, then design questions

Hypothesis: a category of companies may buy a metal externally. Questions: which material is used at that stage, is it made internally or bought, and who sets the specification? If answers contradict the hypothesis, update the profile rather than ignoring the evidence.

#### Avoid leading questions

Instead of “You must also be looking for a higher-purity supplier, right?” ask “What criteria do you currently use to evaluate supplied materials?” Do not begin by demanding confidential formulations. Start with relevance, needs and shareable specifications.

#### Sample and coverage

A few interviews describe those respondents, not the entire market. Record sample sources, roles, refusals and non-responses. No reply does not establish no demand, and is not an explicit rejection.

#### Confirm after an interview

Restate key facts; distinguish personal judgments from formal company requirements; retain qualifications for unconfirmed information; and record agreed follow-up arrangements. Recording, storage and sharing must follow applicable requirements and company rules.

#### Example validation record

Initial hypothesis: the company might need elemental gallium. Interview: the contact says it purchases a different gallium compound; acceptance of elemental gallium is unknown. Conclusion: material fit is not established. Request the specification rather than labelling it a confirmed metal buyer.

### 5.8 A One-Page Market Research Brief

> Source: English Volume 3, page 10; complete PDF combined page 54.

#### Part 1 | Question and conclusion

The title identifies product and boundaries. Start with the current judgment: validate further, prioritize contact, defer or insufficient information. State whether research is preliminary or includes interviews. Do not overstate its depth.

#### Part 2 | Core evidence

List three to five key items, each with source and date. Separate fact and inference. Include conflicting or unsupportive information rather than hiding it.

#### Part 3 | Opportunities and limitations

Opportunity: relevant application stages and candidate companies. Limitations: specifications, purchasing behavior, quantities and feasibility not confirmed. Explain how uncertainty affects action without unsupported market-share or sales forecasts.

#### Part 4 | Action recommendation

Specify target type, questions, owner, intended review time and stopping conditions. Example: “Verify whether three candidates buy this material externally; if none fits, revise the target stage.” Do not say “we will definitely win three customers.”

### 6.2 Records, Company Relationships and Deduplication

> Source: English Volume 3, page 12; complete PDF combined page 56.

#### A company is not a contact

Keep company and contact records separate. One company may have purchasing, R&D and quality contacts. A group may contain different legal entities, plants and buying centers. Do not merge them casually.

#### Suggested company identifier

Use an internal ID and record official domain, normalized name, region and available entity identifiers. Keep Chinese name, English name, brand and abbreviation distinct until relationships are verified. Similar domains alone do not establish affiliation.

#### Deduplication sequence

Find possible matches by domain and entity name; check address and business; confirm whether the entity is the same; retain records and sources; and link duplicate contacts to the master record. Do not overwrite history and lose a request to stop contact.

#### Minimum company record

Company ID, name, website, region, business, fit evidence, contacts and roles, source, status, last action, next action, owner and update date. Collect only business-necessary personal information and use it within authorization.

#### Exercise 6-B

Two business cards share a brand but show different addresses. Verify legal entities, plants and purchasing relationships before linking them. Do not count them as two customers won or merge them without checking.

### 6.3 Company Verification, Risk and Holds

> Source: English Volume 3, page 13; complete PDF combined page 57.

#### Layers of verification

Basic existence: can company and website be checked? Business fit: is the public business relevant? Contact authenticity: can identity and channel be verified? Credit and transaction: further review under company procedures. Compliance: the designated owner confirms applicable checks. An internet search does not replace full due diligence. [S2][S3]

#### Use multiple sources

Company websites, legally accessible registers, formal business documents, public news and authorized third-party reports. Investigate conflicts. Registration is not proof of good credit; a polished website is not proof of purchasing capacity.

#### Signals requiring escalation

Conflicting entity, domain, address or payment details; pressure to bypass review; refusal to explain a critical use while demanding shipment; unusual requests for other customers’ information. These are signals, not proof of fraud. Record facts and pause or escalate under procedures; do not make public accusations.

#### Three independent statuses

Commercial fit: fit / no fit / unknown. Basic verification: checked / concerns / unchecked. Applicable review: may proceed / on hold / awaiting confirmation. A high commercial score cannot override a hold.

#### Limits of U.S. references

[S2][S3] support verification methods. U.S. services and legal context do not automatically apply to Chinese exporters. This volume assigns no screening result, licensing determination or credit limit; appropriate professionals must confirm them.

### 6.4 Prospect Priority: Every Score Needs a Reason

> Source: English Volume 3, page 14; complete PDF combined page 58.

#### Apply prerequisites before scoring

Resolve clear mismatches and outstanding holds rather than treating a high score as permission to quote. Scoring allocates research and follow-up effort; it is not a credit rating, compliance decision or probability of an order.

#### Example model | 100 points

Application fit: 25. Clarity of material/specification: 20. Strength of needs evidence: 25. Access to the appropriate role: 15. Clarity of next action: 15. Rate each dimension from 0 to 5; calculate rating ÷ 5 × weight. This is a teaching suggestion only.

#### Rating anchors

0: no current evidence or confirmed mismatch—state which. 1: a weak lead. 3: explicit traceable information still requiring verification. 5: relevant information confirmed through an appropriate source. Give dimension-specific reasons; do not claim statistical precision between levels.

#### Handle unknowns explicitly

You may temporarily use zero to calculate a lower-bound score, but also label it unknown, not negative. Report coverage separately: dimensions with verifiable information ÷ 5. A low-scoring, poorly covered prospect may deserve more research, not an automatic “no value” label.

#### Turn ranking into action

Add an action and time: clarify material, find the correct role, arrange a discussion, obtain missing information or archive. Do not invent budgets, quantities or decision authority to improve scores.

Purchasing may know inquiries and procedures; technical staff may confirm specifications and tests; quality staff may confirm acceptance; management may approve. Verify roles: seniority alone does not mean responsibility for this purchase.

### 6.6 Prospect Stages and Next Actions

> Source: English Volume 3, page 16; complete PDF combined page 60.

#### Suggested definitions

L0 Lead: candidate identified. L1 Researched: basic business and fit recorded. L2 Contacted: contact occurred, not necessarily demand. L3 Initial need established: substantive material, application or project information. L4 Internal review: requirements submitted for evaluation. Quotations, samples and orders are developed in later volumes.

#### Do not advance stages on enthusiasm

“Thanks for the information” is not purchasing confirmation. A meeting is not supplier approval. Frequent replies are not a budget approval. Use recordable facts for stage changes.

New information; remaining unknowns; next action; owner; update time; and the basis for continuing contact. Repeated reminders without new information are not progress in needs analysis.

The International Trade Administration recommends tailoring follow-up to the buying stage, referring to actual discussions and avoiding excessive mass outreach. [S5] This handbook sets no universal daily-contact rule; frequency depends on the case, company policy and applicable requirements.

Record clear irrelevance, no current project or refusal. For a deferred opportunity, record the trigger for reconsideration. Archiving is not deleting evidence; retain necessary records according to authority and retention requirements.

### Chapter 7 | 7.1 Classifying and Reading Inquiries

> Source: English Volume 3, page 18; complete PDF combined page 62.

#### Classify by current purpose

Information request: products and documents. Technical clarification: specifications or fit. Sample evaluation: trial material. Commercial inquiry: prices or terms. Existing-order issue: the relevant execution process. Categories may overlap and are not determined by the subject line alone.

#### Mark information sentence by sentence

Identify material, specification, quantity, use, documents, timing, destination, roles and requested action. Preserve must, preferred, approximately and subject to approval; do not convert intention into a firm requirement.

#### Three-column method

What the customer explicitly said; what you infer but have not verified; what must be clarified. “About 1 kg for testing” is approximately 1 kg for a test—not monthly demand or a confirmed purchase.

#### What to answer first

Acknowledge receipt, then prioritize information affecting identity and assessability. If the request conflicts with existing information, explain and clarify. Do not create an unapproved price just to reply quickly.

### 7.2 Seven Groups of Needs Questions

#### 1–2 | Material and technical requirements

1. Which material or compound do you need? Is there a current product number or specification?

mandatory?

### 7.3 Staged Interviews and Active Listening

> Source: English Volume 3, page 20; complete PDF combined page 64.

#### First reply | A few key questions

Clarify identity, core specification and use; add quantity or destination if necessary. Do not ask again for information already in an attachment. Explain that the details support review of suitable specifications, documents and supply arrangements.

#### Further discussion | Complete evaluation conditions

#### Common errors

Pressing for an order before specifications exist; repeating questions already answered; promising a date after hearing ASAP; recording price but not application or acceptance; and using jargon that obstructs clarification.

### 7.5 Ambiguous Words, Specification Gaps and Changes

> Source: English Volume 3, page 22; complete PDF combined page 66.

#### Turn adjectives into conditions

“High purity”: ask grade, basis and critical impurities. “Fine powder”: metric, distribution and testing. “ASAP”: specific shipment or arrival date. “Certificate”: type, items and batch requirements. “Long-term cooperation”: present project and certainty of future plans.

#### Separate mandatory from preferred

Preferred is not mandatory; target is not a guarantee; approximately is not exact. Preserve wording and clarify allowable ranges. Do not lower a requirement yourself or promote a preference into an agreed specification.

#### Specification-difference table

For each item, record customer requirement, current supply information, gap, reviewer and status. If internal supply information is unconfirmed, write pending; do not fill a guessed website value. Alternatives need technical review on both sides.

### 7.6 Assessing an Opportunity Is Not Forecasting an Order

> Source: English Volume 3, page 23; complete PDF combined page 67.

#### Basic information

Is the need genuine and clear? Is the right role involved? Does the requested specification still need matching to company capability? What stage is the project at? Is a next action agreed? Explicitly record unknowns.

#### What signals actually mean

Providing a specification shows willingness to discuss, not purchasing approval. Requesting a quotation means interest in terms, not acceptance. A passed sample describes an evaluation, not approval of all later batches. Expected volume is a forecast, not a contract order.

#### Suggested categories

Advance: clear next actions and suitable for review. Clarify: essential information missing. Defer: project timing is not ready or the customer pauses. Stop / refer: confirmed mismatch or a matter for designated personnel. These are teaching categories.

#### Do not invent close probabilities

Without supporting company history, do not turn an enthusiastic reply into “70% likely to close.” Use stages and actions. If company forecasting is required, use its definitions, evidence and review process, distinguishing estimates from confirmed revenue.

#### Focus of follow-up

Ask “What is missing before the next step?” rather than only “When will you order?” If technical approval is unfinished, help complete the information instead of applying unnecessary sales pressure.

### 7.7 Internal Handover: Make the Work Resumable

> Source: English Volume 3, page 24; complete PDF combined page 68.

#### Handover structure

A one-page summary identifies customer, product, application and stage. Attach the original specification. List known facts, unknowns and differences. Finish with specific questions for reviewers, customer expectations and the intended update arrangement.

#### Technical and quality questions

Is identity correct? How should the grade and individual impurity requirements be assessed? Can the size metric and method be supported? Are required documents and batch evidence available? Avoid only asking “They want this—can we do it?”

#### Commercial and other questions

Are quantity and packing sufficient for assessment? Are destination and timing clear? Do samples, prices, contracts or applicable reviews need a designated owner? Do not invent payment terms or export conclusions at this stage.

#### Pending-confirmation response

We have organized your requirements and are checking the specification and documents. We still need your clarification of the particle-size definition and acceptance method. Once information is complete, we will explain supply options and any differences before confirming commercial terms.

#### Completion criteria

The next colleague can distinguish the customer’s requirement from company confirmation, locate original and latest versions, identify the exact question and know when progress should be updated. Share names and sensitive information only as necessary for the work.

### 8.6 Measures, Denominators and Simulated Data

> Source: English Volume 4, page 8; complete PDF combined page 85.

#### Keep statistical objects separate

Companies, contacts, messages and opportunities are not interchangeable. Contacting two people and sending three messages at one company is not six customers developed. Define deduplication, observation period and denominator.

#### Simulated data—not real performance

Research covered 40 companies. One email was sent to each of 50 distinct authorized email addresses. Receiving servers accepted 45; nine unique contacts replied; five replies indicated relevance; three companies clarified needs. No follow-up messages are included.

#### What can be calculated

Server acceptance: 45/50 = 90%, not inbox placement. Contact reply rate: 9/50 = 18%, or 9/45 = 20% among accepted messages; state the denominator. Relevant replies per contacted address: 5/50 = 10%. Do not divide three companies by 50 contacts and call it a company conversion rate.

#### Use open rates cautiously

Image loading and privacy mechanisms may affect opens. An open record does not prove genuine reading; do not tell a customer “I saw you open it.” The Chinese source cites Google’s statement that it does not track open rates or verify third-party open-rate accuracy. [S4]

#### Actions matter more than attractive numbers

Many bounces: check addresses and technical status. Replies without relevance: review the profile. Clear needs without next steps: review questions and handover. Small or different samples do not prove a best channel.

### 9.7 Non-Response, Refusal and Stopping Contact

> Source: English Volume 4, page 16; complete PDF combined page 93.

#### Silence proves neither rejection nor purchase interest

Check bounces, address, role and relevance. Follow-up frequency depends on arrangements, company rules and applicable requirements. There is no universal three-day reminder schedule or guaranteed-response sequence.

“Not interested. Remove me.” Record and stop; do not keep asking whether price is the reason or move to a personal social account.

### 9.9 Sending Technology and Compliance Boundaries

### 10.2 Writing a One-Page Product Sheet

> Source: English Volume 4, page 20; complete PDF combined page 97.

#### Six modules

1. Name and material identity. 2. Verified form/specification overview. 3. Applications listed in the

website or approved document. 4. Confirmable packing/document information. 5. Information required from the customer. 6. Version, contact and limits.

Clear hierarchy, complete units and adequate space. Tables can list material, indicator, unit, evidence and status. Use images only to identify actual products helpfully. Titles, notes and unknowns should remain distinct in black-and-white printing; do not rely on color alone.

#### Call to action

Ask for specification, application, quantity and documents, not merely “inquiries welcome.” Keep the initial page concise and attach a detailed form separately if needed.

### 10.7 Bilingual Review; 10.8 Version Control

> Source: English Volume 4, page 26; complete PDF combined page 103.

#### Three review passes

Facts: correct parameters and sources. Language: matching meaning, numbers and units. Authority: disclosure permitted and relevant roles have approved. Actual approvers follow company policy, not a new employee’s assumption.

#### Translation pitfalls

Do not drop “approximately” and create a guarantee. “Customizable” is not “all sizes in stock.” “Application” is not certified fit. “Sample report” is not a batch certificate. “Lead time pending” is not “ships immediately.”

#### Numbers and units

Check decimals, percentages, upper/lower limits, inequalities, kg/g, mm/µm, per piece versus carton, and shipment versus arrival. Check critical table cells individually rather than relying on back-translation. The micro symbol is explicitly restored where PDF extraction loses it.

#### Version record

Record subject, language, version, date and owner in file name or metadata. Retain changes, approval state and usage scope. Withdraw or mark old copies. Do not publish customer-specific material as general content.

#### AI’s proper role

Drafting, checklists and formatting can be assisted. Humans verify important parameters, sources, translation and authority. Do not let a model invent credentials, test results, stock, customer praise or contacts. Responsibility cannot be delegated to the tool.

### Chapter 11 | 11.1 Accurate, Concise and Confirmable

> Source: English Volume 5, page 3; complete PDF combined page 115.

#### English as a work tool

Prioritize clear material identity, specification, quantity, timing and action. Long, complex sentences are not necessarily more professional. Before speaking, identify whether the purpose is identification, missing information, a progress update or next-step confirmation.

#### Four different statement types

Known fact: “The customer has requested 500 grams.” Pending: “The quantity is still to be confirmed.” Suggestion: “We could review a sample option.” Commitment: “We confirm shipment on…” This requires clear authority and conditions.

#### Avoid vague guarantees

When uncertain, avoid “No problem,” “definitely” or “guaranteed.” Say what needs confirmation. “Should be fine” is not approval; “under review” is not approved.

#### Exercise 11-A

Rewrite “We should have stock and it can probably arrive next week.” Reference meaning: stock and delivery remain unconfirmed; clarify specification, quantity and destination, then check and agree a progress-update time.

#### Study standard

Clearly explaining what you do not know and how you will verify it serves this training better than memorizing an elaborate introduction.

### 11.2 Speaking Numbers, Units and Purity

> Source: English Volume 5, page 4; complete PDF combined page 116.

#### Quantities and units

500 g: five hundred grams. 2 kg: two kilograms. 0.5 kg: zero point five kilograms. 250 g per ingot: each ingot weighs 250 grams. 10 kg net per carton: the net weight in each carton is 10 kilograms. Check per unit, per bottle and per carton; never omit the unit.

#### Purity and particle size

99.999%: ninety-nine point nine nine nine percent, or five nines purity; formal documents must still specify the reporting basis. 2 µm: two micrometres / micrometers. 0.2 ppm: zero point two parts per million. Use the original specification for actual limits and parameters.

#### Limits and qualifications

At least: a minimum. No more than: a maximum including the stated value. Less than: strictly below. Approximately: an estimate. Target: an objective. Subject to confirmation: not yet confirmed. “<” and “≤” differ and must not be merged in translation.

#### Read-back example

Let me read that back: approximately five hundred grams, with the impurity limit stated as less than zero point two parts per million. Is that correct? This limit is a speaking exercise, not a company specification.

#### Dictation practice

A partner gives three quantities, two percentages and three limits. Record, then repeat; do not guess while responding. In at least one round, change kg to g and check whether the difference is noticed.

### 11.2 Time, Currency and the Meaning of Delivery

> Source: English Volume 5, page 5; complete PDF combined page 117.

#### Unambiguous dates

Avoid only “03/04.” Use a clear form such as 4 March 2027 or 2027-03-04. These dates illustrate wording, not an actual delivery arrangement. Give the date, local time zone and agreed time for meetings; avoid ambiguous abbreviations such as CST alone.

#### Working days and the starting event

Ten working days is not ten calendar days. Specify whose working days and what event starts the period, such as verified payment, specification approval and other completed prerequisites. Do not record production as started when a required condition remains unmet.

#### Shipment versus arrival

Shipment date concerns dispatch; arrival date concerns reaching the destination. Ask: “Does the target date refer to shipment or arrival?” Do not translate “ships next week” into “received next week.” A transport estimate is not automatically an arrival guarantee.

#### Currency and cost scope

USD 1,000 per kg identifies currency and unit; a dollar sign alone may be ambiguous. Clarify whether freight, insurance, taxes or other charges are included. Finance confirms tax inclusion and invoice requirements for domestic RMB quotations; no tax rate is assumed.

#### Exercise

“Need it next Friday”: ask the exact date, shipment or arrival, and destination. Do not guess from your own time zone or make an unsupported commitment.

### 11.4 Clarification, Restatement and Commitment Limits

> Source: English Volume 5, page 7; complete PDF combined page 119.

#### When you did not hear clearly

Could you repeat the last part a little more slowly? Could you spell the material name or send the specification in writing?

Let me confirm my understanding. You need the material for an initial evaluation, and the final specification is not yet approved. Is that correct?

### 11.6 Progress, Delay and Problem Communication

> Source: English Volume 5, page 9; complete PDF combined page 121.

#### Be specific even when work is progressing

We have received the specification and submitted it for review. The impurity limits and packaging request are still being checked. Avoid “Everything is going well” without actual information.

#### Not yet complete

The review is taking longer than expected. We have not confirmed the supply option yet. I will update you at the agreed time, even if the review is still in progress. Agree a realistic time; do not arbitrarily insert a 24-hour deadline.

#### Correcting an earlier error

I need to correct an earlier statement. The date mentioned was a proposed shipment date, not a confirmed arrival date. I apologize for the confusion. We are reviewing the schedule and will provide a clear update. Correct promptly so the customer does not keep deciding on a false understanding. Refer contractual effects to the responsible owner.

#### Customer dissatisfaction

I understand the delay affects your plan. Let us confirm the facts and available options before agreeing a revised arrangement. Do not soothe the customer with unapproved compensation or an expedited-delivery guarantee.

#### Exercise

Replace “It is production’s problem; I cannot do anything” with facts, ownership and action without blaming. An unverified cause must remain a hypothesis.

### 11.7 Live English Test and Correction Card

> Source: English Volume 5, page 10; complete PDF combined page 122.

#### Three rounds

1. A truthful 90-second self-introduction. 2. The customer gives material, purity, quantity and time; ask

questions and restate. 3. One condition changes unexpectedly; identify it and explain what needs confirmation.

#### What to observe

Understanding rather than memorization; correct numbers/units; retention of estimates and uncertainty; clear facts, unknowns and next actions; no unauthorized promises. Fast speaking is not the principal measure.

#### Correction examples

Incorrect: “We can deliver next week,” when only the customer requests arrival then. Correct: “You have requested arrival next week. We still need to confirm the schedule.” Incorrect: “No impurities.” Correct: “Please specify the impurity limits and test requirements for review.”

#### Study scoring

Understanding 35; completeness 25; clarity 20; boundaries/actions 20. Record yourself and retest if useful. Obtain authority before recording others, uploading or sharing.

An English introduction, a needs-confirmation email, dictation record and personal error list. A self-test pass does not establish the job requirement of skilled business negotiation; real interview assessment remains necessary.

### 12.4 Cost, Margin and Discounts | Calculation Practice

> Source: English Volume 5, page 15; complete PDF combined page 127.

#### Simulated costs only

For a 2 kg Material M order: material USD 1,200; processing 150; testing 50; packing 40; transport 120; insurance 15; other defined costs 25. Total: USD 1,600.00. Real work must check missing costs, tax, currency, finance and other factors.

#### Margin versus markup

Margin = (sales revenue − corresponding cost) ÷ sales revenue. Cost markup = (selling price − cost) ÷ cost. Use a consistent cost basis. This exercise does not determine the accounting gross-margin definition; use finance’s actual definition.

#### Target margin: 20%

Price = cost ÷ (1 − 20%) = USD 1,600 ÷ 0.80 = USD 2,000.00. For 2 kg: USD 1,000.00/kg. Simply multiplying cost by 1.20 gives USD 1,920.00 and a margin of approximately 16.67%, not 20%.

#### Discounts with cost held constant

0% discount: revenue USD 2,000.00; profit USD 400.00; profit/revenue 20.00%.  

2% discount: revenue USD 1,960.00; profit USD 360.00; profit/revenue 18.37%.  

5% discount: revenue USD 1,900.00; profit USD 300.00; profit/revenue 15.79%.  

10% discount: revenue USD 1,800.00; profit USD 200.00; profit/revenue 11.11%.  

#### Limits

Discounts reduce profit in this example, but it does not follow that every discount should be rejected. Reassess quantity, packing, logistics, credit and fixed-cost effects. Acceptance belongs to authorized personnel. The minus signs are explicitly rendered because extracted PDF text can omit them.

### 12.5 Validity, Metal Prices, Currency and Changes

> Source: English Volume 5, page 16; complete PDF combined page 128.

#### Validity matters

State expiry, quantity and applicable conditions. Reconfirm after expiry. Do not say the price is unchanged without checking. If accepted terms or a contract already exist, obtain professional review before changing them because prices moved.

#### Pricing benchmarks and formulas

If a company uses a material benchmark plus processing or another formula, define source, time window, currency, unit, calculation and adjustment rules. Do not invent which metal-price basis the company uses.

#### Currency risk

Different revenue and cost currencies can affect results. Pricing, currency conversion and risk management require finance approval. This volume predicts no exchange rate and does not advise a beginner to hedge independently.

#### Quantity and specification changes

Sample-to-production scale, purity, packing or timing changes can require reassessment. More volume does not automatically mean a lower price; review capacity, charges and payment exposure.

#### English model

The previous quotation was based on [confirmed assumptions]. Since [condition] has changed, we need to review the offer before confirming revised terms. Do not first promise that the old conditions remain available.

### 12.6 Incoterms® | Separate Four Questions

> Source: English Volume 5, page 17; complete PDF combined page 129.

#### What the rules address

Incoterms® allocate certain delivery responsibilities, costs and risk of loss or damage; they do not settle every contractual matter. Payment, price, ownership and other remedies need separate agreement. [S2][S3] This volume studies the 2020 version; its summaries are not the complete rules.

#### State place and version

Write “FCA [complete named place and exact point], Incoterms® 2020,” not FCA alone. Place affects more than freight. Under C rules, the named destination is not automatically the risk-transfer point. [S3][S5]

#### Four separate questions

Who arranges/pays for transport? When do delivery and cargo-risk transfer occur? Who handles relevant formalities and insurance? When is payment due and when does ownership transfer? The last pair cannot be answered from the trade rule alone.

#### Not a substitute for contract and compliance

A rule does not automatically resolve specifications, payment default, sanctions/licensing, tax eligibility or claim procedures. Transaction eligibility and the seller’s ability to handle destination importation require separate assessment.

#### Exercise 12-A

“The seller pays freight, so bears all risk until the customer receives the goods.” Correct? Not necessarily. Under CPT, CIP, CFR and CIF, cost and risk can relate to different points; review the chosen rule and contract. [S5]

### 12.7 FCA and FOB | Delivery and Transport Mode

> Source: English Volume 5, page 18; complete PDF combined page 130.

#### FCA | Free Carrier

For any transport mode. The seller delivers at the named place to the carrier or other person nominated by the buyer under the rule. [S4] Delivery/loading details differ between the seller’s premises and other places. Check the full rule and exact point before formal use.

#### FOB | Free On Board

For sea or inland-waterway transport. The seller delivers and risk transfers when the goods are on board at the named shipment port. [S4] “FOB airport” or “FOB courier” is not a standard application.

#### Containers and selection

The ICC explanation favors FCA when container goods are handed to a terminal/carrier before loading that the seller does not control. [S4] Neither “all sea shipments must use FOB” nor “all transactions use FCA” is correct. Check the actual handover.

#### EXW caution

EXW may appear to minimize seller duties, but loading, export formalities and buyer capability need assessment. Its name does not guarantee cross-border simplicity. A beginner must not change terms to evade responsibility. [S2]

#### Practice

A buyer’s air carrier collects at a logistics point. Do not mechanically quote FOB; ask logistics and designated reviewers to confirm an appropriate multimodal rule and exact delivery arrangement.

### 12.7 CPT, CIP, CFR and CIF | Costs Are Not Risk

> Source: English Volume 5, page 19; complete PDF combined page 131.

#### CPT and CIP

Usable for various transport modes. The seller arranges and pays transport to the named destination; risk transfers on delivery to the carrier as the rule provides. CIP adds seller insurance duties. [S5] Understand and identify delivery and destination separately.

#### CFR and CIF

For sea or inland-waterway transport. The seller pays freight to the named destination port; risk transfers on board at the shipment port. CIF adds insurance duties. [S4][S5] A destination port in the wording does not mean risk remains with the seller until arrival.

#### Insurance under the 2020 rules

The source describes CIF’s default minimum as Institute Cargo Clauses (C) or similar, and CIP’s broader default as Clauses (A) or similar, subject to agreement. [S6] “A” is not unconditional cover for every loss. Specialists must check terms, exclusions, scope and amounts.

#### Important limits

Insurance does not guarantee bank payment. Buyer cargo risk does not excuse the seller’s packing or delivery obligations. Review the actual contract, rule and insurance rather than assigning liability by abbreviation.

#### Practice

Under a simulated CIP offer, goods are damaged after the agreed carrier handover. Check delivery facts, rule, cause and insurance before saying either “the seller pays everything” or “the seller has no responsibility.”

### 12.7 DAP, DPU and DDP | Check Destination Duties

> Source: English Volume 5, page 20; complete PDF combined page 132.

#### Basic distinctions

Under the 2020 rules, DAP/DDP delivery is generally at destination on the arriving means of transport, ready for unloading. DPU includes unloading before delivery. DDP also allocates import clearance and relevant import duties/taxes to the seller; under DAP/DPU, import formalities generally fall to the buyer. [S2] Check the actual rule and contract.

#### Do not casually accept DDP

Review lawful import capability, taxes, agency arrangements, records, local duties and costs. “I want a door price” does not require DDP, and a surcharge does not solve every risk.

#### DAP is not “every cost included”

Specify exact point, unloading, import procedures and additional fees. A courier’s commercial service name is not an Incoterms® rule; a carrier’s “tax-inclusive service” is not automatically a company contract promise.

#### English response

We can review a destination-delivery option, but the exact rule, named place, import responsibilities and costs must be confirmed before we quote.

#### Learning boundary

Recognize questions, not decide complex disputes from this summary. Logistics, finance, compliance or other designated owners should check formal quotations.

### 12.8 Payment Methods | Separate Mechanism and Timing

> Source: English Volume 5, page 21; complete PDF combined page 133.

#### T/T does not mean advance payment

Telegraphic transfer describes a payment channel/method. Timing, amount and installments must be stated separately. It can be used before or after shipment, with appropriate credit and contract arrangements. “T/T” alone does not define the risk.

#### Cash in advance

Payment before shipment helps reduce the seller’s non-payment exposure, while the buyer faces performance risk. Compliance, transaction and funds-verification issues remain. [S7] A screenshot or transfer application is not finance-confirmed receipt.

#### Letter of credit

A bank’s corresponding payment undertaking depends on credit conditions and compliant documents. [S7][S8] Good physical goods alone do not automatically ensure payment. Review bank, text, documents, deadlines, discrepancies and other risks. A beginner must not independently accept problematic conditions or guarantee zero risk.

#### Documentary collection

Banks handle documents and collection under instructions, not a bank payment guarantee. D/P concerns documents against payment; D/A concerns documents against acceptance, which is not payment. [S7] Cargo control also depends on transport documents and facts; do not assume it is assured.

#### Open account

Goods are delivered before payment falls due, leaving higher seller non-payment exposure. Credit assessment and approval are needed. [S7] “Net 30” still needs a starting event, limit and overdue arrangements. Familiarity is not authority to grant credit.

### 12.8 Receipt Verification, Bank Charges and Changes

#### Payment-term fields

Currency, total, installment percentages/amounts, due events, method, bank charges, receipt confirmation, overdue handling and other approved conditions. A 30/70 split is not a universal requirement or assumed company practice.

Finance/bank/designated staff review the draft, check achievable shipment and document conditions, amend problems, then execute the approved text. Actual bank undertakings, applicable rules and fees depend on the documents.

Company details sent to customers also need approval. This is risk-control advice, not proof that a particular email is fraudulent.

### Chapter 13 | 13.1 Prepare Limits and Options

### 13.3 Price Objection and Discount Discussion

> Source: English Volume 5, page 26; complete PDF combined page 138.

#### Simulated customer

Your price is 10% higher than another offer. Can you match it?

#### English response

Thank you for explaining. Before reviewing the price, could we confirm that the specification, quantity, documentation and delivery basis are comparable? You do not need to share confidential supplier information. If those conditions are aligned, I can submit the relevant details for an internal review, but I cannot approve a reduction myself.

#### Options to evaluate

Without weakening mandatory requirements, review cost implications of quantity, unit weight, packing or transport. A customer’s willingness to change conditions is not proof that a discount can be offered; recalculate and seek approval. Do not invent prior prices, false deadlines or management approval.

#### Translation of the separate concise Chinese model

#### Exercise

Use 12.4 to calculate a 5% discount and explain why revenue alone is insufficient. Do not disclose internal cost or negotiation limits without authorization.

### 13.4 Credit-Period Objections

> Source: English Volume 5, page 27; complete PDF combined page 139.

#### Simulated customer

Our standard terms are net 60 after receipt. Can you accept them for the first order?

#### English response

We understand your preferred terms. Credit terms require an internal review of the buyer, exposure, payment timing and applicable safeguards. I cannot approve net 60 during this discussion. We can review your request and, if appropriate, consider an approved initial-order arrangement.

Requested terms versus company-approved terms. The customer’s normal practice is not mutual agreement; discussing it does not mean credit is granted.

### 13.5 Urgency and Delivery-Time Objections

> Source: English Volume 5, page 28; complete PDF combined page 140.

#### Simulated customer

We must receive it within ten days. Please guarantee the date now.

#### English response

I understand the deadline is important. We need to confirm the exact required arrival date, destination, specification and quantity, then review production, documentation, transport and any applicable approvals. ### 13.6 Quality, Testing and Technical Objections

> Source: English Volume 5, page 29; complete PDF combined page 141.

#### Simulated customer

Another supplier says their material has no impurities. Can you guarantee zero impurities too?

#### English response

We should compare defined specifications and test evidence rather than use an absolute claim. Please share the required impurity limits, purity basis and test method. We can then review the relevant specification and documentation internally.

### Chapter 16 | 16.1 Coordination Workflow and Roles

> Source: English Volume 7, page 3; complete PDF combined page 190.

#### Sales coordinates and checks

Sales does not replace production scheduling, quality release or finance confirmation of funds. Accurately convey requirements, check approval, track milestones, identify gaps and update others; do not bypass reviews with personal verbal instructions.

#### Teaching workflow

Confirm requirements/specification → review contract/order → accept and initiate internally → arrange samples/production → inspect and prepare documents → check shipment conditions → authorized delivery → arrival/feedback → payment tracking → filing/follow-up. Prepayment, evaluation and terms may alter the sequence.

#### Example roles

Technical: material/specification suitability. Quality: tests, documents, release. Production/planning: schedule and progress. Logistics: transport/documents. Finance: funds, credit, settlement. Designated contract/compliance owners: relevant reviews. Sales: communication and action closure. Confirm actual authority after joining.

#### Four separate statuses

Customer request: what the buyer wants. Internal approval: permission under conditions. Actual completion: supported by evidence. Responsibility under review: no premature attribution. Estimates also need a basis/update time; they are not guarantees.

#### Exercise 16-A

Does a purchase order alone permit production and shipment? Not necessarily. Review differences, acceptance, payment/credit and other conditions. The document cannot replace every approval.

### 16.2 Order Review: Identify Gaps Before Initiation

> Source: English Volume 7, page 4; complete PDF combined page 191.

#### Check the latest versions item by item

Entities/order number; product/specification; purity, impurities, form/dimensions; quantity/tolerance; packing/labels; documents/acceptance; unit price, currency/total; delivery place/rule/time; payment, charges and other applicable conditions.

#### Compare documents as well

Purchase order, quotation, specification attachments, contract, confirmations and system entries may differ. Do not assume precedence yourself. Follow contract/company procedures and seek specialist judgment where necessary.

#### Five-column difference table

Customer wording; previously confirmed terms; difference; decision owner; current status. Added “full compensation” or changed credit requires review even when quantity is unchanged. Check more than amounts.

#### Receipt response

We have received the purchasing documents and are reviewing specification, delivery and payment conditions. Receipt does not mean acceptance of every term; we will respond through the approved process after differences are clarified. Company review must address actual legal effect; a disclaimer alone does not solve everything.

#### Exercise

The offer requires payment before dispatch; the purchase order says 60 days after receipt. Flag and refer to finance/authorized staff rather than producing first and considering later.

### 16.3 Samples, Specifications and Batch Traceability

> Source: English Volume 7, page 5; complete PDF combined page 192.

#### Sample request

Record customer/project, identity, specification version, quantity, use, documents, packing, destination, charges and approval. Small quantity does not remove safety, logistics or applicable review.

#### Key records

Link sample ID to the genuine batch; record specifications/documents, quantity/dispatch, receipt/evaluation, limits and follow-up. Do not pair one material’s documents with another or alter a batch number on a report.

#### Limits of sample approval

Approval concerns a particular evaluation under particular conditions. It does not automatically approve all future batches or production purchasing. Confirm remaining qualification, final-specification and purchasing steps.

#### Specification freeze and changes

Here, freeze means executing against a mutually confirmed version, not a permanent prohibition on change. Reassess technical, quality, cost, timing and review effects before an approved new version.

#### Exercise 16-B

The customer approves the trial but wants smaller production particles. Review as a specification change; do not dispatch to the old version merely because the sample passed.

### 16.4 Internal Initiation and Milestone Management

> Source: English Volume 7, page 6; complete PDF combined page 193.

#### Before initiation

Acceptance and applicable approvals complete; specification/quantity clear; payment or credit conditions met for that milestone; packing, documents, delivery and reviews understood. If something may be completed later, name the owner/deadline; do not assume approval.

#### Order summary

One page: order ID, customer, product, specification version, quantity, critical requirements, planned milestones, owners and risks. Attach originals; do not omit important exceptions. Internal/customer records should agree on facts while retaining different access permissions.

#### Milestone fields

Item, planned/actual time, status, evidence, owner, dependencies and next update. Suggested statuses: not started, in progress, awaiting external input, hold, complete. Estimated completion is separate.

#### Dates need evidence

Identify the source role, assumptions, update date and dependencies. Do not copy a desired customer date into the production plan or send an internal preliminary estimate as a promise without confirmation.

#### Exercise

Include specification, scheduling, quality, documents, authorized shipment and payment. Prepayment may belong early, not at the end; order the events according to the simulated approved terms.

### 16.5 Production and Quality Progress Communication

> Source: English Volume 7, page 7; complete PDF combined page 194.

#### Ask for specifics

Instead of only “Is it ready?”, ask current material/process/test stage, completion evidence, next dependency, risk and update. Qualified/authorized staff explain actual processing and testing; sales does not infer them.

#### Production complete is not ready to ship

Inspection, documents, packing, funds/credit, logistics and applicable release may remain. Record responsible owners’ confirmed status. Pressure does not turn production completion into all conditions satisfied.

#### Simulated update

Production has progressed, while quality review and shipping documentation are still in progress. Shipment is not yet confirmed. We will update you after the relevant conditions have been reviewed.

#### Protect internal information

Provide approved progress evidence only. Do not casually photograph boards, other orders or unapproved results. Unapproved results are not a final CoA.

#### Exercise

“Ship first; send the report later.” Record and escalate through quality/shipment procedures. Do not assume permission or invent a universal legal/company practice.

### 16.6 Cross-Checks Before Dispatch

> Source: English Volume 7, page 8; complete PDF combined page 195.

#### Product and packing

Match actual identity, specification, batch, quantity and packing to approved requirements. Check labels, net/gross weights, inner/outer packing and box IDs, and designated quality release. Sales assists checks, not professional release.

#### Commercial and logistics

Check payment/credit milestone, complete delivery information, mode/arrangements, documents/reviews and collection/dispatch. Apply Volume 5 trade-rule distinctions; paying freight is not bearing all risk.

#### Match documents to goods

Check invoice, packing list, transport and relevant test/safety records: names, entities, quantity, weight, batch and address. Legitimate differences in document roles/formats must not hide errors.

#### Hold the affected stage when unmet

A screenshot is not received funds; a booking is not release; a tracking number does not prove carrier receipt; customer consent does not replace required review. Designated staff decide exceptions.

#### Exercise 16-C

Documents say 2 kg; goods are labelled 2 × 500 g. Investigate, rather than simply changing documents to appear consistent for a rushed shipment.

### 16.7 Export Documents: Purpose and Consistency

> Source: English Volume 7, page 9; complete PDF combined page 196.

#### Requirements vary

ITA states that document number/type depends on destination and shipment type. [S1] This handbook supplies no fixed list covering all Chinese exports and assumes no universal certificate.

#### Commercial invoice and packing list

An invoice describes the actual sale/transaction. A packing list details shipment, packing and weights; it does not replace the invoice. [S2][S3] Do not satisfy requests with false values, identities or uses.

#### Transport documents

Air waybills and bills of lading relate to their transport arrangements. Nature, negotiability and release method differ; having a bill of lading does not always mean control of all goods. [S2][S3] Logistics, carrier, bank and designated owners verify actual handling.

#### Other documents

Origin, insurance, quality, safety, permits and other requirements depend on item, destination, contract and applicable rules. Management-system certificates, SDS, CoA and origin records serve different purposes.

#### Errors

Pause affected sending/action, notify the owner and preserve corrections. Do not privately alter issued third-party documents, backdate or fabricate originals.

### 16.8 Changes, Delays and Partial Shipments

> Source: English Volume 7, page 10; complete PDF combined page 197.

#### Scope of change

Specification, quantity, packing, documents, address, use, payment, transport and time can affect execution. Record originator, prior terms, new request and versions. Unapproved changes are not formal execution instructions.

#### Four elements of a delay update

Verified status; effect on prior arrangements; options under review; next update. If cause is unknown, say so rather than blaming production, logistics or customer.

#### English model

The previously discussed shipment timing is at risk because [confirmed fact]. We are reviewing the impact and available options. A revised date is not yet confirmed. We will update you at [agreed time]. Use verified facts and a genuine agreed time; “ASAP” is not an indefinite substitute.

#### Partial shipment is not an automatic remedy

Confirm acceptance/usefulness, extra costs, documents, payment, quality and applicable review. A part-shipment is not total completion or permission to change a contract unilaterally.

#### Cancellation and hold

Record status and refer contract, incurred costs, funds and goods to authorized owners. Do not independently refund, dispose of stock or waive responsibility.

### 16.9 Payment, Arrival and Order Filing

> Source: English Volume 7, page 11; complete PDF combined page 198.

#### Track agreed milestones

Payment may be advance, installments or after delivery. Follow approved due events/amounts. Finance confirms receipt and reconciliation; sales coordinates but does not turn receivables into received money.

#### Receipt is not acceptance

A signed-for transport status is not automatic quality acceptance or waiver. Confirm quantity, packing and documents and use agreed feedback procedures.

#### Simulated payment reminder

Our records show that [amount/currency] is due under [approved term]. Could you confirm the payment status and any information needed? We will check the receipt with our finance team. Use approved facts; do not add unauthorized interest, threats or suspension commitments.

#### Filing

Order/contract, specifications/changes, approvals, samples, production/quality links, dispatch/documents, logistics, finance confirmation, feedback and issue/closure status. Use authorized access and applicable retention; do not accumulate personal data indefinitely.

#### Separate closure dimensions

Execution, collection, open quality/after-sales matters and filing. Delivery is not complete closure; an ongoing investigation remains open.

### 17.2 Account Records and Team Handover

> Source: English Volume 7, page 13; complete PDF combined page 200.

#### Record layers

Entities/relationships; contacts/roles; products/specifications; contracts/payment; quality/logistics preferences; communication/issues; next steps. Include source, date, current validity and permission.

#### Avoid outdated facts

Old addresses, departed contacts, prices and specifications do not automatically remain valid. Update from actual contact and retain changes. Historical order value is not future budget.

### 17.3 Follow-Up After Samples, Quotes and Delivery

> Source: English Volume 7, page 14; complete PDF combined page 201.

#### After samples

Confirm receipt/testing, items, conditions and questions. If testing has not started, ask about timing/support without assuming poor material or an unserious buyer.

#### After quotations

Confirm receipt, differences, technical information and approval needs rather than repeatedly asking for an order. Use approved reminders for expiry/changes without false urgency.

Completed actions, new facts, stage, missing information and next action. Merely sending an email does not automatically advance an opportunity. Silence is neither rejection nor confirmed demand.

### 17.4 Usage Feedback and Technical Coordination

> Source: English Volume 7, page 15; complete PDF combined page 202.

#### Classify before judging

Experience, process difficulty, lack of technical fit, test questions, delivery issue or formal complaint. “Does not work very well” is not immediate proof of nonconformity or customer misuse.

#### Structured questions

Product/batch/specification; observed behavior and timing; impact; available test conditions/results; relationship to agreed requirements; desired resolution. Collect necessary information, not confidential formulations.

#### Sales role

Organize accurately, coordinate technical/quality communication and track replies. Do not suggest unapproved heating, mixing, chemical treatment or recovery steps. Operations follow applicable SDS, professional guidance and customer safety procedures.

#### Relay technical responses faithfully

Check object, prerequisites, limits and version. “Further testing possible” is not “proven suitable.” Important conclusions should come from approved documents or the responsible role.

#### Exercise 17-B

A customer result differs from the report. Do not offer a discount simply to close it; retain methods, units, batches and original requirements for investigation.

### 17.5 Forecasts, Stock Planning and Repeat Orders

> Source: English Volume 7, page 16; complete PDF combined page 203.

#### Separate forecast, plan and order

Possible next-quarter purchasing is not a formal order. Record quantity range, timing, certainty and prerequisites. Designated owners decide materials/scheduling; customer intent is not a company investment commitment.

#### Do not copy the old order

Recheck identity, version, quantity, entities, address, use, documents, packing, payment and delivery. Reconfirm current supply and applicable reviews; a long-standing account is not automatically exempt.

#### Price and credit

Past discounts, price, credit or samples may not apply. Long-term price locks, capacity reservations or increased credit need explicit review and approval.

#### English model

Thank you for sharing the forecast. We will record it as a planning estimate, not a confirmed order. Before any supply commitment, we need to review the final specification, quantity, schedule and commercial terms.

#### Exercise

A 5 kg/month forecast has no budget approval. Record forecast/pending customer approval, not a secured monthly order. Do not privately order production to build stock.

### 17.6 Silence, Lost Business and Recontact

> Source: English Volume 7, page 17; complete PDF combined page 204.

#### Do not infer a cause from silence

A project may pause, roles change, fit fail, testing continue or contact be unwelcome. Without evidence, record unknown, not “competitor took the account.” Do not repeatedly switch channels.

#### When a reasonable contact basis exists

Ask about the past project and suitability of continuing, honoring refusals and applicable marketing rules. Changed staff/contacts do not bypass suppression.

#### Recontact example

We previously discussed [project]. If it is still active, may we confirm any changes to the specification or timing? If it is no longer relevant or you prefer no further contact of this kind, please let us know. Handle the request accordingly; this retains the action stated in the Chinese model.

#### Loss categories

Confirmed material mismatch, price/terms, service/quality, cancelled project, refusal, unknown. Keep evidence and improvements rather than filling reports with guesses.

#### Pause criteria

Stop on clear rejection/irrelevance. Archive or reduce priority for prolonged lack of substantive information under company rules. Do not erase necessary history or suppression status.

### 17.7 Maintenance Plan; 17.8 Effectiveness Review

> Source: English Volume 7, page 18; complete PDF combined page 205.

#### Plan fields

Stage, issue, contact basis, objective, action, owner, time, information sought and stop condition. Differentiate by actual stage instead of sending one email to every account.

#### Service records

Promised/actual replies, document errors, open issues, feedback and improvements. Do not replace accuracy with empty fast assurances; both timeliness and sound conclusions matter.

#### Repeat-business definition

Define customer set, period and repeat order. One order in multiple shipments is not automatically multiple reorders; forecasts are not orders. Keep value, quantity and company counts separate.

Of ten delivered-to companies, four place another confirmed order during the defined period: 4/10 = 40% under that definition. It does not establish annual retention, profit or future reorders. Check population, period and order definition.

Business-relevant contact, accurate information, continuity with colleagues, forecast/order separation and explicit unknowns when evidence is missing.

### Chapter 18 | 18.1 Complaints, Questions and Nonconformity

> Source: English Volume 7, page 19; complete PDF combined page 206.

#### Distinguish without dismissing

Question: explanation requested. Feedback: experience/suggestion. Complaint: dissatisfaction with product/service and an expected response. Product nonconformity depends on agreed requirements and review, not emotional intensity.

#### Process objectives

Enable reporting, record/track, investigate, communicate handling and learn. ISO 10002:2018’s public summary emphasizes customer focus, accessible handling, analysis/evaluation and effectiveness review. [S4] This volume neither reproduces the full standard nor claims company adoption.

#### Categories support coordination

Quality/technical fit, quantity, packing/transport, documents, timing, commercial/service. One event may cover several; open a unified record with linked actions rather than making the customer chase departments.

#### Status boundaries

Receiving a complaint does not verify every allegation. Investigation is not permission to delay or avoid responsibility. Understanding is not a compensation promise. Unresolved responsibility does not excuse lack of a basic reply or necessary escalation.

#### Exercise 18-A

“Your material stopped our line.” Record impact, batch and issue and escalate. Do not immediately admit/blame or require withdrawal of the complaint before acting.

### 18.2 Receipt and First Response

> Source: English Volume 7, page 20; complete PDF combined page 207.

#### Acknowledge impact and next steps

Thank the customer, acknowledge receipt/impact, explain recording/review, request necessary information and agree an achievable progress update. Do not invent an unapproved 24-hour resolution guarantee.

#### English model

Thank you for reporting this. We understand that it may affect your operations, and we have recorded the issue for review. Please share the order or batch reference, the specific problem, affected quantity and any available photos or test information. The cause and proposed resolution are not yet confirmed. Let us agree a time for the next progress update.

#### Responsibility qualification

The Chinese model also explicitly states that responsibility remains to be confirmed. Retain that distinction: neither the reply nor receipt determines liability or an approved remedy.

#### Potential safety or personnel impact

Escalate immediately under company/customer emergency or product-safety arrangements, seeking local professional emergency help if needed. Necessary safety response must not wait for complete photos or commercial fault assessment. Do not give unknown-material handling instructions.

#### Apologies

A sincere apology for unclear communication/inconvenience can be appropriate. Technical causation, legal liability and compensation require responsible review/approval. Do not let a boilerplate disclaimer displace facts.

### 18.3 Necessary, Accurate and Traceable Information

> Source: English Volume 7, page 21; complete PDF combined page 208.

#### Identify the goods

Entity, order, product, specification version, batch, dispatch/receipt dates, quantity/packing, opening/use and remaining quantity. If batch is unavailable, identify through other records; a blank field is not grounds to reject intake.

#### Describe the issue

Behavior, discovery time, affected scope and the requirement allegedly not met; photos, labels, results or transport records. Preserve originals; translation/summary must not overwrite them.

#### Technical and sampling information

Specialists guide collection of sampling location, method, units, test method and limits. Record confidentiality limitations; do not demand excessive secrets as a condition of help.

#### Retention and return

Quality, safety and designated owners determine isolation, retention, retesting or return using the SDS and customer procedures. Do not tell customers to repack, combine samples, change containers or mail unknown hazardous goods themselves.

#### Evidence limits

A photo is a lead, not full causation. One result does not automatically cover every batch. Escalate safety/major impact before all evidence is complete.

### 18.4 Impact Assessment and Escalation

> Source: English Volume 7, page 22; complete PDF combined page 209.

#### Priority is not account size

Consider personnel safety, environment, legal issues and major operational effects, then spread, urgency and information. A large account does not displace another urgent safety issue. Company owners set categories, deadlines and contacts.

#### Suggested statuses

Urgent escalation; priority review; routine review; more information needed. Each has owner/update. Intake staff do not independently impose technical stop-use, recall or destruction; consult authorized personnel under applicable procedures.

#### Internal notice

Facts, source, effects, unknowns, approved actions already taken and decisions needed. Do not circulate unnecessary personal information or unsupported conclusions such as “production is definitely at fault.”

#### Cross-functional work

Quality or the designated owner coordinates technical review; logistics checks transport; finance checks settlement; sales communicates; authorized owners decide remedies/exceptions. A primary contact should not obstruct specialist discussions.

### 18.5 Quality Differences and Test Review

> Source: English Volume 7, page 23; complete PDF combined page 210.

#### Return to agreed conditions

Same specification, version, batch and unit? Overall purity, individual impurity or compound content? Are inequality, detection limits and reporting basis retained?

#### Review sampling and testing

Methods, samples, condition and definitions may differ; this does not prove the customer wrong. Technical/quality staff decide retesting or a mutually accepted third party.

#### Separate fact, hypothesis and conclusion

Fact: the customer reports an item exceeding its cited limit. Hypothesis: method/specification interpretation differs. Conclusion: specialist-reviewed and approved. Do not publish a hypothesis as a verified cause.

#### Retesting arrangements

Confirm identity, quantity, sealing, transport, safety, method, institution, costs and use of results. Do not guarantee identical results from every laboratory or selectively replace reports with favorable results.

#### English update

We are comparing the specification, batch reference, units and test method. No root cause has been confirmed yet. We will explain the findings and any limitations after the review. Unconfirmed means unconfirmed; guesses are not professional findings.

### 18.6 Quantity, Packing, Transport and Timing Issues

> Source: English Volume 7, page 24; complete PDF combined page 211.

#### Quantity

Check per item/carton and total, net/gross, units, tolerances and dispatch/receipt. Establish facts before changing a packing list or blaming weighing.

#### Damaged packing

Collect necessary outer/inner packing, label and condition details; contact logistics/quality. Specialists and customer procedures determine opening, use or sampling. Sales must not suggest handling leaked material.

### 18.7 Interim Measures, Correction and Improvement

> Source: English Volume 7, page 25; complete PDF combined page 212.

#### Three action layers | Teaching framework

Contain impact: approved limitation of further spread. Correct this instance: address the specific error. Improve the process: address verified causes and test effectiveness. These are related, not identical; every issue need not use one complex report.

#### Teaching example

A document unit was entered incorrectly. Correction: issue the proper corrected file through procedure. Improvement: review entry/checking, define unit validation and inspect later records for recurrence. This is not an event claimed to have occurred at Keneng.

#### More than “someone was careless”

Check process, versions, system, handover, review and work conditions; continue if evidence is insufficient. Repeated why-questions or a cause diagram can help, but five questions are not proof.

#### Verify effectiveness

Specify evidence, reviewer, timing and success criteria. Implemented measures do not prove non-recurrence. If evidence fails, reassess rather than closing by writing “training completed.”

#### ISO boundary

The public summary supports complaint analysis, improvement and effectiveness review. [S4] This section is a teaching design, not a reproduction of clauses, certification or a mandatory method for every company complaint.

### 18.8 Commercial Remedies, Returns and Costs

> Source: English Volume 7, page 26; complete PDF combined page 213.

#### Basis and authority first

Responsibility, contract, investigation, cost, customer impact, logistics and applicable requirements. Authorized owners decide replenishment, replacement, rework, discount, refund or other remedies. Dissatisfaction alone does not establish full compensation.

#### A return is not just “send it back”

Confirm condition, packing, safe transport, borders/customs, receiving point/documents, costs and approval. Prior export does not prove a return has no formalities.

#### Write conditions clearly

Order/quantity, currency/amount, action, timing/prerequisites, responsibilities, documents, charges and exclusions. Settlement/liability-ending text needs professional review; do not force a blanket rights waiver into a template.

#### English model

We have reviewed the available information and will submit the proposed resolution for approval. No replacement, refund or compensation is confirmed until the authorized terms are issued. If approved, state the actual approved scope clearly rather than staying vague.

#### Requested remedy before investigation

Ask the owner whether an interim authorized service/commercial arrangement exists. Do not prejudge liability, waive review or promise unapproved costs. Safety does not wait for commercial decisions.

### 18.9 Closure, Customer Feedback and Review

> Source: English Volume 7, page 27; complete PDF combined page 214.

#### Closure is more than an email

### Integrated Exercise | Reference Answer and Scoring

> Source: English Volume 7, page 29; complete PDF combined page 216.

#### Acknowledgment

Thank you for reporting the concerns with order TRAIN-016. We have recorded the packaging, labeling, quantity and test-result issues for review. If there may be damage or a safety concern, please follow your site procedures and the applicable safety guidance while we escalate the matter. Please share the available shipping, package and test information, without taking unsafe steps to obtain evidence. The cause, responsibility and resolution are not yet confirmed. Let us agree a time for the next update.

#### Investigation roles

Logistics: dispatch/receipt. Quality: batch, labels, quantity records, specification/report. Technical: test differences. Finance/authorized owner: possible commercial remedy. Sales: records/updates. Follow actual company roles; do not appoint yourself as quality-release authority.

#### Unsupported conclusions

### Chapter 19 | 19.1 What a Work System Manages

> Source: English Volume 8, page 3; complete PDF combined page 228.

#### Four objects

Accounts: companies and contacts. Opportunities: specific projects or needs. Tasks: actions assigned to someone. Information: specifications, quotes, approvals and evidence. Do not mix all four in a single untidy cell or invent data for completeness.

### 19.2 Tasks, Priorities and Dependencies

> Source: English Volume 8, page 4; complete PDF combined page 229.

#### Write executable tasks

Verb + object + output + owner + time. Example: sales prepares a specification-gap table and submits it to the technical owner. Avoid only “follow up with customer.”

#### Priority

Handle safety, legally required or mandatory escalations first; then existing commitments affecting delivery; then evidenced opportunities, information preparation and development. Company owners define categories—not whoever speaks loudest.

#### Waiting and dependencies

Awaiting customer specification, internal review or finance needs a dependency owner, next-check time and preparatory work. Waiting is neither permanent neglect nor permission for sales to approve for others.

#### Completion evidence

Sending a request is not another person’s completion; a progress update is not the final conclusion. Evidence must match the task output, such as approved information sent with version retained, or designated confirmation received.

#### Exercise

New-lead research, a promised reply and a suspected misdirected sensitive attachment compete. Use the incident process and notify owners first, then coordinate the commitment. Research volume must not delay exception response.

### 19.4 Account, Opportunity and Follow-Up Records

> Source: English Volume 8, page 6; complete PDF combined page 231.

### 19.5 Measurement Definitions and Data Integrity

> Source: English Volume 8, page 7; complete PDF combined page 232.

#### Four elements for every metric

Observation period, statistical object, event definition, numerator/denominator. Separate companies, contacts, messages, tasks, orders, value and quantity. Partial shipments are not automatically repeat purchases.

#### Simulated on-time completion

Of 12 tasks due and required in the period, nine were completed as agreed: 9/12 = 75%. Do not silently move overdue items to the next period to improve the figure. Record cancellation/change dates, reasons and definitions.

#### Replies and conversion

Email reply rate is not company needs rate; forecast value is not contract value; contract value is not collected cash. Identify denominator changes. Small samples do not establish precise closing probabilities.

#### Do not hide safety through optimization

Fewer reported incidents/complaints may reflect missing records, not lower risk. Do not reward concealment, invented satisfaction or refusal to receive issues. Use detection, response, evidence and improvement to check integrity.

### 20.7 Reporting; 20.8 Self-Checks

> Source: English Volume 8, page 17; complete PDF combined page 242.

#### Six report elements

What happened; when/where; business/information involved; known effects; approved actions taken; and who must decide what. Separate facts from assumptions and preserve appropriate confidentiality.

#### Sequence

#### Do not promise reporting outcomes

#### Five daily questions

#### Completion criteria

Respect limits while proposing the next action. “Compliance matters” is insufficient without pausing, asking the right owner and recording truthfully.
