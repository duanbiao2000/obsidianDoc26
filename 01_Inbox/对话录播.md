# IELTS Speaking 高分对话 ×3

三套完整对话,覆盖 Part 1(热身)+ Part 2(话题卡独白)+ Part 3(延伸讨论),按当前常考话题设计,用词和句式对标 Band 8-9。

---

## Set 1: Travel & Memorable Places

### Part 1 — Warm-up

**Examiner:** Do you like traveling?
**Candidate:** Definitely. I try to get away at least a couple of times a year — there's something about being in an unfamiliar place that just resets my whole mindset.

**Examiner:** Do you prefer traveling alone or with others?
**Candidate:** Honestly, it depends on my mood. Traveling solo gives me the freedom to wander off the beaten track (随心所欲地探索), but traveling with friends makes the experience more memorable because you get to share the moment.

**Examiner:** What kind of place would you like to visit in the future?
**Candidate:** I've always had a soft spot for (对……情有独钟) coastal towns — somewhere quiet, with good food and not overrun by tourists.

**Examiner:** Do people in your country travel much these days?
**Candidate:** Yes, way more than a decade ago. Budget airlines and social media have really opened people's eyes to how accessible travel can be.

### Part 2 — Cue Card

*Describe a memorable trip you have taken.*
*You should say:*
*- where you went*
*- who you went with*
*- what you did there*
*- and explain why it was memorable*

**Sample answer:**
I'd like to talk about a trip I took to a small fishing village on the southern coast a couple of years back. I went with two close friends, and to be honest, it wasn't a heavily planned trip — we more or less winged it (即兴而为), which turned out to be part of the charm.

We spent our days doing pretty simple things: wandering through the local market, trying street food we couldn't even name, and just sitting by the harbour watching fishermen bring in the day's catch. One evening, we ended up chatting with a local family who invited us to a small gathering, and that's honestly the moment that stuck with me the most.

What made the trip memorable wasn't any particular landmark — it was how unscripted everything felt. We weren't ticking boxes off a list; we were just present. It reminded me that sometimes the best travel experiences come from letting go of the itinerary altogether.

### Part 3 — Discussion

**Examiner:** Why do you think people enjoy traveling to unfamiliar places?
**Candidate:** I think it's largely about breaking out of routine. When everything around you is unfamiliar, you're forced to pay closer attention, and that heightened awareness is oddly refreshing.

**Examiner:** Do you think tourism can have negative effects on local communities?
**Candidate:** Absolutely, particularly when it's not managed responsibly. Overtourism can drive up living costs for locals and put a strain on infrastructure, so there needs to be a balance between economic benefit and preserving a place's character.

**Examiner:** How has technology changed the way people travel?
**Candidate:** Enormously. Booking, navigation, translation — it's all instant now. That said, I sometimes think it's made travel a little less adventurous, since people rely on reviews and pre-planned routes rather than discovering things on their own.

## Set 2: Technology & Social Media

### Part 1 — Warm-up

**Examiner:** How much time do you spend on your phone every day?
**Candidate:** Probably more than I'd like to admit — realistically a few hours, between messaging, browsing, and the occasional doom-scrolling session (无意识地刷手机).

**Examiner:** Do you use social media often?
**Candidate:** I do, though I've become more selective about it lately. I mainly use it to keep in the loop with friends rather than consuming content passively.

**Examiner:** What's your favorite piece of technology?
**Candidate:** Probably my laptop — it's basically an extension of how I work and communicate, so I'd be lost without it.

**Examiner:** Do you think people rely too much on technology?
**Candidate:** To some extent, yes. It's a double-edged sword (双刃剑) — incredibly convenient, but it can also make us less resourceful.

### Part 2 — Cue Card

*Describe a piece of technology you find useful.*
*You should say:*
*- what it is*
*- how often you use it*
*- what you use it for*
*- and explain why you find it so useful*

**Sample answer:**
I'll talk about my noise-cancelling headphones, which I picked up about a year ago, mostly on a whim. I use them virtually every day, whether I'm commuting, working, or just trying to tune out background noise at home.

What I use them for varies — sometimes it's music, sometimes podcasts, but more often than not it's simply to create a bubble of focus in a noisy environment. Before I had them, I found it genuinely difficult to concentrate in shared spaces like cafés or open-plan offices.

They've turned out to be useful in ways I didn't initially expect — beyond just entertainment, they've become a tool for managing my attention. In a world full of constant notifications and background chatter, having a reliable way to switch off external noise has made a real difference to my productivity and, honestly, my mental clarity too.

### Part 3 — Discussion

**Examiner:** Do you think social media has changed the way people communicate?
**Candidate:** Definitely, and not entirely for the better. Communication has become faster but arguably shallower — we exchange more messages, yet fewer meaningful conversations.

**Examiner:** Should there be limits on how much time young people spend online?
**Candidate:** I lean towards yes, at least for younger children. Their habits are still forming, so unrestricted access can crowd out other important developmental activities.

**Examiner:** How do you think technology will change in the next decade?
**Candidate:** I imagine we'll see far more seamless integration — technology fading into the background rather than demanding our constant attention, which would honestly be a welcome shift.

## Set 3: Work, Study & Future Plans

### Part 1 — Warm-up

**Examiner:** Are you currently working or studying?
**Candidate:** I'm currently working, though I try to keep learning new things on the side to stay sharp.

**Examiner:** What do you like most about your job/studies?
**Candidate:** I'd say the variety — no two days look quite the same, which keeps things from feeling monotonous.

**Examiner:** Is it important to have a good relationship with colleagues?
**Candidate:** Absolutely. A supportive environment makes even a demanding workload feel manageable, so I don't take that for granted.

**Examiner:** What are your plans for the future?
**Candidate:** I'm hoping to take on more responsibility over the next couple of years, and possibly pick up a new skill set that complements what I already do.

### Part 2 — Cue Card

*Describe a skill you would like to learn.*
*You should say:*
*- what the skill is*
*- why you want to learn it*
*- how you would learn it*
*- and explain how it would benefit you*

**Sample answer:**
The skill I'd like to develop is public speaking — something I've always found a bit daunting, if I'm honest. It's not that I lack things to say; it's more that speaking confidently in front of a group doesn't come naturally to me.

I want to learn it mainly because it keeps coming up as a bottleneck (瓶颈) in my career — the moments where I have the most to contribute are often the ones requiring me to present in front of others. I'd probably start by joining a local speaking club, since practicing in a low-stakes environment with feedback seems far more effective than just reading about technique.

In terms of benefits, I think it would ripple into other areas of my life too — not just presentations at work, but everyday situations like networking or even just being more articulate in casual conversations. It's one of those skills that quietly pays dividends (带来长远回报) across the board.

### Part 3 — Discussion

**Examiner:** Do you think formal education prepares people well for the workplace?
**Candidate:** Only partially. It builds a solid knowledge foundation, but a lot of practical skills — communication, adaptability — tend to be picked up on the job rather than in a classroom.

**Examiner:** Is it better to specialize in one field or have broad, general skills?
**Candidate:** I think it depends on the industry, but increasingly, I'd argue a hybrid approach works best — deep expertise in one area, complemented by broad enough skills to collaborate across disciplines.

**Examiner:** How do you think work will change in the future?
**Candidate:** I expect more flexibility around where and how people work, along with a growing emphasis on skills that are harder to automate, like judgment and interpersonal communication.

### 使用建议

抄写这三段确实能帮你把地道表达"肌肉记忆化",但光靠抄写主要练的是书写和阅读输入,对口语和听力的直接提升有限。如果想让效果最大化,建议在抄写之外配合:
- **跟读(shadowing)**:边听边跟着说,练语音语调
- **录音自测**:把 Part 2 独白录下来回放,对比原文找差距
- **脱稿复述**:抄完之后合上文本,试着用自己的话复述一遍
