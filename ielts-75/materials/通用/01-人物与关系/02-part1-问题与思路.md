# 人物与关系 · Part 1 问题与思路

## 1. Who is the person you are closest to?

**思路**：A 点人 → R 为什么亲近 → E 具体场景。

**示范**：The person I'm closest to is my mother. The main reason is that she has always supported me unconditionally, whether I was succeeding or failing. For example, whenever I'm stressed about exams, she always finds a way to encourage me, which makes me feel safe and understood.

## 2. Do you have many friends?

**思路**：A 表态（数量不多但精）→ R 原因 → E 例子。

**示范**：Not a large number, but a few close ones. The main reason is that I believe the quality of a friendship matters more than the quantity. Even though I don't have many friends, the ones I have are people I can rely on and share everything with.

## 3. What makes a good friend?

**思路**：A 列举品质 → R 逐点解释 → E 总结。

**示范**：I'd say a good friend needs a few qualities. First of all, trustworthiness — a friend you can confide in. Then, the willingness to support you, especially when things go wrong. What I value most, though, is understanding, because a true friend accepts you as you are, even when you're not at your best.

## 4. Do you keep in touch with old friends?

**思路**：A 表态 → R 方式 → E 具体例子。

**示范**：Yes, I try to. The main reason is that old friendships are hard to replace, and it takes effort to maintain them. For example, I often message my school friends and try to meet up when we're free, even though we're all busy with our own lives now.

## 5. Is it easier to make friends as an adult or as a child?

**思路**：A 表态（童年更容易）→ R 原因 → E 让步（成人友谊更深刻）。

**示范**：I'd say it's easier to make friends as a child. The main reason is that children meet peers regularly at school and share activities naturally, without the barriers adults face. That said, friendships formed in adulthood, though harder to build, tend to be deeper and more meaningful, since they're based on shared values and mutual choice.

## 6. Do you think family relationships are more important than friendships?

**思路**：A 折中表态 → R 家庭的意义 → R 朋友的意义 → 总结。

**示范**：I'd say both are important, but in different ways. Family relationships provide a sense of belonging and unconditional support, which is a foundation you can always count on. On the other hand, friendships offer companionship and understanding that you choose for yourself. So in my view, they complement each other, and neither can fully replace the other.
